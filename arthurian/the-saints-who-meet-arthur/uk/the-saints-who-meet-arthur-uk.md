## About this translation

**Written:** 1086–1199 CE

This book is fiction. Its saints work miracles, and its Arthur fights dragons and submits to saints’ authority. The lives name real places and religious communities, but these narratives don’t establish that the encounters happened.

This anthology selects Arthur passages from six separately written Latin saints’ lives, written in 1086–1199 CE. The oldest is Lifris’s life of Cadoc, written shortly before 1086. Carannog’s and Efflamm’s followed about 1100, Padarn’s in the 1120s and Illtud’s about 1140. The newest is the life of Goeznovius: its preface claims 1019, but the bishop it addresses is on record in 1179, and Hubert Guillotel reads its date as 1199. The Welsh passages concern Arthur’s encounters with saints and their communities; Efflamm’s complete Latin life continues beyond the dragon episode, and Goeznovius places Arthur in the historical preface before the saint’s family arrives. The Latin comes from Rees’s 1853 edition and La Borderie’s editions of 1883 and 1892.

**The selection.** Cadoc’s prologue and section 18 are separated by omitted chapters; later references to Arthur’s sanctuary grant are discussed in an endnote. Carannog’s altar episode runs through the second landing and church grant. Padarn’s Jerusalem journey precedes the tunic demand. Illtud’s visit to Arthur precedes service to another ruler. Efflamm retains all nine lessons and 23 paragraphs; Goeznovius retains the salutation and sections 1–3. The other material in the Welsh lives and La Borderie’s French commentary isn’t translated here.

**The witnesses.** Rees’s base is Cotton Vespasian A XIV, with Cotton Titus D XXII collated for Cadoc. La Borderie’s Efflamm uses the Tréguier legendary copy in BnF ms. fr. 22321 and variants from BnF ms. lat. 1148. His Goeznovius extract uses the Vetus collectio Nannetensis and variants from the Chronique de Saint-Brieuc. Each page citation identifies its printed edition; Efflamm’s printed 258 [285] preserves the misprint. La Borderie’s bracketed manuscript pages and his footnotes stand in the Latin; where the English follows one of his corrections, an endnote says so.

## Part 1: Arthur meets Cadoc's father on a hill and Cadoc at a ford

### Gwynllyw carries off Gwladys, and Arthur helps him escape her father

<a id="page-1"></a>[\[Rees, Cambro British Saints (1853), 23\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-1) A long time later, King Gwynllyw,[^1-1] now secure in his kingdom, wanted to marry a girl of very noble birth.[^1-2] She was elegant and very beautiful, dressed in silk. Her name was Gwladys, and her father was a minor king called Brychan. Gwynllyw had heard how lovely she was, and he burned to have her as his lawful wife.

So he sent several messengers to her father to press him to give her to him in marriage. Her father was furious at the request. He refused to promise his daughter, insulted his envoys and dismissed them without honour. They went home angry and told their lord how they'd been treated.

When the king heard, he flew into a rage. As fast as he could, he armed three hundred of his young men to carry the girl off by force. They set out at once and came to Brychan's court, which is called Talgarth. There they found Gwladys sitting with her sisters at the door of her room, talking modestly. They grabbed her and hurried back.

When her father Brychan heard of it, he was heartbroken and mourned for his dear daughter. He called on all his friends and allies to help him get her back. Once his helpers had gathered, he went after the enemy at full speed. When Gwynllyw saw them coming, he had Gwladys brought to him and set her on his horse.

<a id="page-2"></a>[\[Rees, Cambro British Saints (1853), 24\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-2) He didn't run, though. He rode slowly ahead of his army with the girl in the saddle, waiting for his men and urging them to fight bravely. Brychan's force boldly attacked the cruel king and his followers. They cut down two hundred of them, and Brychan chased the rest as far as a hill on the border between the two kingdoms. In Welsh it's called Bochriucarn, which means the jaw of the stone road.

Gwynllyw reached the edge of his own country unhurt, with Gwladys, though he mourned the many killed in the fighting. On top of that hill sat three bold heroes playing dice: Arthur and two of his soldiers, Kay and Bedivere.[^2-1]

They saw the king coming towards them with the girl. At once Arthur burned with lust for her. Full of wicked thoughts, he turned to his companions. "I want you to know I'm on fire with desire for this young woman that soldier is carrying off on his horse."

They tried to stop him. "Don't even think of such a crime," they said. "We look after the poor and the troubled. So let's hurry and help this man in his hard fight."

"Since you'd both rather side with him than take her from him by force for me," Arthur said, "go and meet them, and find out which of them is the rightful heir of this land."

They set off at once and asked, as the king had told them. Gwynllyw replied, "Yahweh is my witness, and so are the wisest of the Britons: this country is mine by inheritance."

The messengers returned to their lord and told him what they'd heard. Then Arthur and his companions armed themselves and charged Gwynllyw's enemies. They turned and ran in great confusion, and Arthur's men chased them back home.

So Gwynllyw, victorious under Arthur's protection, went on to his palace with Gwladys. It stood on the hill that from then on took his name in Welsh, Altgundliu, which means Gwynllyw's Hill. The one country is called Gwynllwg after Gwynllyw, and the other Brycheiniog after Brychan.[^2-2]

### Cadoc shelters Ligessauc from Arthur, and they settle his price at the Usk

<a id="page-3"></a>[\[Rees, Cambro British Saints (1853), 48\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-3) At that time a very strong leader of the Britons, Ligessauc, son of Eliman, killed three soldiers of Arthur, the famous king of Britain.[^3-1] People also knew Ligessauc as Long Hand.[^3-2] Arthur hunted him everywhere, and he found no safe place. Everyone was too frightened of Arthur to shelter Ligessauc. At last, worn out from running, he fled to Cadoc, the holy man.

Cadoc felt sorry for him and took him in kindly. Cadoc trusted in Yahweh and wasn't the least bit scared of Arthur, by Jesus's command: "Don't be afraid of those who can kill the body but not the soul. Fear the one who can send both to hell."[^3-3]

So Ligessauc lived safely with Cadoc in the region of Gwynllwg for seven years, and Arthur never knew. Then someone betrayed Ligessauc to Arthur once more. Arthur came to the river Usk with a large army, meaning to bring a case at law, since he didn't dare take on the holy man by force.

Cadoc sent envoys to Arthur and asked whether he'd let wise judges settle the dispute. Arthur agreed. Saint Cadoc summoned three leading men from different parts of the country, David, Teilo and Dochdwy, and Reneder and Maidoc as well.[^3-4] Many other clergy and elders from all of Britain came to decide it. They all met beside the great river Usk, with Cadoc leading the way. There, from either bank, the two parties argued the case like enemies, in bitter words.

<a id="page-4"></a>[\[Rees, Cambro British Saints (1853), 49\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-4) They kept at it for a long time. After that, the most learned of the judges decided Arthur should receive three of the best cows for each man killed. Others ruled he should get a hundred head of cattle. Since ancient times, that had been the Britons' judgement and their price for the servants of kings and leaders.

Arthur accepted, but with a sneer. He refused animals of a single colour, and wanted them in two: red in front and white behind. He kept dodging every other offer to get them.

The judges had no idea where to find a herd like that, and they hesitated, unsure what to do. So the holy man, in the name of the Trinity, told the young men at the meeting to drive nine cows to him, or a hundred, as some say, whatever they looked like. They were brought in front of Cadoc and Yahweh's other servants. At once, by Yahweh's great power and the kind prayers of the righteous, they took on the colours Arthur's wicked greed demanded.

All the clergy, and many others faithful to Yahweh who'd gathered around Cadoc, watched this miracle. They were overjoyed and gave Yahweh great glory.

Then the holy man asked how he was bound by law to drive them over. The judges on the other side answered, "By rights you herd them to the middle of the ford."

So he took them that far. Arthur came to meet them with Kay and Bedivere, while the others sat on the bank. Kay and Bedivere eagerly grabbed the animals by the horns to drag them across. But in their hands, in front of everyone, by Yahweh's will, they turned into bundles of ferns.

When Arthur saw it, he humbly begged Cadoc to pardon the wrong he'd done him. Cadoc let it go, as the gospel says: "Forgive, and you'll be forgiven."[^4-1]

Then the king talked it over with his army and extended Cadoc's right of refuge to seven years, seven months and seven days.[^4-2]

<a id="page-5"></a>[\[Rees, Cambro British Saints (1853), 50\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-5) There was more. Someone from another land who'd stayed under Saint Cadoc's protection might want to go home once that time was up, and leave for some other part of the world, or cross the sea. A violent storm and a contrary wind could drive them into Cadoc's harbour, Barry Island. If they came back to their old sanctuary, then by old custom they were to be welcomed and kept in Cadoc's service for life.

Arthur and all his leaders confirmed it, with the elders of all Britain. "We too declare these terms in everyone's hearing," they said. "Whoever breaks them, may Yahweh bring on them every plague written in the Old and New Law, and wipe their name out of the book of life.[^5-1] Anyone who keeps them, let the blessings the scriptures promise come down on them and fill them. Grant their soul eternal rest."

When the meeting was over, all the cattle changed at the ford turned up safe in their own barns, with their owners. From that day the place has been called in Welsh Trefredinauc, the fern farm. The crossing where the case was heard is known as Rithguutebou.

Everyone left in peace. Saint Cadoc granted three estates to the three famous leaders, David, Teilo and Dochdwy, who were worn out from their long journey. Their names and the men who received them are written below. To Saint David he gave Landdeui Pennbei; to Teilo, Merthir Tecmed; and to Dochdwy, Landubrguir.

## Part 2: Carannog asks Arthur where his altar has landed

### Carannog tames the serpent, and Arthur returns the altar and grants land

<a id="page-6"></a>[\[Rees, Cambro British Saints (1853), 99\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-6) After that, Carannog came back again to his own country, Ceredigion,[^6-1] to his cave, with many clergy.[^6-2] There he worked more miracles than anyone can count. Christ sent him an altar from heaven, worthy of honour, whose colour nobody could make out. Later Carannog came to the river Severn to sail across, and he set it on the sea. It floated ahead, going where Yahweh wanted Carannog to go.

At that time Cadwy and Arthur ruled that country, living at Din Draithou.[^6-3] Arthur was going around looking for a huge, terrible serpent of great strength, which had laid waste to twelve shares of the land of Carhampton. Carannog came and greeted Arthur, who was glad to see him and took his blessing. Then Carannog asked Arthur if he'd heard where his altar had come ashore.

"If I get something for it, I'll tell you," Arthur answered.

"What do you want?" said Carannog.

"Bring out the serpent hiding near here, if you're really Yahweh's servant," Arthur said, "so we can see it."

Then Saint Carannog went off and prayed to Yahweh. At once it came with a great noise, like a calf running to its mother. It bowed its head to Yahweh's man like a slave to its master, with a humble heart and gentle eyes.[^6-4]

Carannog put his stole around its neck and led it like a lamb, and it didn't raise its wings or its claws.[^6-5] It was as thick through as a seven-year-old bull, and the cloth would barely reach around.

Then they went together to the fort and greeted Cadwy, who welcomed them. The serpent was led into the middle of the hall and fed in front of the people, and they tried to kill it. Carannog wouldn't let them. It had come at Yahweh's word, Carannog said, to destroy the sinners in Carhampton and to show Yahweh's power.

Then it was taken out through the fort's gate, and Carannog set it loose. He told it to leave, harm nobody and never come back. It went away, as Yahweh had ordered.[^6-6]

Carannog got his altar back. Arthur had meant to make a table of it, but anything put on it was thrown off a long way.

<a id="page-7"></a>[\[Rees, Cambro British Saints (1853), 100\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-7) The king asked Carannog to accept Carhampton by a charter that would last forever, and Carannog built a church there. Then a voice came to him from heaven telling him to put the altar back in the sea. After that, Cadwy and Arthur were sent to ask where it had gone, and they were told it had come ashore at the mouth of the Guellit. "Give him twelve shares of land again, where it was found," the king said. Later Carannog came and built a church there, and it was called the city of Carrou.[^7-1]

## Part 3: Arthur asks Padarn for the tunic from Jerusalem

### An angel sends David, Padarn and Teilo to Jerusalem

<a id="page-8"></a>[\[Rees, Cambro British Saints (1853), 192\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-8) While this was going on,[^8-1] a messenger from heaven appeared to Saint David at Glyn Rhosyn, where he served Christ, his god.[^8-2] "Get up and go to Jerusalem to be ordained there," it said. "Take two companions worthy of it with you, to receive it beside you: Padarn and Teilo."[^8-3]

David sent for them at once, and they came without delay. The three set out together through foreign peoples and were given the gift of tongues.[^8-4] Though they spoke one language themselves, they talked to everyone in the speech each had been born to. At last they reached Jerusalem, and their preaching there was the finest since the apostles'.

<a id="page-9"></a>[\[Rees, Cambro British Saints (1853), 193\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-9) Then the chief archbishop laid his hands on them and ordained them. The three saints were showered with gifts, and Padarn got two: a staff, and a tunic woven in one piece.[^9-1] They came home safely and divided Britain into three dioceses, one each. It would've stayed that way if the malice of wicked rulers hadn't broken it up later.

### Arthur demands Padarn's tunic, and the earth swallows him

Padarn was resting in his church after that long voyage. Meanwhile a tyrant called Arthur was roaming the lands on either side, and one day he came to the cell of Saint Padarn, the bishop. While he was talking with Padarn, he noticed the tunic. Greed stabbed him, and he demanded it for himself.

"No wicked man is fit to wear this," the saint answered. "Only a cleric is."

Arthur stormed out of the monastery in a rage. Then he came back, furious, to take it, against the advice of his holy companions. One of Padarn's disciples saw him returning and ran to Saint Padarn. "The tyrant who just left is on his way back, shouting insults," he said. "He's pounding the dirt flat under his feet."

"Then let the earth swallow him," Padarn answered.

As he said it, the ground opened its deep hollow and swallowed Arthur up to the chin. Right away Arthur knew his guilt. In the pit Arthur praised Yahweh and Padarn together and begged for pardon, until the earth let go.

On that spot he knelt and asked Padarn to forgive him, and Padarn did. He took Padarn as his patron forever,[^9-2] and then he left.

## Part 4: Illtud visits Arthur and joins another ruler's household

### Illtud visits Arthur's court and becomes Poulentus's steward

<a id="page-10"></a>[\[Rees, Cambro British Saints (1853), 159\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-10) Meanwhile Illtud, a soldier of high standing,[^10-1] heard how magnificent the court of his cousin King Arthur was, and he wanted to visit so great a conqueror.[^10-2] He set out from the land we call Further Britain and sailed over, and there he saw a vast crowd of fighting men.

He was received with honour and given the gifts a soldier wants. Once he'd had his fill of presents, he left the royal court well pleased and took the road to Poulentus, king of the land of Glamorgan,[^10-3] with his wife Trynihid, a very respectable woman, at his side.

<a id="page-11"></a>[\[Rees, Cambro British Saints (1853), 160\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-11) Poulentus saw he was a well-bred and honourable soldier, and kept him with great affection. He loved him more than all his other men and gave him lavish gifts. So Illtud stayed, held in the highest respect, until the king chose him to lead the royal household. He ran it without a single quarrel, a peaceful governor, second only to his lord.

The gospel's commands were stored in the soldier's heart, and he never stopped repeating them to anyone who'd keep them. What he said guided his listeners to do everything well, and doing so lifted them to heaven's reward. On the outside he dressed and looked like a fighting man. Inside he was the wisest of the Britons. So King Poulentus made him master of his soldiers,[^11-1] for his fine eloquence and unmatched understanding. No one at the time could match Illtud's talent. Learned men testified to it and proved it true.

### The earth swallows Poulentus's men, and Illtud turns to Cadoc

One day Illtud was leading the royal household on a hunt through Saint Cadoc's lands. While the party rested, it had a harsh message carried to the famous abbot: he was to have a meal brought out to them, or they'd take food by force. Saint Cadoc thought it out of line: its words were rough, and it all but demanded tribute from a free man. Still, he sent the party enough for a meal.

When it came, they sat down to eat, but never got a bite. For their unlawful demand and their sacrilege, the earth swallowed the whole evil crowd. For so much wickedness they vanished without a trace. Illtud the soldier, leader of the fighting men, escaped. He hadn't gone along with the wrongful request, and wasn't at the spot where the party waited for its food. He was far off, flying the hawk he often let loose after birds.

<a id="page-12"></a>[\[Rees, Cambro British Saints (1853), 161\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-12) When Illtud saw this miracle he was afraid. The sight left him bitterly sorry for his past sins, and he hurried to Saint Cadoc. He knelt and begged him for advice on mending what he'd neglected.

Cadoc answered with sound guidance and pressed him. First, Illtud should give up worldly clothes, then take up again what was put aside, a cleric's dress, and serve the highest creator, Yahweh, for life and for an eternal reward.

Illtud humbly obeyed, and firmly promised to carry it out. Then he went back to King Poulentus, got his permission and quit his service.

The king, the queen and the whole court were sad to see Illtud go. At last he came to the bank of the River Thaw with his wife and his squires. It was summer, so he built a shelter of reeds to keep the rain off their bed. The horses grazed in the meadows, and the two of them lay down for the night, their eyes heavy, and slept.[^12-1]

## Part 5: Efflamm meets Arthur hunting a monster in Brittany

### Efflamm is born to an Irish king, and his marriage seals a peace

<a id="page-13"></a>[\[La Borderie, Saint Efflam (1892), 282\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-13) Of Saint Efflamm, confessor.[^13-1]

November the sixth.[^13-2]

First lesson

Saint Efflamm was Irish by birth, the son of a king. Our lord Jesus Christ wanted him born to put an end to that country's misery. The miserable Irish were crying out to Yahweh day and night and begging for their old troubles to be over.

While he was still hidden in his mother's womb, many men, and women too, saw in visions much of the miracles he'd work. So they predicted that this man they didn't yet know would be powerful, a firm pillar for his people, and his country's adviser and guardian. All the people waited with the deepest longing for the man who'd be the kingdom's protector, above all those worn down and exhausted by hard campaigns and still in real fear of how the war's uncertain fortunes would fall. He was going to root out for good every evil that had overrun that land.

The country was weighed down with troubles. A neighbouring king of Ireland, wanting to make his own power greater and jealous of the Irish people's good fortune, surrounded himself with countless soldiers and attacked them without warning. He butchered many of them like dumb animals, some were put in hard chains, and crowds of slaves were burned to ash and died.[^13-3] For their part, the Irish sometimes fought back like men, and both peoples spilled a lot of blood.

<a id="page-14"></a>[\[La Borderie, Saint Efflam (1892), 283\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-14) Their great-grandfathers had begun this slaughter of each other, their grandsons had added to it, and by the time this child arrived it had become a custom. But Yahweh wanted joy born for the suffering when so great a boy came into the world. The two rulers, who were copying their ancestors' ways, decided to end the old feud,[^14-1] and to make the peace treaty unbreakable, this son of the Irish king was engaged to the other's daughter.

As a child he was thoroughly schooled, and he outdid the others in learning. The grace of his character won everyone's admiration. As he got older, his strength of mind grew too.

Before he made his bride the partner of his bed, young in body but old in judgement, he weighed like a man how much glory is owed to virginity, and that crowned virgins hold the seat nearest Christ. He gave up marriage's union and put out the fires of lust completely. He wore himself thin with fasting to set an example for others. He hated every indulgence like a crime, and worked to serve his creator with body, mind and hand. He kept his flesh under chastity's rule, and longed with devotion and without a stop for the virtues of a holy spirit. Giving plenty to the poor, he did fruitful work over and over. That's how wisely he looked after himself.

This saving plan, long hidden, he revealed first to the companions of his age and studies. He added that he meant to put off his royal dress and leave his father's wealth behind.[^14-2] He'd cross the sea by ship to explore distant deserts and vast wildernesses. Far from home, forgetting his country and his people, he could give himself more freely to the one he carried in his heart.

His companions praised the holy plan and begged with one voice that he'd let them share so happy an exile. Saint Efflamm agreed to his friends' fair request. But while they were getting ready for the journey, his father kept Efflamm too busy to leave and arranged for his bride to become his wife. Out of respect for his father he resisted in his heart, not with his mouth. The day was named, and the barons gathered, sharing the king's joy,

<a id="page-15"></a>[\[La Borderie, Saint Efflam (1892), 284\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-15) and the royal halls rang with festive noise. So the wedding was held in the usual way, and Efflamm went to the marriage bed with his bride, both of them virgins.[^15-1]

### Efflamm leaves his bride, lands in Brittany and finds Arthur hunting a monster

Second lesson

Efflamm didn't soothe his wife with the sweet talk of Venus, but with chaste words. Efflamm urged her to love Yahweh with all her heart and all her soul,[^15-2] adding humble prayers that she'd work to carry her womanhood in no womanly way.[^15-3] The examples given were the many virgins who served Yahweh with chaste bodies and devoted minds and happily earned the prize of eternal life.

So this saving husband brought it about that the girl who'd just wanted to lose her virginity now longed with all her heart to keep it untouched. From her kind answer the holy man's good sense saw that his wife had been converted. All night the groom moved his bride towards virtue, and she taught him no less, since she'd already become a chosen vessel and received the grace of the Holy Spirit.[^15-4]

So when the moment was right, he laid out his holy plan to his partner from start to finish. She was stunned to hear it, and it sank deeper into her heart than he thought. She turned it over and over, wondering what this was and where it led. But a whole night awake had worn her out, she was faint with worry, and fell asleep.

The saint mistrusted a woman's weakness, since she's a clay pot,[^15-5] with no self-control. He was already sorry he'd laid the matter bare to her, so he slipped out of the royal bedroom and hurried to the harbour. There he found his companions getting the ship ready, as he'd told them to the day before. Mild air and a wind that Yahweh then gave in plenty urged them on. They put to sea with Yahweh as their guide, and with a fair breeze blowing they came to Brittany.

<a id="page-16"></a>[\[La Borderie, Saint Efflam (1892), 258 [285]\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-16) They landed at the rock the people of that province call Hyrglas and brought the ship in happily. On that beach, about a mile from it, there was a stone cave, four metres or so deep from its entrance.

The holy men went ashore, glad to have reached land. While they walked along the shore admiring the lay of the place, they happened on a monster coming out of the woods and heading for the cave, whose mouth opened about five metres wide. The beast turned its tracks with amazing cunning and went backward,[^16-1] to rob whoever followed of any skill at tracking by that false trail. Who'd believe it had such a trick in it? It had fooled plenty of hunters like that. In the same way it had often dodged a meeting with the mighty Arthur, who at that time was hunting monsters in those parts of Brittany. But at last, by Yahweh's will, the deadly beast's cunning was fooled.

As it happened, Arthur had set his whole mind on what the beast was doing,[^16-2] and was tracking it everywhere. While he was searching around the rocks full of hiding places, he met the holy men on the shore. He was amazed that they'd dared to live in such a vast, dreadful wilderness, and asked them carefully who they were and where they were from. They told him, and showed him for certain where the monster's cave was.

Arthur was glad the saints had come, and he rejoiced with his whole heart that the den had been shown to him. Many times he'd gone away angry at himself and sad, as if the monster had beaten him, since he couldn't track it down.

<a id="page-17"></a>[\[La Borderie, Saint Efflam (1892), 286\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-17) So he armed himself with a three-knotted club and a shield covered in a lion's skin to guard his brave chest. Then he charged the common enemy like a man, fighting alone for everyone.

Against him, the horrible monster defended itself with its own weapons. Worse, it made the first attack on Arthur, outraged that a single man would come at it. It had so often beaten whole peoples. Gathering its strength it charged and struck Arthur's strong shield, which its sharp claws pierced easily. No wonder: even the hardness of stone gave way to their points, as that place still bears witness, carrying the gashes in itself. Arthur pulled his body back and was unhurt. Angrier now, he struck the beast with all his might. A stroke like that would have knocked down walls, but the enemy's tough hide kept it from killing. Still, the pain sank deep. He fought that way all day long, and still the serpent had no deadly wound.

### Efflamm's prayer brings water from the rock for the thirsty Arthur

Third lesson

When Arthur felt night coming on, he put the fight off until the next day. He went back to the holy men, tired from the work and faint with heat. His throat was parched with a fierce thirst, and he asked them for a drink. Efflamm answered for them all: "My lord, we have no water. We need it just as much. So all that's left is to ask Yahweh for it."

Arthur praised the saint's fair advice. They all knelt and lay face down on the ground. Efflamm, stretched out full length, prayed as the voice of all his companions:

"Yahweh, your unchanging power created everything without any matter laid down beforehand. Every single thing serves you by its own nature, and at your command all things change their quality, so that hard things melt soft and rough things become smooth. Show your power over us. In the desert you ordered water to flow from the rock for a thirsty people when your servant Moses prayed.[^17-1] In the same way, Yahweh, in all your mercy, please pour some out for us."

<a id="page-18"></a>[\[La Borderie, Saint Efflam (1892), 287\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-18) When the prayer was over, Efflamm climbed the high boulder, made the sign of the cross and struck it. Water burst into the air in a great stream.[^18-1] The thirsty man drank and gave thanks, then fell face down at Saint Efflamm's feet. Arthur begged the saint to lay hands on him in blessing and remember him in prayers to Yahweh.

Arthur often had to risk his life to free his people, unsure if he'd survive. The holy man blessed him and promised to do as he asked.

### Efflamm drives the monster into the sea, and an angel feeds the saints

Fourth lesson

Arthur went away happily. The water from heaven had ended his thirst and restored his strength. The saint's blessing protected him. Arthur trusted Efflamm's power and let him decide how to fight the monster. Armed with faith, Christ's fighter ordered the huge beast to come out. To strengthen his companions' belief, Efflamm prayed aloud:

"Jesus Christ, my lord, you gave your disciples authority to bind and free whatever they wished.[^18-2] You also said they'd order demons to flee and pick up snakes.[^18-3] Tell this serpent to leave. Save this country from such a deadly threat, so it can give you praise and thanks."

His companions answered, "Amen." The monster rose onto the rock and looked in every direction. It let out a cry mixed with a great, pitiful groan, so terrifying that even distant places shook. Then it lowered its head, gagged and vomited blood from its mouth and nostrils. The stones there still look red with it, as fresh as ever, to bear witness.[^18-4] Then, as the saints watched, the beast went down to the sea and headed into deep water, never to return.

<a id="page-19"></a>[\[La Borderie, Saint Efflam (1892), 288\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-19) Through these two miracles Yahweh wanted to honour its servant Efflamm at the start of exile and build confidence in its grace.

Efflamm had left his father and mother and given up everything he owned to follow Yahweh faithfully.[^19-1]

With the monster gone, the holy men could walk more safely along the shore. The further they went inland, the more the beauty of the country pleased them. Walking together, they praised the places they saw, each speaking in turn. As they wandered in wonder, they came to a cell not far from the shore, called Donguel in the Bretons' language. It was built by the ancients, as the material and the workmanship still show. They thanked Yahweh. People hungry for earthly things, looking for a kingdom, would've been less happy to find a royal hall ready for them.

At dawn the next day, after their prayers, the saints went outside. Walking around, they happened on a stream running through grass and followed it to its spring. Its water was clear to the bottom and flowed with a cheerful murmur. Yahweh's servants were glad to drink there, then happily returned to their cell. On the table they found a meal ready for them and an angel sitting beside it.

"Brave men, don't be afraid," the messenger said. "Eat the food your father Yahweh has sent from heaven. It wanted me to comfort you in exile."

The visitor left them comforted. They thanked Yahweh and broke their holy fast with what it provided. At once the grace of the Holy Spirit filled their hearts, and they received the spirit of knowing what was to come.[^19-2]

### Efflamm's bride Enora crosses the sea in a hide boat to follow him

Fifth lesson

They decided to eat this life-giving meal on Sundays, Tuesdays and Fridays, the days they usually broke their fast.[^19-3] No words could describe it!

<a id="page-20"></a>[\[La Borderie, Saint Efflam (1892), 289\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-20) Yet Yahweh had it ready at the appointed hour on those days. The food taught the holy men a quiet, settled life.[^20-1] They went no further, but stayed there in religious service, as Yahweh wished.

Later they decided that each should have a retreat of his own, to spend more time thinking about the divine.[^20-2] They separated, leaving Efflamm alone in the house he loved. His companions made homes nearby and met with him on feast days and at mealtimes.

Meanwhile Enora, who'd shared his bed without losing her virginity, wanted to share his chastity and religious life too. Day and night she worried over how to follow a man who had no known country or fixed home.[^20-3] At last a new idea came to her.[^20-4] She cast off her royal clothes and went secretly, alone, to a nearby harbour. There she told a man living above it what she longed to do. With pleas and payment she persuaded him to make a boat of hide for her strange purpose. He did as she asked.

"Yahweh, I put my soul and body in your hands," she said.[^20-5] "Guard me and guide me on the right paths."

Enora made the sign of the cross on her forehead and wrapped herself in the prepared hide. Trusting in Yahweh, she gave her life to the sea's waves.

<a id="page-21"></a>[\[La Borderie, Saint Efflam (1892), 290\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-21) Led by Yahweh, she landed unharmed below the town above the Léguer, her vessel still whole.[^21-1] An enclosure built of squared stones in the sea below it took her in on the rising tide. When it fell, the walls held her on the dry shore.

Next morning its keeper came for his usual catch. Every day he found a certain kind of fish there, but this was all the sea had left him. At first the man backed away from it, having never seen anything like it. Then he recognised the hide, hoped something lay inside and went closer to pick it up. Secretly carrying it under his cloak, he took it to a hidden part of his house. Work pressed him to hurry on to other things, when suddenly a voice reached his ears. He stood amazed, unable to tell who was speaking or where. By Yahweh's will, the hide came undone. A young woman appeared, talking to the astonished keeper.[^21-2] 

Seeing her left him even more surprised. At last her gentle voice calmed him and brought him to his senses. He asked why she'd been shut in a hide, how she'd travelled that way and what family she came from.[^21-3]

<a id="page-22"></a>[\[La Borderie, Saint Efflam (1892), 291\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-22) The keeper also wanted to know where she meant to go. Once he'd heard her answers, he told her of the holy man's change of life. People throughout Brittany already talked about what Yahweh was doing through Efflamm.

### A tyrant chases Enora to Efflamm's door, and his hand sticks there

Sixth lesson

The tyrant of that town near the enclosure asked what had been found there the day the young woman arrived. As we've said, some fish were caught there every day. When the answer came that, against all custom, the catch was empty, he thought the keeper had tricked him. Furious, the ruler hurried to the man's house and used cruel tyranny to force him to tell the truth. 

Enora had already been shown the way and was hurrying towards Efflamm. The tyrant followed her tracks at speed on horseback. Thinking he was just about to catch her, he let the reins go loose. The chase was useless, and so was his rage. With Yahweh's help, the young woman walked easily ahead, faster than the horse behind her.

The pursuer gave neither himself nor his furious mount a rest. Yet some slowness sent from heaven held him back, and he couldn't catch her. Enora reached Efflamm's home and found shelter from her fear. Just as she was about to enter, her enemy stretched out his right hand to grab her and put his left against the doorframe. The first went stiff; the other stuck to the wood.

<a id="page-23"></a>[\[La Borderie, Saint Efflam (1892), 292\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-23) Trapped there and now regretting his wickedness, the miserable man cried out through tearful prayers and begged the saint for help. Efflamm remembered the holy Gospel, where Yahweh says, "I don't want a sinner to die, but to turn back and live."[^23-1] 

Efflamm went over, touched the tyrant and made his hands work again, restoring his command of himself in Christ's name.

Humbled by heaven's blow, the ruler began to understand what had happened and the offence against Yahweh.

To make amends, he threw himself face down at the holy man's feet and asked forgiveness. He restored lands to the saint for the wrongs he'd done and granted him all his income in that region. Then he took the blessing and went home happy.

### Enora lives apart from Efflamm, and the pilgrim Iestyn hails him

Seventh lesson

Efflamm learned how Enora had travelled and why, and thanked Yahweh, the best of all.

He was delighted with her decision and taught her to understand religious conduct and divine mysteries. For a while they lived together in purity and chastity. But the saint limited this freedom, as though it gave too much pleasure, and banned his wife from looking at him. Still, a woman's spirit is fragile: without his support, she might lose a faithful guardian. He allowed her the comfort of helpful conversation.[^23-2] She was glad of this separation of their bodies, joined as she was to her companion in spiritual love.

With help from the brothers nearby, Enora soon built a church beside her companion's home. Every day she came at the hours of prayer and stood outside by the wall. For a little while she'd talk with the holy man about how to live, then return home without seeing him.

<a id="page-24"></a>[\[La Borderie, Saint Efflam (1892), 293\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-24) Much later Enora left for Cornwall, meaning to stay. She gathered women to serve Yahweh and founded a church with them. She spent the rest of her life in that country and died, winning the prize that lasts forever.[^24-1] She's remembered there every day, and her feast is celebrated with great joy.

In the place he loved, Efflamm served Yahweh faithfully and never again let a woman into his house. Hard on his body and constant in prayer, the saint did good to himself and others.[^24-2]

Efflamm lived his celibate life on earth like a man who already belonged to heaven. Meanwhile a holy man named Iestyn arrived, full of miraculous power. He was coming back from a pilgrimage to the churches of Saint Peter and the other apostles.[^24-3] Worn out by so long a journey, Iestyn wanted to rest, and made his way to Efflamm's cell. He'd lived there before Efflamm and had set out on his travels from it.[^24-4] When he saw Saint Efflamm, Iestyn greeted him:

"Young man of great devotion, loved by Yahweh and the angels, this lodging is less than you deserve, and heaven's palace longs for you. You're a welcome guest here. This house was once my home. Now it belongs to you, and always will. This region will take its name from yours and serve you for many generations. The whole land will owe you itself and its wealth."

### Efflamm and Iestyn each offer the other the hermitage

Eighth lesson

"How happy your parents are to have had such a son, an heir to the kingdom of heaven! Through you, yes, through you, they'll be blessed.[^24-5] Yahweh has been generous to you, as this place and your way of life show more and more."

Iestyn said this and more. His kind host Efflamm answered gently:[^24-6]

"Father of great authority, may Yahweh favour you and repay your work in full. It's right for me to obey you and take your advice. I think it's only fair to give way to your age, since I can see the journey has weakened you and all you've been through has worn you out.[^24-7]

<a id="page-25"></a>[\[La Borderie, Saint Efflam (1892), 294\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-25) "What more is there to say? Even if there were no other reason, your earlier claim would rightly make me leave you this place, since it was yours first."

So they argued, each wanting to give way to the other. Then a voice from heaven comforted them and settled their friendly quarrel:

"Yahweh is pleased with this argument, and here's how it'll end. Yahweh has given this house to Saint Efflamm to keep forever. But this region will take its name from yours, Saint Iestyn. So you'll share the honour, and this place will keep you both in memory."

That's why the district is called Plestin, after Iestyn.[^25-1]

Iestyn lived a deeply devout life in a wood near the place. His presence made the spot so sacred that wonders are still reported there today.[^25-2] Nobody dares even gather the branches rotting on the ground there, let alone break them or cut down a tree.

But who could describe Saint Efflamm's great miracles in his hermitage,[^25-3] his remarkable zeal and his holy life? He pleased Yahweh his father from his mother's breast, and grew in virtue more every day. He ran his course through the world without a pause, and at last he died, giving Yahweh back a pure soul it would accept.[^25-4]

The saints who'd gathered at his death buried him in the place Yahweh had given him. They built a small chapel over the grave to honour it. His precious body lay hidden in the earth for a long time, and as the years passed, people almost forgot him.

### A hermit sees blood rise from the floor of Efflamm's cell

Ninth lesson

<a id="page-26"></a>[\[La Borderie, Saint Efflam (1892), 295\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-26) We believe others made their home in that place without knowing it held so holy a grave. But by Yahweh's grace the treasure hidden so long was found at last and brought out for people's good.

At one time a devout Christian lived there as a hermit. He kept watch in prayer and tamed his body's cravings with sleepless nights, hunger, thirst and cold. Without knowing it, he was blessed Efflamm's true successor and heir. Every week, on a fixed day, Saturday, he made a habit of cleaning Efflamm's cell with great care. One day, while he was busy with this decent, familiar work, he suddenly saw drops of fresh blood rising from a patch of the floor. The sight stunned him so badly that he stood frozen for a long time.

Once the shock passed, the hermit could think clearly again. He tried to work out where those drops came from and what they meant, but got nowhere. At times he felt the urge to dig into the ground, but awe held him back. At last he made up his mind and went for advice. The bishop heard everything he'd seen, in order, and was amazed. After thinking it over, he called together experienced men of deep faith.

Led by the hermit, they made their way to Saint Efflamm's cell. There, on the wise men's advice, they prayed to Yahweh with great devotion and fasted for three days, then dug up the ground. They uncovered the holy body. An inscription lying with it, and many miracles, showed whose it was.[^26-1] Yahweh's kindness had brought the remains to light, and it chose to make its confessor famous on earth through splendid wonders.

<a id="page-27"></a>[\[La Borderie, Saint Efflam (1892), 296\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-27) The eyes of the blind opened, and they could see. Those suffering from fevers that left them gasping cooled and recovered. Those possessed by demons were set free when the evil spirit was driven out, and returned to full health. The lame got the use of their feet back and praised Yahweh together for Saint Efflamm.[^27-1] Many kinds of illness were cured through Efflamm's prayers.

Once the body was known and the miracles seen, the bishop and the whole clergy burst into joyful praise of Yahweh. They were of one voice and one heart.

The king had come with his leading men for the finding of so holy a body. Overjoyed, he honoured the saint with fitting gifts and gave Efflamm fields that stretched far and wide, free of any dues.

All the people of Brittany saw they'd found a true healer of their diseases, and thanked Yahweh, the highest physician, who thunders on high.[^27-2] Praise, honour and glory to Yahweh forever and ever. Amen.

## Part 6: Goeznovius's narrator places Arthur before the saints' migration

### The priest William greets Bishop Eudo

<a id="page-28"></a>[\[La Borderie, Goeznovius (1883), 91\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-28) From the life of Saint Goeznovius.[^28-1]

To my lord and father in Christ, the honoured Bishop Eudo, and to the brothers who serve Christ with him: William, their priest, sends greetings in Jesus. This is the year one thousand and nineteen since Jesus became human, and your twenty-fourth as bishop, my lord.[^28-2]

### Conan Meriadoc brings the Britons over the sea to Armorica

We read in a *British History*[^28-3] that the Britons under Brutus and Corineus conquered Albion and the islands around it, and named it Britain.[^28-4] Their numbers grew, and they saw their kingdom thrive. Then Conan Meriadoc, a fighter of true faith, crossed the sea to the gulf of Armorica in Gaul with a countless crowd of them, since they'd grown too many for one country to hold.[^28-5]

His first seat was beside the river Guilidona, within Plougoulm, at a place still called Castrum Meriadoci.[^28-6] With his Britons, Conan won that whole land between the two seas, as far as the city of Angers, with all the territory of Nantes and Rennes. He did it with a courage worth praising.

<a id="page-29"></a>[\[La Borderie, Goeznovius (1883), 92\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-29) Conan's men killed all the natives, who were still pagans. That's why they were called Pengouet, which means Dog-heads. The women they spared, cutting out only their tongues, so that Breton speech wouldn't be changed through them.[^29-1] They used them as wives and for other work, as the times required.

Churches were then built all over to praise Yahweh, and the whole land was divided into parishes and tribes.[^29-2] By heaven's grace it came to be called Lesser Britain. So the Britons of Armorica and of the island kept the same laws and treated each other with a brother's love. For a long time they were ruled like one people under one government.

### Vortigern brings in the Saxons, Arthur drives them back, and saints flee

In time Vortigern, a king who'd taken the throne by force, called fighting men from Saxony to help him defend the kingdom of Great Britain, which he held unjustly. He made them his partners in ruling it.[^29-3] They were pagans with the devil in them, eager by their very nature to spill human blood. They did the Britons great harm. Later the great Arthur, king of the Britons, brought down their pride for a while.[^29-4] He drove most of them out of the country and forced them into service. But after many famous victories in Britain and Gaul, this same Arthur was at last called away from human affairs,[^29-5] and the way lay open again for the Saxons to come back.

<a id="page-30"></a>[\[La Borderie, Goeznovius (1883), 93\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-30) The Britons were crushed, churches were torn down and saints were persecuted. The persecution went on through the reigns of many kings of the Saxons and the Britons, who fought each other. These Saxons took the name Angles, or English, from Anglia, the oldest city of Saxony, and gave it to the island as well. But to this day the Britons still call them Saxons.

At that time many holy men freely offered themselves for martyrdom.[^30-1] Others followed the Gospel's advice and crossed from Great Britain, which is now the Saxons' country,[^30-2] to this Lesser Britain. Some fled the pagans' tyranny. Far more went quietly, giving up everything,[^30-3] so they could serve Yahweh more faithfully in lonely places, in a way it would welcome.

### Tudogilus leaves Britain with Goeznovius and his other children

In those days there was a man named Tudogilus, born to one of the Britons' noble families. He had two sons and a daughter, we read. The firstborn was called Goeznovius, the second Majan, and the girl Tudona. Tudogilus left his country and everything he owned and came to Lesser Britain with his children.

<a id="page-31"></a>[\[La Borderie, Goeznovius (1883), 94\]](../the-saints-who-meet-arthur-latin.md#page-31) He built a chapel within Plebs Denarii, between the rivers Bazlananda and Doena, which run along both sides of that parish and mix with the sea. There he lived a hermit's life for a long time. At last, old and rich in good works, Tudogilus died a blessed death in Yahweh.[^31-1]

Majan built one of his own on the other side of the inlet that now lies between the two places, about a quarter of a mile from Castellum Collobii.

Tudona, the saints' sister, chose a spiritual marriage over the pleasures of the flesh. Such a union may open in mourning, but it leads to happiness without end. The other way starts in joy, but it slides towards grief and closes in misery. She built a chapel first in Albennoca, then another in Belnoci, and gave herself up to Yahweh.

Goeznovius came last and put up his at a place named Landa, four miles from the city of the Osismii.[^31-2] Today it's called Peniticium Goeznovei, Goeznovius's place of penance.
