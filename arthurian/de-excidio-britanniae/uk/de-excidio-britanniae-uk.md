# On the Ruin of Britain
<!-- locale: en-GB -->

*On the Ruin of Britain*, by Gildas, translated by [Ryan Allen](../de-excidio-britanniae-how-it-was-made.md#ryan-allen)

[All the Arthurian books](../../index.md) · **Read in:** [US English](../de-excidio-britanniae.md)

## About this translation

**Written:** 540–547 CE

This book is fiction. It's a sermon by a real man of the 500s, and what it says of the past before him is hearsay and error. He says himself he had no British records to go on, he puts the Roman walls centuries too late, and he bends the whole past to one point: that Yahweh sent the Saxons to punish the Britons for their sins. Ambrosius and the siege of Badon can't be checked against anything else from his time. He never names Arthur.

Gildas wrote *On the Ruin of Britain* in Latin around 540–547 CE, the only book by a Briton that survives from the century Arthur is set in. It isn't a story of Arthur but the background to every one: it tells how the Romans left, how the Saxons came, and how Ambrosius Aurelianus led the Britons to the siege of Mount Badon. Later writers built Arthur on it. The [*History of the Britons*](../../historia-brittonum/historia-brittonum.md), written almost three hundred years later, makes Badon the last of Arthur's twelve battles, and Geoffrey of Monmouth makes two of the kings Gildas condemns Arthur's successors. Gildas names Ambrosius but no leader at Badon, and that gap is the main evidence in the long argument over whether Arthur was real. Most of the book, from Part 5 on, is a sermon against Britain's rulers and priests, built on quotation after quotation from the Bible. Six hundred years later, Caradoc of Llancarfan wrote [a life of Gildas](../../vita-gildae/vita-gildae.md) that brings in Arthur.

**The text.** Theodor Mommsen dates the book to a few years before 547 CE, the year the [*Welsh Annals*](../../annales-cambriae/annales-cambriae.md) give for the death of King Maelgwn. Gildas calls out Maelgwn as a living man. Gildas says he was born in the year of Badon, forty-four years before he wrote. This translation follows the Latin as Mommsen printed it in 1898 from London, British Library, Cotton MS Vitellius A VI, an eleventh-century copy now mostly lost or faded, filled where it can't be read from Avranches 162 and Cambridge, University Library, Dd. I. 17. The Bible quotations are translated from Gildas's own Latin. The endnotes cite each one as a modern English Bible names and numbers it, so the Psalms are numbered as in English Bibles, most of them one higher than in the Latin Bible Gildas read.

**In the Arthur story.** Gildas never names Arthur, but later writers built the legend on his tyrant, his battle and his kings. Wherever he names someone or something the Arthur story retells, a box straight after the paragraph, headed "In the Arthur story," says what the legend makes of it and in which books. Those boxes, like the endnotes and glossary cards that end on an asterisk, are this translation's words, not Gildas's.

## Part 1: Gildas says why he writes after ten years of keeping silent

### Gildas has kept silent ten years, and the Bible's warnings move him to write

<a id="page-25"></a>[\[Mommsen 25\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-25) This letter is more tears than speech.[^25-1] It's badly written, but it's meant well. Don't think I write it because I look down on everyone, or think I'm better than anyone. I'm crying over what we've all lost: the good draining away, the bad piling up. I feel for my country's troubles, and I'd be glad to see it healed. I mean to tell of a cruel war and its dangers, not for the bravest soldiers but for the cowards.

> **In the Arthur story:** Gildas never names Arthur in this letter, and later writers wondered why. Gerald of Wales, writing about 1194 CE, says Arthur had killed Gildas's brother, so Gildas threw everything he'd written in Arthur's praise into the sea. Caradoc of Llancarfan's *Life of Gildas*, also on this shelf, tells the same quarrel: Arthur hunts down and kills Gildas's brother Hueil, and Gildas forgives him. The *Life* also has Gildas make peace between Arthur and Melwas, who had carried Guinevere off to Glastonbury.<sup>[\*](#new-in-this-translation)</sup>

I'll admit I kept silent for ten years or more, and it hurt me deeply. Yahweh who searches the heart is my witness. My lack of skill held me back, as it still does, and so did my own poor merits. I didn't write even the smallest warning.

All the same, I read the old stories. Moses, who gave Israel its law, never entered the promised land, for doubting a single word.[^25-2] Aaron's sons died quickly for bringing strange fire to the altar.[^25-3] A people who broke Yahweh's commands, six hundred thousand strong,[^25-4] fell scattered through the deserts of Arabia to wild animals,[^25-5] the sword and flames;[^25-6][^25-7] only two truthful men, Joshua and Caleb, were spared.[^25-8] Yet Yahweh loved them deeply. It laid them a smooth road over the gravel of the Red Sea's bed.[^25-9] Their food was bread from heaven,[^25-10] their drink new water from a rock that travelled with them.[^25-11] Moses's raised hands alone made their army unbeatable.[^25-12]

I read how they passed through the unknown gate of the Jordan,[^25-13] and the walls of Jericho fell at Yahweh's command when the trumpets sounded.[^25-14] A small cloak and a little gold that Achan stole from the plunder Yahweh had banned brought many down.[^25-15] The treaty with the Gibeonites was won by a trick,[^25-16] yet breaking it cost lives.[^25-17] The holy prophets complained of people's sins, Jeremiah most of all, who mourned the ruin of his home in four alphabets.[^25-18]

<a id="page-26"></a>[\[Mommsen 26\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-26) I saw in our own time what he'd cried over. "The city sits alone, a widow, once full of people, mistress of nations, first of the provinces, now made to pay tribute": meaning the Church.[^26-1] "The gold has darkened, and its best colour has changed": that's the splendour of Yahweh's word.[^26-2] "The sons of Zion," our holy mother the Church, "famous and dressed in the purest gilt, have clung to piles of manure."[^26-3] He was a great man, and found it unbearable. Low as I am, it added to my grief too. He mourned a people who had lived so finely in good times that he said, "Her Nazirites were whiter than snow, redder than old ivory, lovelier than sapphire."[^26-4]

I looked at these things and many more in the old scriptures, as a mirror of our own life. Then I turned to the new ones. There I saw more clearly what had perhaps been dark to me before, now that the shadow was gone and the truth shone steadier. I read that Jesus said, "I came only to the lost sheep of the house of Israel."[^26-5] Yet he warned his own people: "The sons of this kingdom will be thrown out into the dark outside, where there'll be crying and grinding of teeth."[^26-6] To a foreign woman he said: "It isn't right to take the children's bread and throw it to the dogs."[^26-7] He told the religious teachers: "Doom to you, scribes and Pharisees, you hypocrites."[^26-8]

I heard, "Many will come from east and west and sit down with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven."[^26-9] But he warned others who called him lord: "Then I'll say to them, Get away from me, you who do evil."[^26-10]

I read, "Happy are the barren, and the breasts that never nursed."[^26-11] But in his story of the ten who waited up with their lamps: "Those who were ready went in with him to the wedding. Afterwards the other girls came, saying, lord, lord, open to us. The answer was, I don't know you."[^26-12] I heard plainly, "Anyone who believes and is baptised will be saved, but those without faith will be condemned."[^26-13] I read in Paul's letters that a wild olive branch grafted onto the good tree would be cut off from the richness of its root if it lost its fear and grew proud.[^26-14]

I knew Yahweh's mercy, but I feared its judgement too. I praised its grace, but I dreaded that it pays each of us back for what we've done.[^26-15] Sheep of one fold weren't all alike. So I rightly called Peter most blessed for confessing Christ completely,[^26-16] and Judas most miserable for his love of money.[^26-17] Stephen was glorious for winning a martyr's palm,[^26-18] Nicolaus pitiful for the stain of his filthy heresy.[^26-19] I read, "They held everything in common."[^26-20] But also: "Why have you agreed to put Yahweh's spirit to the test?"[^26-21] Then I saw how careless the people of our time had grown, as if there were nothing to fear.

<a id="page-27"></a>[\[Mommsen 27\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-27) I turned all this over many times, stunned, my heart stung, with much more I've left out to be brief. Yahweh set a people apart from every nation,[^27-1] a royal seed and a holy race,[^27-2] and said to it, "Israel is my firstborn."[^27-3] Yet when they strayed from the straight path, it didn't spare them, or their priests, prophets and kings through so many centuries, or its own apostle, or the members of the first Church. What, then, will it do with the black ink of our own age?[^27-4]

It commits the wicked, monstrous sins of every criminal in the world. It also carries a weight of foolishness and fickleness they seem to be born with. It can't be wiped out or escaped.

What then? I say to myself: "You poor fool, were you given such a charge, as if you were a famous, leading teacher? Are you to stand against so violent a flood, against this rope of rooted crimes stretched long and wide, unbroken for so many years? Keep what was entrusted to you, and say nothing.[^27-5] Anything else is telling the foot to keep watch and the hand to speak. Britain has rulers. It has watchmen. Why do you plan to mutter your nonsense? It has them, it has them, if not more than it needs, then no fewer."

But those men are bent under so great a weight that they've no room to breathe. So objections like these, and much sharper ones, kept crowding into my mind, as if they shared my debt. As I said, they wrestled there a long time, as if in a narrow porch of dread. I'd read that there's "a time to speak and a time to be silent."[^27-6] At last the creditor won.

"Maybe you aren't bold enough to wear the fitting mark of golden freedom," it said, "among the creatures of truth and reason, who rank next to the angels. Then at least don't run from the example of the donkey that understood. It had no speech until Yahweh's spirit breathed on it. It wouldn't carry Balaam, the turbaned magician who meant to curse Yahweh's people, and it crushed his foot against the narrow wall of the vineyards.

"It felt his blows like an enemy's. Still it pointed out to him, almost with a finger, the angel from heaven standing against him, his sword out of its sheath. Blinded by his raw stupidity, ungrateful and raging, he hadn't seen him there. He went on beating its innocent sides against all right."[^27-7]

So now I pay a debt that was demanded of me long ago. Zeal for the house of Yahweh drives me to it, with the reasons of its holy law and my own thoughts, and my brothers' devout prayers. It's a poor payment, but faithful, I think. It's friendly to all of Christ's fine young soldiers, and heavy and unbearable to the foolish ones who've deserted the faith. If I'm not mistaken, the first will take it with tears that flow from Yahweh's love. The others will take it with grief too, but the kind wrung from a guilty conscience caught out, by anger and cowardice.

### Gildas sets out what he'll tell of Britain before he calls out its people

<a id="page-28"></a>[\[Mommsen 28\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-28) But before I keep that promise, god willing, I'll try to say a little about the island. First its place in the world, its defiance and conquest, its rebellion and its harsh slavery again. Then its religion, its persecution and holy martyrs, its many heresies and its tyrants. Then the two peoples who laid it waste, its defence, a second ravaging, a second revenge and a third plundering. Then its famine, the letters to Agitius, its triumph, its crimes, the enemies suddenly announced and the famous plague. Last, the meeting of advisers, the enemy far crueler than the first, the overthrow of cities, the survivors, and the country's last victory, granted in our time at Yahweh's will.

## Part 2: Britain is described, and the Romans conquer it, rule it and leave

### Gildas describes the island of Britain, its rivers, cities, hills and streams

The island of Britain lies almost at the farthest edge of the world, towards the northwest and west.[^28-1] It's balanced, as they say, by the divine scales that weigh the whole earth. It runs from the southwest up to the northern pole, eight hundred miles long and two hundred across, not counting its many long headlands, wrapped by the ocean's curving bays. A broad ring of sea guards it on every side, all but impassable, except the strait to the south, where people sail to Belgic Gaul.[^28-2]

> **In the Arthur story:** Geoffrey of Monmouth opens his *History of the Kings of Britain*, about 1136 CE, on this description of the island, eight hundred miles long and two hundred across, and calls Britain the best of islands. It's the kingdom Arthur rules in Geoffrey and in every telling built on him, from Wace's French and Layamon's English to Malory.<sup>[\*](#new-in-this-translation)</sup>

Two fine rivers enrich it, the Thames and the Severn, like two arms, and smaller ones too; goods from overseas were once carried in along them by boat. Twenty-eight cities and some forts adorn it, well built with walls, notched towers, gates and houses. Their roofs rose to a threatening height on strong frames.

<a id="page-29"></a>[\[Mommsen 29\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-29) Its plains spread wide and its hills lie pleasantly, fit for heavy farming, and its mountains suit grazing animals. Flowers of many colours, pressed by people's feet, paint them a picture far from ugly, like a chosen bride wearing different necklaces. Plenty of bright springs feed it, pushing snowy pebbles along in their streams. Shining brooks glide with a gentle murmur, promising sweet sleep to anyone lying on their banks. Lakes overflow with a cold torrent of living water.

### The Britons defy Yahweh and their rulers, and Gildas turns to Roman times

Ever since people first lived on it, this island has risen up, stiff in neck and mind. It's been ungrateful now to Yahweh, now to its own citizens, and at times to kings from overseas and their subjects as well. What could human daring do, or let in, that's uglier or more wicked? To refuse Yahweh fear, good people love, and those in high rank their due honour, short of harming the faith. To break your word to Yahweh and to people. To dread neither heaven nor earth, and live by your own schemes and lusts.

So I'll leave aside the ancient errors common to every people, which bound the whole human race before Christ came in the flesh. I won't count my country's own devilish monsters, nearly more than Egypt's. We still see some of them inside or outside the abandoned walls, as ugly and stiff as ever, their faces grim. I won't name the mountains, hills and rivers themselves or cry out against them. They were deadly once and are useful to people now, but a blind people heaped divine honours on them.

I'll pass over the old years of its monstrous rulers. They were famous in lands a long way off. Porphyry of Tyre, the rabid dog of the East who barked against the Church, even added this in his mad, empty writing: "Britain," he says, "a province fertile in tyrants."[^29-1]

I'll try to bring out only the wrongs Britain suffered, and did to its own citizens and to distant peoples, in the time of the Romans' emperors. I'll tell them as well as I can. I won't tell them from my own country's writings or records, since if there ever were any, they can't be found. Enemies' fires burned them, or exiles' ships carried them away. I'll tell them from accounts from overseas, which are broken by many gaps and aren't clear enough.

### The Romans conquer the world and bring Britain under their laws

<a id="page-30"></a>[\[Mommsen 30\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-30) The kings of the Romans won rule over the world. They subdued every neighbouring land and island towards the east, and their greater fame made the first peace with the Parthians, on India's borders. After that, wars stopped almost everywhere on earth. The blue flood of the western ocean couldn't hold back or put out the keen, hard edge of their flame.[^30-1] It crossed the sea and brought the island laws to obey, with no one resisting.

The people were no fighters, but they couldn't be trusted. The Romans didn't need sword, fire and siege engines to subdue them, as they had with other peoples. They did it by edict, by threats alone and the terror of their courts. The Britons showed obedience on their faces and pressed their resentment deep into their hearts.

### The Britons rise against Rome's governors, and Rome sends an army against them

As soon as the Romans went back to Rome, the treacherous lioness struck. The Romans had gone home for lack of land, as they said, suspecting no rebellion. She butchered the governors they'd left behind to proclaim their plans for the country more fully and make them firm.[^30-2]

When this was reported to the senate, it rushed an army over to punish the sly little foxes, as it pretended they were. No fleet was ready at sea to fight bravely for the country. No square column, no right wing, no other gear of war was set out on the shore. Instead the Britons turned their backs to the pursuers for a shield and their necks to the swords, a cold shiver running through their bones.[^30-3] They held out their hands like women to be tied. So it became a saying, mocked far and wide, that the Britons are neither brave in war nor faithful in peace.

### The Romans leave overseers over the Britons and go back to Italy

So the Romans killed many of the traitors and kept some as slaves, so the land wouldn't be completely empty. Then they quit the country, which had no wine or oil, and went to Italy. They left some of their own men behind as overseers. These were to lay whips on the natives' backs and the yoke on their necks, and make the name of Roman slavery stick to the soil. They'd wear down the sly people with lashes more than with soldiers, and if need be hold a drawn sword at its side, as the saying goes. So it would be counted not Britain but a Roman land, and any bronze, silver or gold it had would be stamped with Caesar's image.[^30-4]

### Christ's teaching reaches the cold, far island of Britain

<a id="page-31"></a>[\[Mommsen 31\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-31) Meanwhile Christ, the true light, gave his rays, that is, his teachings, to this island, stiff with icy cold in a far corner of the earth, away from the sun we see. He shows his dazzling brightness to the whole world, not from the sky of this passing time alone but from heaven's highest point, beyond all time. His light first came here, as we know, late in the reign of Tiberius Caesar.[^31-1] In those days his religion spread with nothing to stop it, since the emperor, against the senate's will, threatened death to anyone who informed against Christ's soldiers.[^31-2]

### Diocletian's persecution reaches Britain's churches and holy books

The island's people took up Christ's word half-heartedly. Still it lasted, fully with some and less with others, until the nine-year persecution of the tyrant Diocletian.[^31-3] In it churches were thrown down all over the world. Every holy book that could be found was burned in the streets. The chosen priests of Yahweh's flock were butchered with their innocent sheep, so that in some provinces no trace of the Christian religion would show, if that could be done.

The Church's history tells of it all:[^31-4] the flights, the slaughter, the many kinds of death, those who fell away from the faith, the crowns of glorious martyrs,[^31-5] the persecution's rabid fury and the saints' patience against it. The whole Church hurried in a dense column, each trying to be first, leaving the world's darkness behind for the lovely kingdoms of heaven, as if going home.

### Yahweh gives Britain its martyrs Alban, Aaron and Julius

So Yahweh showed us its great mercy. It wants everyone saved, and calls sinners no less than those who think themselves righteous. As a free gift, in the time of that persecution, as we suppose, it lit the brightest lamps of holy martyrs for us. So Britain wouldn't be completely darkened by the thick gloom of black night.

Their graves and the places where they suffered would now fire a great love of Yahweh in those who saw them. But because of our sins, the barbarians' bitter partition has taken so much of this from our people. I mean Saint Alban of Verulamium,[^31-6] Aaron and Julius, citizens of the City of the Legions,[^31-7] and the others, men and women, who stood firm in Christ's battle line in many places with the greatest courage.

> **In the Arthur story:** The City of the Legions is Caerleon, on the Usk in south Wales. Geoffrey of Monmouth makes it Arthur's grandest city: Arthur wears his crown there at a great Whitsun feast, and Geoffrey gives it two churches, one for Julius and one for Aaron, the martyrs Gildas names here. The Welsh tales of *Owain*, *Geraint* and *Peredur* open with Arthur holding court at Caerleon, and Malory's Arthur holds a feast there early in his reign.<sup>[\*](#new-in-this-translation)</sup>

### Alban hides a hunted confessor and takes his place

Alban was the first of them. Out of love he hid a confessor who was being hunted down and was about to be caught. In this too he imitated Christ, who lays down his life for his sheep.

<a id="page-32"></a>[\[Mommsen 32\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-32) He sheltered the man first in his house, then traded clothes with him, and was happy to face the hunt himself, dressed as his brother. Between confessing Christ and shedding his blood, he pleased Yahweh. In front of the wicked men who carried Rome's standards with horrid pomp in those days, Yahweh blessed him with wonderful miracles.

By fervent prayer he opened an unknown way. It was like the parched, little-used road of the Israelites when the ark of the covenant stood long on the gravel in the middle of the Jordan. With a thousand men he crossed the bed of the Thames, that great river, their feet dry, its waters hanging on either side like steep mountains.[^32-1] His first executioner saw such wonders and changed from a wolf into a lamb. Alban made him thirst more fiercely for the triumphant palm of martyrdom with him, and take it more bravely.

The rest were tortured with every kind of torment and torn apart in ways unheard of. Without delay they set up the trophies of their glorious death, as if at the fine gates of Jerusalem. Those who survived hid in woods, deserts and secret caves. They waited for Yahweh, the just ruler of all, to bring harsh judgement someday on their executioners, and protection for their own souls.

### The persecution ends, the churches are rebuilt, and Arian teaching divides them

Before ten years of that storm had run their course, the wicked edicts withered as the men who made them died. Then all of Christ's young soldiers welcomed with glad eyes the mild weather and clear light of heaven, as if after a long winter night.[^32-2] They rebuilt the churches that had been torn down to the ground. They founded, built and finished shrines of the holy martyrs, and set them up everywhere like banners of victory. They kept feast days and made the sacraments with clean hearts and mouths. All the Church's sons rejoiced, as if warmed in their mother's lap.

This sweet harmony between Christ the head and his members lasted until the Arians betrayed it.[^32-3][^32-4] Savage as a snake, it spewed its poisons on us from overseas and split brothers who had lived as one, to their ruin. Then, as if a road had opened across the ocean, wild beasts of every kind sank lethal bites into the country. From their horrible mouths they darted the deadly venom of one heresy or another. It was a land always wanting to hear something new, and never holding firmly to anything.

### Britain sends Maximus to Gaul, and he makes himself emperor at Trier

At last the saplings of tyrants grew and were about to burst out into a monstrous forest. The island kept the Roman name, but not Roman ways and law. It cast off a shoot of its own bitterest planting and sent it to Gaul.

<a id="page-33"></a>[\[Mommsen 33\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-33) It was Maximus. A great band of followers went with him, and even imperial insignia, though he never wore them fittingly anywhere. A rioting army had made him emperor, not lawfully but in a tyrant's way. By cunning rather than courage, through the nets of his perjury and lies, he first joined every neighbouring district and province to his wicked kingdom, against the Roman state. He stretched one wing to Spain and the other to Italy, and set up the throne of his most unjust empire at Trier.

> **In the Arthur story:** Maximus, the Roman general in Britain who made himself emperor in 383 CE, comes into the Arthur story twice. Geoffrey of Monmouth's Maximianus takes Britain's young men across the sea and settles them in Armorica under Conan Meriadoc, so Brittany begins, and Arthur later names him among the Britons who ruled Rome when he refuses to pay Rome tribute. The Welsh tale *The Dream of Macsen Wledig* makes him a hero who dreams of a bride in Britain and crosses the sea to marry her.<sup>[\*](#new-in-this-translation)</sup>

He raged so madly against his masters that he drove out two lawful emperors, one from Rome and the other from his most holy life. Before long, hemmed in by such deadly ventures, he had his wicked head cut off at the city of Aquileia. He'd thrown down, in a way, the crowned leaders of the whole world's empire.[^33-1]

### The Irish and the Picts raid Britain, left without its soldiers

After that, Britain was stripped of all its armed soldiers, its military forces and its rulers, cruel as they were. It lost its great mass of young men too, who followed in that tyrant's footsteps and never came home again.[^33-2] Knowing nothing at all of war, it was trampled first by two very fierce peoples from overseas, the Irish from the northwest and the Picts from the north. It groaned in shock for many years.

> **In the Arthur story:** The Picts and the Irish, the Scots in Geoffrey of Monmouth, become Arthur's next enemies after the Saxons. In Geoffrey, Arthur traps them on the islands of Loch Lomond and starves them until their bishops come barefoot to beg for mercy. Wace and Layamon tell the same siege.<sup>[\*](#new-in-this-translation)</sup>

### The Britons write to Rome, and a legion comes and orders a wall built

Under their attacks and harsh oppression, it sent envoys to Rome with letters. In tears it begged for an armed force to defend it. It vowed to be subject to the Roman empire always, with all its heart, if the raiders were kept far off. A legion was soon sent, forgetting past wrongs and well equipped with arms.

It crossed the ocean to the country on ships and fought the cruel enemy at close quarters. It struck down great numbers of them, drove them all from the borders, and freed the subject people from the savage slaughter and the captivity hanging over them. It told them to build a wall across the island between the two seas. Once manned, it would hold off the raiders, a terror to them and a protection to the citizens. But a crowd with no sense and no leader built it of turf rather than stone, and it did no good.[^33-3]

### The legion goes home, and the old enemies return by sea

<a id="page-34"></a>[\[Mommsen 34\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-34) As the legion went home in great triumph and joy, the old enemies returned. They came like ravenous wolves mad with hunger, leaping into the fold with dry jaws when no shepherd is in sight. Carried on the wings of oars, the arms of rowers and sails swelling in the wind, they broke through the borders and killed everyone. Whatever they met, they cut down like ripe grain, trampled and passed over.

### The Britons beg Rome again, and Roman horse and ships come to their aid

Again envoys were sent with complaints, their clothes torn, as the saying goes, and dust on their heads, begging the Romans for help. Like frightened chicks, they huddled under the most faithful wings of their fathers. Otherwise their poor country would be wiped out, and the Romans' name, already only an empty word in their ears, would be gnawed by foreigners' contempt and grow worthless.

The Romans were moved, as much as human nature can be, by the story of such a tragedy. Like eagles in flight they sped their horsemen on land and their sailors at sea. Suddenly at first, then terribly, they drove the claws of their swords into their enemies' necks. They cut them down like leaves falling in their season.

They came like a mountain torrent swollen by many storm streams. It bursts its banks with a roaring course, its back furrowed and its crest sharp, its waves raised to the clouds, as they say. The eyes of those watching blur at its whirling lines, however often they blink. It foams wonderfully as it overcomes the masses set against it with a single surge.[^34-1] So these fine helpers drove the enemy's columns headlong overseas, those that could escape at all. Until then the raiders had carried their plunder across the water greedily every year, with no one resisting.

### The Romans tell the Britons to defend themselves and help them build a wall

Then the Romans told the country they could no longer be troubled so often with such hard expeditions. They wouldn't wear out the Roman standards, so great and fine an army, on land and sea for the sake of roving bandits who were no soldiers. Instead, they urged, the Britons should get used to arms on their own. They should fight like men with all their strength for their soil, property, wives and children. Their freedom and lives counted for even more. Their enemies were no stronger than they were, unless laziness and sloth undid them. So they should hold out hands that weren't bare and fit for chains, but armed with shields, swords and spears, ready to kill.

<a id="page-35"></a>[\[Mommsen 35\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-35) The Romans thought this too would help the people they were leaving behind. So they built a wall unlike the first, with public and private money, the poor natives working with them. They set it the usual way, straight from one sea to the other, between the cities that happened to be placed there for fear of the enemy. They gave the frightened people firm advice and left them patterns for making weapons. On the ocean's shore to the south, where their ships were kept, they set towers at intervals to watch the water, since the savage barbarian beasts were feared from that side too. Then they said goodbye, meaning never to come back.

## Part 3: Picts and Irish raid, the Britons write to Agitius, and Saxons are invited in

### The Irish and Picts land again and take the north up to the wall

So, as the Romans went home, the foul hordes of the Irish and the Picts poured out of the coracles that had carried them across the Tithica valley,[^35-1][^35-2] each trying to land first.[^35-3] They came like dark swarms of worms wriggling out of the narrowest cracks of their holes when the sun is high and the heat grows. They differed somewhat in their ways, but they shared one and the same lust for shedding blood. They covered their evil faces with hair more than their private parts, and what's next to them, with clothes.

They learned that the Britons' allies had gone home and refused to come back. So they grew bolder than ever, and took the whole north and the far end of the land, up to the wall, as if they were its natives.[^35-4]

Against them a force was set on top of the fortifications. It was slow to fight, unfit to flee, useless with trembling hearts, and it wasted away day and night on its stupid seat. Meanwhile the naked men's hooked weapons never stopped. They dragged the miserable citizens from the walls and dashed them to the ground. An early death at least did this for those it snatched away: they escaped quickly from the pitiful sufferings that hung over their brothers and children.

What more is there to say? They left their cities and the high wall. Once again the citizens fled. Once again they scattered, more hopelessly than ever. Once again the enemy hunted them, and once again the slaughter came faster and crueler. Their enemies tore the pitiful people apart like lambs by butchers, and they lived like wild animals. They even robbed each other, to keep themselves alive a little while on the scraps the poorest had. Unrest at home added to the disasters from outside. At last the constant plunder stripped every region of its staff of food, except what comfort hunting gave.

### The Britons write to Agitius, then hold out in the hills and strike back

<a id="page-36"></a>[\[Mommsen 36\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-36) So the desperate survivors sent a letter again, to Agitius, a man of Roman power. They wrote: "To Agitius, consul for the third time, the groans of the Britons." A little further on they complained: "The barbarians push us back to the sea, and the waves drive us back to them. Between them we die one of two deaths, slaughtered or drowned." But they got no help for it.[^36-1]

> **In the Arthur story:** Geoffrey of Monmouth keeps this plea, the groans of the Britons, and carries it on. Refused by Rome, the Britons turn to Brittany, whose king sends his brother Constantine with an army. Constantine wins and is crowned, and his three sons are Constans, Aurelius Ambrosius and Uther Pendragon, Arthur's father. So in the legend this letter brings Arthur's grandfather to Britain. Robert de Boron's *Merlin* opens on the same family, with King Constans and his sons.<sup>[\*](#new-in-this-translation)</sup>

Meanwhile a terrible, notorious famine clung to them as they wandered and wavered. It forced many of them to surrender at once to the bloody robbers, for a little food to keep them alive. Others never gave in. They kept fighting back from the mountains themselves, from caves and passes and thick thornbushes. Then for the first time they slaughtered the enemies who had plundered the land for many years. They trusted not in people but in Yahweh, as Philo says: "Divine help must come where human help stops."[^36-2] The raiders' boldness rested for a while, but not our wickedness. The enemy drew back from the Britons, but our people didn't turn away from their crimes.

### The raiders go home, and Britain grows rich and wicked in peace

It's always been this people's habit, and still is, to be weak against an enemy's weapons but strong in civil war and in carrying the burden of sin. Feeble at following the banners of peace and truth; bold for crimes and lies. So the Irish, shameless raiders, went home, to come back before long. The Picts settled in the far end of the island for the first time, and stayed there from then on, though they sometimes plundered and did damage.

In that truce a cruel wound scabbed over for the desolate people. But a deadlier famine, of another kind, sprouted silently. While the destruction rested, Britain overflowed with more plenty than any age before could remember. Luxury of all sorts came with it.

<a id="page-37"></a>[\[Mommsen 37\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-37) It flourished so strongly that it could rightly be said then: "There's sexual sin heard of among you of a kind not found even with the pagans."[^37-1] Not only this vice spread, but every one that human nature is prone to. Above all came what still overturns all good in it. People hated truth and those who defended it. They loved lies and those who told them. They took evil for good and honoured wickedness as kindness. They wanted darkness rather than the sun, and welcomed Satan as an angel of light.[^37-2]

Kings were anointed, not by Yahweh, but because they were crueler than the rest. Soon after, the men who had raised them butchered them, with no inquiry into the truth, and chose others more savage still. If one of them seemed gentler and somewhat closer to what was right, everyone's hatred and weapons turned on him without a second thought, as if he were Britain's destroyer. What displeased Yahweh and what pleased it weighed the same, when what offended it wasn't actually preferred.

So Isaiah's words to that ancient people could rightly be fitted to our country: "Lawless sons, you've abandoned Yahweh and provoked the Holy One of Israel to anger. Why be struck again, adding more wickedness? Every head is sick and each heart is sad; from the sole of the foot to the top of the skull there's nothing healthy in it."[^37-3] They did everything against their own salvation, as if the true physician of all had no medicine to give the world.

Not only ordinary people did this, but Yahweh's flock itself and its shepherds, who should have set the whole people an example. Most of them lay dazed with drink, as if soaked in wine. They were torn by swelling hatreds, bitter quarrels, the grasping claws of envy and a judgement that couldn't tell good from bad. So it plainly seemed, as it does now, that contempt was poured out on the princes. Their own empty pursuits misled them, and they wandered where there's no path, off the road.[^37-4]

### News comes that the old enemies are coming, but the Britons don't repent

Meanwhile Yahweh wanted to clean up its household, stained by such a foul plague, and correct it with the mere news of trouble. A rumour everyone knew flew like a winged thing into every pricked-up ear: the old enemies were about to come back, meaning to destroy the whole country and settle it from one end to the other, as before. Yet this did them no good at all.

<a id="page-38"></a>[\[Mommsen 38\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-38) Like senseless beasts, they clamped the bridle of reason between clenched teeth, as the saying goes. They left the narrow path of salvation and ran down the broad road of every vice, which slopes towards death.[^38-1] As Solomon says, "A stubborn slave isn't corrected by words"; the fool is whipped and doesn't feel it.[^38-2] A deadly plague fell savagely on the people. In a short time, without a sword, it struck down so many that the living couldn't bury them all.

Even this didn't reform them. In them too the words of the prophet Isaiah came true. "Yahweh called for mourning, for shaved heads and sackcloth. But look at them killing calves and slaughtering rams, eating and drinking. They say, Let's eat and drink, because tomorrow we die."[^38-3] The time was coming near when their wickedness would be complete, as the Amorites' once was.[^38-4] A meeting was held to decide the best, most wholesome way to drive back the deadly, frequent raids and plunder of the peoples I've named.

### The proud tyrant and his advisers bring the Saxons into Britain

Then all the advisers, together with the proud tyrant,[^38-5] were struck blind.[^38-6] They thought up this protection for the country, or rather its ruin. The Saxons, fiercest of all, of unspeakable name, hated by Yahweh and people, were to be let into the island like wolves into the fold, to drive back the northern peoples. Nothing more disastrous or bitter was ever done to it.

> **In the Arthur story:** Gildas names no one, but every later telling calls the proud tyrant Vortigern. Bede names him about 731 CE, and the *History of the Britons* tells his whole story: he takes in Hengist's Saxons, marries Hengist's daughter, and builds a fort that keeps falling until a fatherless boy shows him two dragons fighting under it. Geoffrey of Monmouth has him arrange the murder of the young King Constans to take the crown, and makes the boy Merlin. Wace, Layamon and Robert de Boron's *Merlin* follow Geoffrey, and each one ends with Vortigern burned in his tower.<sup>[\*](#new-in-this-translation)</sup>

What utter darkness of mind! What hopeless, raw dullness of thought! They'd feared these people more than death while they were away. Now they invited them of their own will, so to speak, under the roof of one house. "Foolish princes of Zoan," as it was said, "giving Pharaoh senseless advice."[^38-7]

Then a pack of cubs burst from the den of the barbarian lioness in three keels, as their own tongue calls them; we'd say long ships.[^38-8] They sailed with fair winds, and with omens and prophecies. A sure sign among them predicted that they'd hold the country they steered for three hundred years, and for a hundred fifty years, half that time, would often lay it waste. At the order of the ill-starred tyrant, they first fixed their terrible claws in the east of the island, as if to fight for the country, but more surely to attack it.

> **In the Arthur story:** These three long ships make the most famous landing in the legend. Bede, the *History of the Britons* and Geoffrey of Monmouth name their leaders, the brothers Hengist and Horsa, and Geoffrey has Hengist's daughter Rowena win Vortigern over at a feast with a cup of wine. Later Hengist's men murder the British lords at a peace meeting, and in Geoffrey, Merlin raises Stonehenge over their graves. The Saxons who follow are the enemies Arthur fights in Geoffrey, Wace, Layamon and the French *Merlin* romances.<sup>[\*](#new-in-this-translation)</sup>

<a id="page-39"></a>[\[Mommsen 39\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-39) Their mother learned that the first troop had prospered. She sent a larger pack of followers and dogs, which came on ships and joined their bastard comrades. From there the seed of wickedness, the root of bitterness, sprouted in our soil with fierce shoots and tendrils, a poisonous planting worthy of what we deserved. The barbarians let into the island asked for supplies, as if they were soldiers about to face great dangers for their good hosts, as they falsely claimed. They got them for a long time, and that shut the dog's mouth, as the saying goes.

Then they complained that their monthly allowance wasn't generous enough, making up pretexts on purpose. They swore that unless more lavish gifts were heaped on them, they'd break the treaty and plunder the whole island. Before long they followed their threats with action.

### The Saxons break the treaty and attack Britain from sea to sea

A fire of just vengeance for our past crimes blazed from one sea to the other, piled up by the wicked hands from the east. Once lit, it ravaged every neighbouring city and field. It didn't die down until it had burned almost the whole face of the island and licked the western ocean with its red, savage tongue. This attack can be compared with the Assyrians' long ago on Judea.[^39-1] What the Psalms mourn came true in us too, as history tells: "They've burned your sanctuary to the ground with fire; they've desecrated the house of your name."[^39-2] Another psalm says: "Yahweh, the nations have come into your inheritance; they've polluted your holy temple," and the rest.[^39-3]

Swords flashed all around and flames crackled as the constant battering rams brought down every town. All who lived in them fell too, the Church's leaders, priests and people. It was a pitiful sight. In the middle of the streets lay the bottoms of towers torn from their high hinges, the stones of tall walls, holy altars and pieces of corpses. They were covered as if with clotted crusts of purple blood, all mixed together as in some horrible winepress.

There was no burial at all, except in the ruins of houses or in the bellies of beasts and birds. That's said with all respect for the holy souls, if many were found then for the angels to carry to the heights of heaven. That once good vine had turned so bitter that hardly a grape or an ear of grain could be seen, as Isaiah says, as if left behind the pickers in a vineyard or the harvesters in a field.[^39-4]

## Part 4: Ambrosius Aurelianus leads the Britons against the Saxons, up to the siege of Mount Badon

### Some Britons flee overseas, and Ambrosius Aurelianus leads the rest against the Saxons

<a id="page-40"></a>[\[Mommsen 40\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-40) So some of the poor survivors were caught in the mountains and slaughtered in heaps.[^40-1] Others, worn out by hunger, came and surrendered to the enemy, to be slaves forever, if they weren't killed on the spot; that counted as the greatest kindness. Others went to lands overseas, wailing loudly under the swelling sails. In place of a rowers' chant they sang: "You've given us up like sheep to be eaten and scattered us among the nations."[^40-2] Others trusted their lives, always fearful, to high hills, steep and threatening, to the thickest forests and to cliffs by the sea. They stayed in their country, though trembling.

> **In the Arthur story:** The Britons who sailed to lands overseas settled in Armorica, which took their name and became Brittany. The legend keeps it close: in Geoffrey of Monmouth, Arthur's nephew Hoel rules Brittany and fights at his side, and Arthur kills the giant of Mont-Saint-Michel there. Breton storytellers carried Arthur's tales into France, as Marie de France's Breton lays show, and Tristan marries his second Iseult in Brittany.<sup>[\*](#new-in-this-translation)</sup>

Some time later, when the cruel robbers had gone home, Yahweh gave strength to the survivors. The most desperate citizens flocked to them from many places on every side, as eagerly as bees to the hive when a storm is coming. They begged it with all their hearts not to let them be wiped out entirely, "loading the heavens with countless prayers," as the poet says.[^40-3]

Their leader was Ambrosius Aurelianus, a modest man. He alone of the Roman people had perhaps survived the shock of so great a storm. His parents, who had surely worn the purple, were killed in it, and in our time his descendants have fallen far short of their ancestor's goodness. Under him the survivors gathered their strength and challenged the victors to battle. By Yahweh's will, the victory went to them.

> **In the Arthur story:** Ambrosius Aurelianus becomes Arthur's uncle. Geoffrey of Monmouth calls him Aurelius Ambrosius, son of King Constantine and older brother of Uther Pendragon. He comes home from Brittany, burns Vortigern in his tower, beats Hengist, and has Merlin raise Stonehenge before a Saxon poisons him. The *History of the Britons* had already given his name to the fatherless boy who shows Vortigern the dragons, and Geoffrey splits the two, calling the prophet Merlin Ambrosius. In Robert de Boron's *Merlin*, the older brother's part goes to Pendragon.<sup>[\*](#new-in-this-translation)</sup>

### Britons and Saxons fight by turns until the siege of Mount Badon

From then on, now the citizens won, now the enemy. So Yahweh, as it does, tested this people, the Israel of today, to see whether it loved Yahweh or not.[^40-4] This lasted until the year of the siege of Mount Badon, almost the last slaughter of the villains, and not the least.[^40-5] That was forty-four years and a month ago, as I know, since I was born in it.[^40-6]

> **In the Arthur story:** Gildas names no leader at Mount Badon, but the legend gives the victory to Arthur. The *History of the Britons* makes it the twelfth of his battles, where he alone kills 960 men in one charge, and the *Welsh Annals* say he carried the cross of Jesus Christ there for three days and three nights. Geoffrey of Monmouth puts the battle at Bath, where Arthur fights with his sword Caliburn and his shield Pridwen, and Wace and Layamon follow him. In the Welsh *Dream of Rhonabwy*, Arthur's host is gathering for this battle.<sup>[\*](#new-in-this-translation)</sup>

But even now the country's cities aren't lived in as they were before. They still lie deserted, wrecked and neglected, because foreign wars have stopped, but civil ones haven't. The memory of the island's hopeless fall, and of the help no one hoped for, stuck in the minds of those who witnessed both wonders.

<a id="page-41"></a>[\[Mommsen 41\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-41) Because of it, kings, officials and private people, priests and churchmen each kept to their own rank. But they died, and an age followed that hadn't known that storm and knew only the calm of the present. Then every rein of truth and justice was so shaken and overturned that not a trace of them shows in those ranks, not even a memory, except in a few, a very few. So great a crowd falls headlong to hell every day that these count for almost nothing. Our venerable mother the Church hardly sees them resting in her lap, though they're her only true sons.

Don't think I'm attacking the life of these men. Everyone admires them, and Yahweh loves them. Their holy prayers hold up our weakness like pillars and wholesome props, so it doesn't collapse entirely. The heap of wrongs drives me to speak more freely, or rather more mournfully, about those who serve not only their belly but the devil, instead of Christ, the god blessed forever. When I do, I'm not arguing. I'm crying. Why should our citizens hide what the peoples all around not only know but already throw in their faces?

## Part 5: Gildas condemns five kings: Constantine, Aurelius Caninus, Vortiporius, Cuneglasus and Maelgwn

### Gildas says Britain's kings are tyrants who rob, lie and wage civil war

Britain has kings, but they're tyrants.[^41-1] It has judges, but wicked ones. They plunder and terrorise often, but the innocent. They avenge and protect, but only the guilty and the thieves. They have plenty of wives, but whores and adulterous ones. They swear constantly, but falsely. They make vows, but lie almost at once. They wage war, but civil, unjust ones.

They hunt robbers down hard throughout the country, yet they love and reward the ones who sit at their own table. They give generously to the poor, but on the other side they heap up a huge mountain of crimes. They take the judge's seat, but rarely look for the rule of right judgement.

They despise the harmless and humble. Yet if chance brings Yahweh's enemies along, they praise them to the skies as far as they can, as people say: the bloodthirsty, the proud, murderers, their own comrades, adulterers. Those should have been eagerly wiped out, name and all. They keep many prisoners loaded with chains in their jails, crushed more by the kings' own scheming than by any guilt. They linger at the altars to swear, and soon after treat them with contempt, as if they were filthy stones.

### Gildas calls out Constantine of Dumnonia for murdering two boys at the altar

Constantine, the tyrant cub of the unclean lioness of Dumnonia, knows this monstrous wickedness well.[^41-2]

<a id="page-42"></a>[\[Mommsen 42\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-42) This year he bound himself by a dreadful oath never to trick his people. He called first on Yahweh, then relied on the choirs of saints, with Mary, to stand with him. Yet he butchered two royal boys, and as many guardians, in the laps of two revered mothers, the Church and their own. He did it under a holy abbot's cloak, among the sacred altars themselves, as I've said.[^42-1] His wicked sword and spear were his teeth, and he tore their tender sides, their very hearts.

> **In the Arthur story:** Geoffrey of Monmouth builds the end of Arthur's reign from this charge. His Constantine is the son of Cador of Cornwall, part of Gildas's Dumnonia, and the wounded Arthur hands him the crown after Camlann. Mordred's two sons rise against him, and Constantine kills them both at the altar, one in a church at Winchester and one in a monastery in London, as Gildas says he killed the two royal boys. Malory ends *Le Morte d'Arthur* the same way, with Sir Constantine, Cador's son, chosen king.<sup>[\*](#new-in-this-translation)</sup>

Their blood, like robes of clotted purple, reached the seat of the heavenly sacrifice. Hardly anyone of that time handled weapons more bravely than those men. Yet they stretched out their arms not to the sword but to Yahweh and the altar. On the day of judgement those hands will hang up the revered banners of their patience and faith at the gates of your city, Christ.

Nor had he lived a life worth praising before this. Many years earlier, overcome by the constant, changing stink of his adulteries, he'd driven out his lawful wife. Christ and Paul forbid it: "What Yahweh has joined, no one should separate";[^42-2] "Husbands, love your wives."[^42-3]

In the clod of his heart, barren for good seed, he'd planted a bitter shoot of unbelief and foolishness from the vine of Sodom.[^42-4] His sins, public and private, watered it like poisonous rains. It rose eagerly to offend Yahweh, until it brought murder and sacrilege into the open. Still caught in the nets of his old crimes, he adds new ones.

### Gildas calls on Constantine to repent and come back to Christ

Come, I'll scold him as if he were here, since I know he's still alive. Why are you so dazed, butcher of your own soul? Why light for yourself, of your own will, the fires of hell that'll never go out? Why stab yourself with your own sword and spear, as an enemy would? Haven't even the poisoned cups of your crimes filled your heart?

Look back, please, and come to Christ. You're struggling, bent under an enormous load, and he himself said he'll give you rest.[^42-5] Come to the one who doesn't want a sinner's death, but wants them to turn and live.[^42-6] Undo the chains on your neck, son of Zion, as Isaiah says.[^42-7] Return, even from the far hiding places of your sins, to the most loving father. The young man who turned in disgust from the pigs' filthy food came home, dreading death by hunger. That father was happy to kill the fattened calf for the wanderer, and brought out the best robe and a royal ring.[^42-8]

<a id="page-43"></a>[\[Mommsen 43\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-43) Then you'll get a foretaste of the hope of heaven, and feel how good Yahweh is.[^43-1] If you sneer at this, be sure you'll soon be whirled around and burned in hell's dark torrents of fire, which no one escapes.

### Gildas calls out Aurelius Caninus for murder, adultery and love of civil war

What are you doing too, lion cub,[^43-2] to borrow Jacob's words, Aurelius Caninus?[^43-3] Aren't you savagely swallowed, as if by rushing sea waves, in the same filth as the king I've just named? Murder, promiscuity and adultery, if not something deadlier? Don't you hate peace in your country like a deadly snake? You thirst for civil wars and constant unjust raids. So you shut your soul out of heaven's gates of calm and rest.

> **In the Arthur story:** Geoffrey of Monmouth makes Aurelius Caninus the king after Constantine, as Aurelius Conanus, Constantine's nephew. He wins the throne by jailing an uncle with a better claim and killing that uncle's two sons, and he dies in the third year of his reign. Gildas's charge of murder and civil war is the whole of his story in the legend.<sup>[\*](#new-in-this-translation)</sup>

Left alone now, like a tree drying up in the middle of a field, remember, please, your fathers' and brothers' empty pride, and their young, untimely deaths. Almost all your family is gone. Will you be spared to live to a hundred, or as long as Methuselah, for your holy merits?[^43-4] Not at all. Unless you turn to Yahweh very soon, as the psalm says, that king will shortly swing its sword at you.[^43-5] It said through Moses: "I kill, I make alive; I strike, I heal; no one can rescue anyone from my hand."[^43-6]

So shake yourself out of your stinking dust, and come back with your whole heart to the one who made you.[^43-7] Then, when his anger soon flares up, you'll be happy for hoping in him.[^43-8] Otherwise eternal punishment will be waiting to grind you forever in hell's cruel jaws, never to swallow you up.

### Gildas calls out Vortiporius, tyrant of Dyfed, and urges him to repent

Why do you stand there stiff and stupid too, Vortiporius, tyrant of Dyfed?[^43-9] You're like a leopard in your habits, spotted with wickedness, and your head is already greying.[^43-10] You sit on a throne full of tricks, fouled from top to bottom by murders and adulteries of every kind. You're the bad son of a good king, as Manasseh was of Hezekiah.[^43-11] The end of your life is slowly coming near. Why aren't you satisfied by the violent whirlpools of sin you gulp like the best wine, or rather that swallow you? Why, as if to crown all your crimes, do you weigh down your miserable soul with incest with your shameless daughter? You'd put away your own wife, and she'd died an honourable death.

> **In the Arthur story:** Vortiporius is the next king in Geoffrey of Monmouth's line after Arthur. There he beats the Saxons when they rise with help from Germany, and he rules Britain for four years. The real Vortiporius left his name on a stone found in Dyfed, which calls him "Protector" in Latin and gives his name again in the Irish ogham script.<sup>[\*](#new-in-this-translation)</sup>

Please don't spend what's left of your days offending Yahweh. This is the welcome time, and the day of salvation shines on the faces of those who repent.[^43-12] In it you can do good, so that your flight won't come in winter or on the Sabbath.[^43-13] "Turn from evil and do good," the psalm says. "Look for peace and follow it. Yahweh's eyes will be on you when you do right, its ears open to your prayers, and it won't wipe your memory off the land of the living. You'll cry out, and it'll answer, rescuing you from all your troubles."[^43-14]

<a id="page-44"></a>[\[Mommsen 44\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-44) Christ never turns away a heart broken and humbled by fear of him.[^44-1] Otherwise the worm that torments you won't die, and the fire that burns you won't go out.[^44-2]

### Gildas calls out Cuneglasus for warring on Yahweh and on his own people

Why have you been rolling in the old filth of your wickedness ever since your youth, you too, Cuneglasus? You Bear, rider over many, driver of the chariot of the Bear's fort, you defy Yahweh and crush its people. Your name, in the Roman tongue, means "tawny butcher."[^44-3] Why do you wage such a war against both people and Yahweh? Against people, your own citizens, with your own weapons; against Yahweh, with endless crimes.

> **In the Arthur story:** Gildas calls Cuneglasus "Bear," and the Bear's fort is his own fort, probably Dinarth in north Wales, whose name means "bear's fort." Since Arthur's name may come from the Welsh word for bear, some modern writers have guessed that Gildas is hinting at Arthur here. No old telling makes that link, and Geoffrey of Monmouth, who uses Gildas's other kings, leaves Cuneglasus out.<sup>[\*](#new-in-this-translation)</sup>

Besides countless other lapses, why have you driven out your own wife? Why do you look up to her wicked sister, who had promised Yahweh to stay a chaste widow forever? You gaze at her, as the poet says, as the very darling of the gods, with your mind's whole worship, or rather a nymph's dullness.[^44-4] You do it against Paul's ban: he says adulterers have no place in the kingdom of heaven.[^44-5] Why do your constant wrongs stir up the groans and sighs of the saints living beside you in the body? Someday these will break your bones, as a savage lioness's teeth would.

Please stop being angry, as the Psalm says.[^44-6] Give up the deadly fury you breathe out at heaven and earth, meaning at Yahweh and its flock; it'll only waste you away. Change your ways instead, so that the saints will pray for you. They have power beyond this world. When they bind the guilty here, those are bound there too, and when they free those who repent, they're freed.[^44-7] As Paul says, don't be proud, and don't hope in uncertain riches, but in Yahweh, who gives you plenty of everything.[^44-8] Then by mending your ways you'll store up a good foundation for the future, and have true life, lasting forever.

Otherwise you'll see for yourself, even in this life, the evil and bitterness of leaving Yahweh your god without fear of it.[^44-9] In the next you'll burn in the foul ball of eternal fire, yet never die. The souls of the wicked live on in it forever, as the saints' do in joy.

### Gildas calls out Maelgwn, the strongest of the kings and the most wicked

Why do you roll so stupidly in the black ink of such old crimes, dragon of the island, Maelgwn,[^44-10] as if soaked in wine pressed from the vine of Sodom?[^44-11][^44-12] You drove many of the tyrants I've named from their kingdoms, and from their lives too. You come last in my writing but first in evil. You're greater than most in power and in malice too, more generous in giving, more lavish in sin, strong in arms but stronger in ruining your soul.

> **In the Arthur story:** Maelgwn, king of Gwynedd, becomes Geoffrey of Monmouth's Malgo, the handsome, warlike king who rules Britain after Vortiporius and wins the islands around it. The *Welsh Annals* give his death in the great plague of 547 CE. In the Welsh tale of Taliesin, the boy poet beats Maelgwn's court poets in a contest and frees his master Elffin from Maelgwn's prison. Gildas's "dragon" is the old Welsh word for a war leader that gives Uther his name Pendragon, "chief dragon."<sup>[\*](#new-in-this-translation)</sup>

<a id="page-45"></a>[\[Mommsen 45\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-45) Why do you tie such huge loads of guilt, like high mountains, onto your royal neck of your own will? The King of all kings made you higher than almost all Britain's leaders, in kingdom as in build. Why aren't you better than the others in your ways too, instead of worse?

Listen calmly a while to the sure proof of these charges. I'll leave out the private, lighter ones, if any are light, and name only those spread far and wide. In the first years of your youth, didn't you crush your uncle the king savagely with sword, spear and fire? Nearly all his bravest soldiers fell with him, whose faces in battle looked little different from lion cubs'.

You gave little thought to David's words in the Psalms: "Bloodthirsty, lying men won't live out half their days."[^45-1] What reward could you hope for from the just judge for this alone, even if nothing had come after it? He also says through Isaiah: "Doom to you who plunder; won't you be plundered too? You who kill, won't you be killed too? When you've finished plundering, then you'll fall."[^45-2]

### Maelgwn gives up his kingdom and vows to live as a monk

Your dream of a violent kingdom came true as you wished. Then you felt the pull to return to the right path. Perhaps your conscience gnawed at you over your sins. Day and night you chewed over the holy life and its rules, first to yourself, then out loud for the people to hear. Then you vowed yourself a monk forever, in front of almighty Yahweh, the angels and everyone watching, with no thought of breaking faith, as you said. It seemed you'd broken those strong nets that usually catch fat bulls like you headlong: kingdom, gold, silver and, what's more, the tug of your own will.

Didn't you snatch yourself away like a dove from a raven? You flew with a whistling swoop through the empty air, dodging the quick hawk's cruel, curved claws, to the saints' caves, the refuges you trusted so much. How glad Mother Church would have been if the enemy of all mortals hadn't dragged you, so sadly, from her lap! How the spark of the hope of heaven would have blazed up in the hearts of the hopeless if you'd stayed among the good!

<a id="page-46"></a>[\[Mommsen 46\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-46) What rewards of Christ's kingdom would have waited for your soul on the day of judgement, if that sly wolf hadn't snatched you from Yahweh's fold! You'd been changed into a lamb, and you went not much against your will, for him to make you like himself again. What joy your salvation would have given Yahweh, the loving father of all the saints! But the ill-fated parent of all the damned carried you off, like an eagle with great wings and talons, against all right, to the miserable band of his sons.

In short, your turning to good gave heaven and earth as much joy then as your wicked relapse gives them grief now, a sick hound's return to its vomit.[^46-1][^46-2] Since then you've offered your limbs to sin and the devil as weapons of wickedness. In your right mind you'd have given them eagerly to Yahweh as tools of justice.[^46-3]

Your pricked-up ears catch no praises of Yahweh sung sweetly by the tuneful voices of Christ's young recruits, no chant of the Church's music.[^46-4] They hear your own, which are nothing, from the mouths of your heralds. These crash like drunken revelers, a crew of rogues stuffed with lies, spraying foaming spit on everyone near.[^46-5] So a vessel once made ready for Yahweh's service is turned into the devil's instrument. What was thought worthy of heaven's honour is rightly thrown into the pit of hell.

### Maelgwn leaves the monastery and takes his nephew's widow as his wife

Yet your mind, dulled by the heap of your foolishness, isn't slowed by such stumbling blocks, as it might be by a barrier. It's hot as a colt, thinking every pleasure it hasn't tried is sweet, and it's swept on with wild fury over the wide fields of crime, adding new sins to the old. You turn your back on your first marriage. It was made after you broke your monk's vow, and it was unlawful, though she was your own wife. You seek another woman, not just any widow but a living man's beloved, and he was no stranger but your brother's son.

So that stiff neck, already weighed down with so many loads of sin, bends lower still from the depths under a double murder, the peak of your sacrilege. You killed that nephew, and the wife you'd had for some time.

<a id="page-47"></a>[\[Mommsen 47\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-47) Then you married the woman whose plotting and urging had piled up such sudden mountains of evil. Your flatterers' lying tongues cry out that it's public and lawful, since she was a widow, but they say it with the tips of their lips, not from deep inside. In our eyes it's the wickedest of matches.

What holy person, stirred by such a story, wouldn't burst out sobbing at once? What priest whose heart lies open, upright, to Yahweh wouldn't hear this and wail aloud Jeremiah's words: "Who'll give my head water and my eyes a fountain of tears? I'll cry day and night for my people who've been killed"?[^47-1]

You've hardly taken in what the Book of Sirach says against you: "Doom to you, wicked men, who've abandoned the law of the most high god! Your birth will be into a curse, and at death that'll be your share. Everything from the earth goes back to it; so sinners go from damnation to ruin."[^47-2] That is, if they don't turn back to Yahweh, at least after hearing this warning: "My son, you've sinned. Don't add any more, but pray about the old ones too."[^47-3] It urges them too: "Don't wait to come back to Yahweh, or put it off from day to day, because its anger comes suddenly."[^47-4]

As scripture says, "When a king listens to a lie, all his subjects are wicked."[^47-5] Surely, as Solomon said, "a just ruler lifts up his country."[^47-6]

### Maelgwn had the best teacher in Britain, and Gildas begs him to listen

You don't lack warnings, though. You had for your teacher the refined master of almost all Britain.[^47-7] Take care, then, that what Solomon notes doesn't happen to you: "Telling wisdom to a fool is like waking someone from a deep sleep. When you're done, they'll ask what you said first."[^47-8]

"Wash the malice from your heart, Jerusalem," as it was said, "so that you'll be saved."[^47-9]

Please don't refuse Yahweh's mercy, beyond all words. Through Jeremiah it calls the wicked from their wrongs: "Suddenly I'll speak against a nation and a kingdom, to uproot, scatter, destroy, wipe it out."[^47-10] It urges the guilty hard to repent: "If that people turns from its sin, I'll change my mind too about the evil I said I'd do to it."[^47-11] Through Moses, Yahweh asks: "Who'll give them such a heart that they listen to me and keep my commands, so that all goes well with them every day of their lives?"[^47-12] The song of Deuteronomy says the same: "A people without sense or wisdom! If only they were wise enough to understand and see their end coming: how one could chase a thousand, two put ten thousand to flight."[^47-13]

Jesus says again in the gospel: "Come to me, all of you who are weary, carrying heavy loads, and I'll give you rest. Take my yoke on you, learn from me, since I'm gentle and humble in heart; your souls will find peace."[^47-14]

<a id="page-48"></a>[\[Mommsen 48\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-48) Suppose you hear this with deaf ears, sneer at the prophets, despise Christ and count me as nothing, though I'm of the lowest worth. I still hold, however I can, with true devotion, to this prophetic saying: "If I don't fill myself with strength in the spirit and power of Yahweh, to tell Jacob's house its sins and Israel's its crimes."[^48-1] I won't be one of the "mute dogs that can't bark."[^48-2]

I keep Solomon's warning too: "Whoever calls the wicked good will be cursed by peoples and hated by nations, but those who confront them will hope for better."[^48-3] He also says: "Don't spare your neighbour when they fall, or hold back a word in the time of salvation."[^48-4] He tells us: "Rescue those being led off to be killed, and don't hesitate to redeem those being slaughtered."[^48-5] As Solomon says elsewhere, "riches won't help on the day of anger: justice frees from death."[^48-6] He asks too: "If the just are hardly saved, where will the wicked and the sinner end up?"[^48-7]

If you won't hear, that dark torrent of hell will surely whirl you round in its savage eddies and fierce waves. It'll torment you forever and never destroy you. Then you'll see your punishment with your own eyes and regret what you've done, but too late, and it'll do you no good. In this welcome time, the day of salvation, you put off turning to the right path of life.

## Part 6: Gildas calls the prophets as witnesses against the kings

### Gildas meant to stop here, but calls the prophets to speak against the kings

This sad, mournful history of the wrongs of this age should surely have ended here, or even sooner, so that my mouth would speak no more of what people do.[^48-8] But I don't want them to think me afraid or worn out. I also mean to keep tirelessly clear of Isaiah's warning. So I'll set out briefly how loudly the prophets' threats cry out against these five kings.

Isaiah warns: "Doom to those who call good evil, and the reverse, swapping darkness with light, bitter with sweet."[^48-9] He speaks of those "who look and don't see, who listen without hearing." Their "heart is covered by a thick cloud of vices."[^48-10] The kings I've named are wild, mad men, like Pharaoh's attendants, who boldly urged his army on into the Red Sea to die.[^48-11] They and those like them are the five horses.[^48-12] These sayings will cover the work of my little book most faithfully, like a fine roof, so that the envious can't get through it, however hard they rain down on it.

So let the holy prophets answer for me now, as before. They were, in a way, Yahweh's mouth and the Holy Spirit's instrument, forbidding mortals evil and favoring the good. Let them reply to the stubborn, proud princes of this age, so they won't say I threaten them with such terrors out of my own invention and mere talkative recklessness.

<a id="page-49"></a>[\[Mommsen 49\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-49) No wise person doubts that the sins of this time are heavier than those of the first. Paul says: "Anyone who breaks the law dies on the word of two or three witnesses. How much worse a punishment do you think they deserve who've trampled on the son of Yahweh?"[^49-1]

### Gildas cites Samuel scolding Saul, who lost his kingdom for disobeying Yahweh

First we meet Samuel. At Yahweh's command he set up the lawful kingdom. He was promised to Yahweh before he was born.[^49-2] All Israel trusted him as a true prophet, from Dan to Beersheba, and he was known for wonders no one could doubt.[^49-3] Through his mouth the Holy Spirit thundered at every power in the world.

He warned Saul, the very first king of the Hebrews, because Saul hadn't carried out some of Yahweh's orders. "You've been a fool," Samuel told him. "You haven't kept the commands Yahweh your god gave you. If you'd obeyed, Yahweh would now make your rule over Israel last forever. As it is, your reign won't stand."[^49-4]

What did Saul do that compares with the crimes of our time? Adultery? Murdering his own family? Not at all. He changed part of an order. As one of our own writers rightly says, what matters isn't how bad the sin is. It's that a command was broken.[^49-5]

Saul tried to clear himself of the charges, as he thought. Like anyone caught out, he cleverly added excuses: "But I did listen to Yahweh. I went the way it sent me."[^49-6]

Samuel punished him with harsh words. "Does Yahweh want burnt offerings and slaughtered animals?" he asked. "Wouldn't it rather be obeyed? Obeying is better than sacrifice, and listening is better than the fat of rams. Defiance is as bad as fortune-telling. Refusing to give in is like worshipping idols. You've rejected Yahweh's word, so it's thrown you away too. You won't be king."[^49-7]

A little later Samuel says: "Today Yahweh has torn the kingdom of Israel from you. It's given it to a neighbour of yours, a better man than you. The one who triumphs in Israel won't spare you or relent. It isn't human, to be sorry for what it's done."[^49-8] He means that no hard, wicked heart will move it.

So note this: Samuel called refusing to give in to Yahweh a kind of idolatry. Our criminals shouldn't pat themselves on the back because they don't openly sacrifice to pagan gods. They trample Christ's most precious pearls like pigs.[^49-9] That puts them among those who worship idols.

### Gildas cites how Yahweh punished David and his son Solomon

This one example would be enough to correct the wicked. It's like a witness no one can answer. Still, every evil in Britain should be proved by many witnesses, so let's go on to the others.

What happened to David for counting his people? Gad the prophet brought him Yahweh's message: "You have three choices. Pick one, and I'll do it to you.

<a id="page-50"></a>[\[Mommsen 50\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-50) "Seven years of famine, or three months running from your enemies while they hunt you down, or three days of plague in your land."[^50-1]

David was trapped. He chose to fall into the hands of a merciful Yahweh rather than of men, and seventy thousand of his people died. It humbled him. He loved them as an apostle loves, and wished to die himself so the plague would spare them. "I'm the one who sinned," he said. "I'm the shepherd who did wrong. What have these sheep done? Turn your hand against me, please, and against my father's family." So he offered his own death to pay for the thoughtless pride in his heart.[^50-2]

What does the Bible tell next of his son? "Solomon did what Yahweh disliked. He didn't follow Yahweh fully, as his father had." Yahweh told him: "You've done this. You haven't kept my covenant or the orders I gave you. So I'll tear your kingdom apart and give it to your servant."[^50-3]

### Gildas cites the prophets' sentences on Jeroboam, Baasha and Ahab

Listen to what happened to Jeroboam and Baasha. They were two kings of Israel as sacrilegious as ours.[^50-4] Yahweh's sentence came to them through the prophet Jehu son of Hanani: "I made you ruler over Israel, but my people have angered me with their worthless gods. So I'm raising up a successor to Baasha and his line. I'll do to Baasha's family what I did to Jeroboam son of Nebat's. Dogs will eat Baasha's people who die in the city. The birds will have the bodies in the fields."[^50-5]

Ahab was another criminal king of Israel, and as bad as ours. His schemes and Jezebel's tricks crushed the innocent Naboth over his father's vineyard. Elijah was taught by Yahweh's fiery word, and through his holy mouth Yahweh threatened Ahab. "You've murdered," Elijah says, "and taken his land as well." He went on: "This is what Yahweh says. Where the dogs licked up Naboth's blood, they'll lick up yours." It's known for certain that this happened just as he said.[^50-6]

Don't let "a lying spirit, speaking empty words through your prophets," mislead you, as it did Ahab. Don't let it keep you from hearing Micaiah: "Yahweh has put lies in the mouths of all who prophesy for you here. Yahweh has promised you disaster."[^50-7] Even now there are surely teachers inspired from the wrong side. They stand up for crooked pleasure rather than the truth. "Their words are smoother than oil, yet they're spears."[^50-8] They say, "All's well, all's well," but for those who go on sinning, it won't be.[^50-9] As Isaiah says elsewhere, "There's no joy for the wicked, says Yahweh."[^50-10]

### Gildas cites Azariah's warning to King Asa coming back from battle

<a id="page-51"></a>[\[Mommsen 51\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-51) Azariah son of Oded spoke to Asa too, as Asa came back from slaughtering an army of a million Ethiopians: "Yahweh is with you while you're with it. Look for it, and you'll find it. Turn your back on it, and it'll abandon you."[^51-1]

Jehoshaphat helped Ahab, a wicked king. The prophet Jehu son of Hanani scolded him for it: "You help a sinner and befriend Yahweh's enemy. So Yahweh's anger is on you."[^51-2] Then what will become of men tied up in the cords of their own crimes? If we want to fight in Yahweh's battle line, we must loathe their sins, but not their souls. As the psalm says, "You who love Yahweh, hate evil."[^51-3]

Jehoshaphat's son Jehoram was a murderer. He butchered his own fine brothers so that he, a bastard, could rule in their place. What did the prophet Elijah, Israel's chariot and its driver, say to him?[^51-4] "This is what Yahweh says, the god of your ancestor David. You haven't walked in the ways of your father Jehoshaphat, or of Asa of Judah. You've taken after the kings of Israel instead, unfaithful to Yahweh as Ahab's family was. You've killed your brothers, Jehoshaphat's sons, who were better than you. So Yahweh will strike you and your children with a terrible blow." Elijah went on: "You'll fall gravely ill with a disease of the bowels. Day after day it'll get worse, until your insides come out."[^51-5]

Joash, king of Israel, turned away from Yahweh as you do. Listen to how the prophet Zechariah son of Jehoiada threatened him. Zechariah stood up and told the people: "This is what Yahweh says. Why do you break Yahweh's commands? It's why nothing goes well for you. You've abandoned Yahweh, so it'll abandon you."[^51-6]

### Gildas turns to Isaiah, who calls the rulers of Israel princes of Sodom

What should I say of Isaiah, the first of the prophets? He opens his prophecy, his vision, this way: "Listen, heavens! Hear me, earth! Yahweh has spoken. I fed my children and raised them, but they've turned against me. The ox knows its owner, and the donkey knows where its master feeds it. Israel doesn't know me. My people don't understand."[^51-7]

A little later he fits the threats to the stupidity that deserves them: "Zion's daughter will be left like a shelter in a vineyard, like a hut in a cucumber field, like a city laid waste."[^51-8] Then he turns to the rulers in particular: "Hear Yahweh's word, you princes of Sodom. Listen to Yahweh's law, you people of Gomorrah."[^51-9]

Note well: wicked kings get that name. Yahweh forbids such men to offer it sacrifices and gifts. Yet we greedily accept what Yahweh won't take from any nation. To our own ruin, we won't let it be given to the needy, who have almost nothing. Yahweh speaks to those loaded with wealth and bent on the filth of sin: "Stop bringing me useless sacrifices. Your incense disgusts me." It warns them too: "When you reach out to me, I'll look away. Pray all you like, I won't listen." It says why: "Your hands are full of blood."[^51-10]

<a id="page-52"></a>[\[Mommsen 52\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-52) He shows how he might be won back: "Wash, and be clean. Take your wicked plans out of my sight. Stop doing wrong, and learn to do good. Look for justice, help the oppressed, stand up for the orphan." Then he adds what he'll give in return: "Your sins may be scarlet, but they'll be white as snow. Red as crimson, they'll be like wool. If you're willing and you listen to me, you'll eat the good things of the land. If you refuse and make me angry, the sword will devour you."[^52-1]

### Isaiah condemns corrupt judges and men who drink from morning to night

Take Isaiah as a true witness who speaks in public. He tells you how your good and your evil will be paid back, with no coat of flattery. He doesn't whisper honeyed poison into your ears the way your hangers-on do. He aims his sentence at greedy judges: "Your princes can't be trusted. They're partners of thieves. They all love bribes and chase payoffs. They won't defend the orphan, and the widow's case never reaches them. So Yahweh of armies, the mighty one of Israel, says: I'll get satisfaction from those who oppose me and revenge on my enemies." Isaiah ends the warning: "Criminals and sinners will be crushed together. All who've abandoned Yahweh will be destroyed."[^52-2]

Isaiah warns the arrogant: "The proud man's eyes will be brought low, and tall men will bow."[^52-3] He goes on: "Doom to the wicked: they'll be paid for what they've done."[^52-4] Then he turns on those who drink: "Disaster for you who get up early to start drinking and keep at it till evening, until you're burning with it. Harp, lyre, drum, pipe and wine are at your feasts. But you don't look at what Yahweh does, or think about what its hands have made. That's why my people have been carried off as captives: they didn't know. Their nobles have starved, and their crowds are dried up with thirst."[^52-5]

"That's why hell has opened its throat wide, its mouth without limit. Their heroes and their common people, their proud and their famous, will all go down into it." Later he comes back to them: "Doom to you who are mighty at drinking wine and brave at mixing drinks. You acquit the guilty for a bribe and rob the innocent of their rights. Fire eats up straw, and flame burns wood. Their root will turn to ash like that, and their shoots will blow away like dust. They've thrown away the law of Yahweh of armies and sneered at the word of Israel's Holy One. For all this, Yahweh's fury hasn't turned away. Its hand is still raised to strike."[^52-6]

### Isaiah describes the day of judgement, when Yahweh will lay the earth waste

A while later Isaiah turns to the day of judgement and the sinners' unspeakable terror. "Wail," he says, "because Yahweh's day is near." If it was close then, what are we to think now? "Ruin will come from Yahweh. All hands will go limp. Every human heart will melt and break. Cramps and pains will grip them. They'll writhe like a woman giving birth. They'll stare at each other in shock, their faces burning. Yahweh's day is coming, cruel and full of anger and rage. It'll make the land a desert and crush its sinners out of it.

"The stars of heaven won't spread their light. The sun will be dark when it rises, and the moon won't shine in its season. I'll punish the world for its evil and the wicked for their crimes. I'll end the pride of the unfaithful and humble the arrogance of the strong."[^52-7]

<a id="page-53"></a>[\[Mommsen 53\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-53) Again: "Yahweh will lay the earth waste and strip it bare. It'll ruin its face and scatter its people. Priest will fare like people, master like slave, mistress like maid. Buyer and seller will be alike, lender like borrower, creditor like debtor. The land will be stripped and plundered to the last, because Yahweh has said so. The ground mourns and withers. The world fades, and its proud people grow weak. Its own inhabitants have killed it. They've broken the laws, changed what's right and torn up the covenant that was meant to last forever."[^53-1]

### Isaiah says a curse will eat up the earth and joy will leave it

"That's why a curse will eat up the earth." Isaiah says what that'll mean: "Everyone glad at heart will groan. The cheerful drums will stop, and the revelers' noise will fall still. The sweet harp and its song will go silent. They won't drink anymore, and anything they swallow will taste bitter. The city of emptiness is broken down. Every house is shut, and no one goes in. People will cry in the streets for wine. All happiness is gone, and the land's joy is carried off. The town is left empty, and disaster will batter its gates. This is how it'll be at the centre of the world, among its peoples."[^53-2]

Then Isaiah says there's no escape: "The traitors have betrayed, and the criminals with them. Terror, a pit and a net are waiting for you who live on the earth. Run from the cry of fear, and you'll fall in the hole. Climb out, and the trap will catch you. The floodgates of heaven will open, and the world's foundations will shake. The ground will be smashed to pieces, rocked and tossed about like a drunk. It'll be carried off like a tent pitched for one night. Its wickedness will weigh on it. It'll fall and never get up.

"On that day Yahweh will punish the army of heaven above and the kings of the earth below. They'll be gathered into a pit like one bundle and shut in prison there. After many days they'll be dealt with. The moon will blush, and the sun will be shamed. Yahweh of armies will reign on Mount Zion and in Jerusalem, glorified in front of its elders."[^53-3]

### Isaiah says the people's sins have hidden Yahweh's face from them

A while later Isaiah gives the reason for such threats: "Yahweh's arm isn't too short to save, and its ear isn't too dull to hear. Your wrongs have come between you and your god. Your sins have hidden its face from you, so it won't listen. Your hands are stained with blood and your fingers with crime. Your lips have told lies, and your tongue speaks evil. No one calls for justice, and no one judges honestly. They trust in nothing and talk nonsense. They plan trouble and bring about wickedness."[^53-4]

Near the end of his book Isaiah says of the wicked: "What they do is worthless, and their hands are busy with wrong. Their feet run to evil and hurry to shed innocent blood. Their thoughts are futile, and ruin lies along their roads. They don't know the way of peace, and nothing they do is fair. Their paths are crooked. No one who walks them knows what calm is. That's why judgement keeps away from you, and what's right doesn't reach you."[^53-5]

<a id="page-54"></a>[\[Mommsen 54\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-54) A little later: "Justice is turned back and stands far off. Honesty has fallen in the street, and fairness can't get in. Truth is forgotten, and anyone who turns from evil is robbed. Yahweh saw it. It was displeased that no one judged rightly."[^54-1]

### Gildas turns to Jeremiah, called by Yahweh before he was born

That's enough of Isaiah, a few words of his many.

Now listen for a while to Jeremiah. Yahweh knew him before he was formed in the womb, made him holy before he was born and set him over all nations as a prophet.[^54-2] Here's what he said about a foolish people and hard-hearted kings. He opens gently: "Yahweh spoke to me. Go, it said. Shout it in Jerusalem's ears." Then Yahweh gives him the message: "Hear Yahweh's word, house of Jacob, every family of Israel. This is what Yahweh says. What fault did your ancestors find in me? They went far from me. They chased what's worthless and grew hollow themselves. They never asked, Where's the one who brought us up out of Egypt?"[^54-3]

Yahweh reminds them: "Long ago you broke my yoke and snapped your ropes. You said, I won't serve."[^54-4] It asks what went wrong: "I planted you as a choice vine, all from true seed. How have you turned into something crooked and strange to me? Scrub yourself with lye, use all the soap you like, and your guilt still stains you in front of me, says Yahweh."[^54-5]

Yahweh asks them next: "Why do you want to argue your case against me? You've all abandoned me, says Yahweh. I struck your children for nothing. They wouldn't learn. Hear Yahweh's word. Have I been a desert to Israel, or a land where nothing ripens? Then why do my people say, We've left you, and we'll never come back? Does a girl forget her jewellery, or a bride the sash across her breast? Yet my people have forgotten me for days beyond counting."[^54-6] Later he says of them: "My people are foolish. They don't know me. They're stupid children with no sense. They're clever at doing evil, but they don't know how to do good."[^54-7]

### Jeremiah says the people won't learn and the wicked set traps for others

Then Jeremiah speaks for himself: "Yahweh, your eyes look for faithfulness. You struck them, but they felt no pain. You crushed them, but they wouldn't be taught. They set their faces harder than rock and refused to come back."[^54-8]

Yahweh says too: "Announce this to the house of Jacob. Spread it through Judah. Listen, you foolish people with no sense. You have eyes, but you don't see. You have ears, but you don't hear. Won't you fear me, says Yahweh? Won't you tremble in front of me? I set the sand as the sea's border, a law it'll never cross. Its waves heave, but they get nowhere. They swell, but they can't pass it. This people's heart is disloyal and rebellious. They've turned and gone. They've never said to themselves, Let's fear Yahweh our god."[^54-9]

Jeremiah warns again: "Wicked men live among my people. They lie in wait like hunters, setting traps and nets to catch people. Their houses are packed with tricks, like a cage crammed with birds. That's how they've grown great and rich, fat and sleek. They've ignored my words in the worst way. They haven't pleaded the orphan's case or judged the poor fairly. Shouldn't I punish them for this, says Yahweh? Shouldn't I take revenge on them?"[^54-10]

### Gildas hopes Britain's kings will listen where Jeremiah's hearers didn't

<a id="page-55"></a>[\[Mommsen 55\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-55) But god forbid that what follows happens to you: "You'll say all this to them, and they won't listen. You'll call them, and they won't answer. Tell them: This is the nation that didn't obey Yahweh its god or take correction. Faithfulness is dead. It's gone from their mouths."[^55-1]

Later Jeremiah asks: "When people fall, don't they get up again? When they turn away, don't they come back? Then why has this people in Jerusalem turned its back on me so stubbornly? They've held on to lies and refused to come back. I listened closely. No one says what's right. No one is sorry for their sin, or asks, What have I done? They've all gone their own way, like a horse charging into battle. The kite in the sky knows its season. The turtledove, the swallow and the stork keep the time of their coming. My people don't know Yahweh's judgement."[^55-2]

Jeremiah was horrified at such violent, blind sacrilege and such drunkenness. He cried for people who didn't cry for themselves, as our unhappy tyrants don't now. He wished Yahweh would give him more grief: "My people's daughter is crushed, and so am I. I'm stunned. Is there no balm in Gilead, no doctor there? Then why hasn't her wound healed?[^55-3] I wish my head were water and my eyes a fountain of tears. I'd cry day and night for my people who've been killed.[^55-4]

"If only I had a travelers' shelter in the desert! I'd leave my people and go away from them. They're all adulterers, a gang of traitors. They've bent their tongues like bows to shoot lies, not truth. They've grown strong in the land by going from one evil to the next. They don't know me, says Yahweh."[^55-5]

Jeremiah gives Yahweh's sentence again: "Yahweh said: They've abandoned my law, which I gave them. They haven't listened to my voice or followed it. They've gone after their own wicked hearts instead. So this is what Yahweh of armies, the god of Israel, says: I'll feed this people wormwood and give them bitter water to drink."[^55-6] A little later he adds what Jeremiah writes again and again in Yahweh's own voice: "So don't pray for this people. Don't lift up praise and prayer for them. I won't listen when they cry out to me in their trouble."[^55-7]

### Yahweh forbids prayer for the stubborn, so Gildas asks what the kings will do

What will our doomed leaders do now? A few people find the narrow way and leave the broad one.[^55-8] Yahweh forbids those few to pray for you, while you stay in your sins and anger it so much. If you came back to Yahweh from the heart, the opposite would be true. Then they couldn't bring vengeance on you, however hard they tried. Yahweh would rather no soul perished. It holds back, so that someone cast off won't be lost for good. Even the prophet Jonah couldn't call down punishment on Nineveh, eager as he was.[^55-9]

<a id="page-56"></a>[\[Mommsen 56\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-56) But let's leave our own words for now and listen to Jeremiah's trumpet: "You may ask yourself, Why has this trouble come on me? It's because of your many crimes. If an Ethiopian can change his skin, or a leopard its spots, then you can do good too, though you've learned evil."[^56-1] He means you could if you wanted to. Jeremiah goes on: "This is what Yahweh says about this people. They love to wander, and can't keep their feet still. Yahweh isn't pleased with them. Now it'll remember their crimes and punish their sins. Yahweh told me: Don't pray for good things for this people. When they fast, I won't hear their prayers. When they offer burnt offerings and sacrifices, I won't accept them."[^56-2]

Even Jeremiah can't move Yahweh: "Yahweh told me: Even if Moses and Samuel stood in front of me, my heart wouldn't turn to this people. Throw them out of my sight. Let them go."[^56-3] Yahweh tells the city: "Who'll pity you, Jerusalem? Who'll mourn for you, or come to ask how you are? You've left me, says Yahweh. You've turned your back on me. I'll reach out my hand against you and kill you."[^56-4] Yahweh warns once more: "This is what Yahweh says. I'm making a plan against you. Each of you, turn back from your wicked ways. Set your paths and your efforts right. But they said, It's hopeless. We'll do as we think best. Each of us will do what our evil hearts want.[^56-5]

"So this is what Yahweh says. Ask the nations who has ever heard of the horrors the girl Israel has done. Does the snow of Lebanon ever leave its rocky fields? Do its cold streams ever stop flowing? Yet my people have forgotten me."[^56-6]

A while later he offers a choice: "This is what Yahweh says. Do what's just and right. Rescue people who are being robbed from those who rob them. Don't wrong the stranger, the orphan or the widow, or crush them unfairly. Don't shed innocent blood. If you truly do this, kings of David's line will ride in through the gates of this house to sit on his throne. If you won't listen, I swear by myself, says Yahweh, this place will become a ruin."[^56-7]

Again, speaking of the criminal Jeconiah: "As I live, says Yahweh, even if Jeconiah were the ring on my right hand, I'd tear him off. I'd give him to those who want him dead."[^56-8]

### Gildas cites Habakkuk on cities built with blood

Holy Habakkuk cries out too: "Doom to those who build a city with blood and found it on crime. They say, Isn't this from Yahweh almighty? Whole peoples have perished in the fire, and nations have dwindled."[^56-9] He opens his prophecy with a complaint: "How long will I cry out before you hear? I shout to you, but why do you make me watch hardship and pain, misery and godlessness? The case has been heard, and the judge has taken a bribe. So the law is torn apart, and no trial ever ends in justice. The wicked use their power to crush the just. So crooked verdicts come out."[^56-10]

### Gildas cites Hosea on princes who rule without Yahweh

Listen, too, to what the blessed prophet Hosea says of princes: "They've broken my covenant and gone against my law. They cried out, We know you're against Israel. They've hunted down the good as if it were wicked. They've made themselves kings, but not through me. They've held power without acknowledging me."[^56-11]

### Gildas cites Amos on punishment for the nations' repeated crimes

<a id="page-57"></a>[\[Mommsen 57\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-57) Listen to holy Amos too, as he threatens: "Judah's people have sinned three times, four times, and I won't take back their punishment. They've rejected Yahweh's law and haven't kept its commands. Their idols have misled them. I'll send fire on Judah, and it'll burn down Jerusalem's walls. This is what Yahweh says. Israel has sinned three times, four times, and I won't take back its punishment. They've sold the honest for money and the needy for a pair of shoes. They've beaten the heads of the poor with their fists and pushed the humble off the road."[^57-1]

Amos pleads with them: "Look for Yahweh, and you'll live. If you don't, it'll blaze up like fire against Joseph's house and burn it down, with no one to put it out."[^57-2] He says why they won't listen: "The Israelites hated anyone who criticised them in court, and they loathed an honest word."[^57-3] When Amos was told to stop prophesying in Israel, he gave no warm flattery in reply: "I wasn't a prophet, or a prophet's son. I was a goatherd who picked sycamore figs. Yahweh took me from the flock. It told me, Go and prophesy to my people Israel. Now hear Yahweh's word." Amos was speaking to the king.[^57-4]

"You say, Don't preach against Israel. Don't gather crowds against the house of Jacob. So this is what Yahweh says. Your wife will become a prostitute in the city. Your sons and daughters will be killed. Your fields will be measured out and shared. You'll die on unclean ground, and Israel will be carried off from its land as a captive."[^57-5]

Amos turns on the rich: "Listen to this, you who cruelly trample the poor and lord it over the country's needy. You say, When will the month be over, so we can make money? When will the Sabbath end, so we can open our storerooms?" Yahweh answers them: "Yahweh swears by Jacob's pride: I'll never forget what you've done in your contempt. Won't the earth shake for this? Won't all who live on it mourn? Ruin will rise like a river."[^57-6]

"I'll turn your feasts into funerals. Everyone will wear sackcloth and shave their heads. I'll make it like mourning an only son, and a bitter day for those with him."[^57-7] At the end of his book, Amos warns: "All the sinners among my people will die by the sword. They're the ones who say, No disaster will come near us."[^57-8]

### Gildas cites Micah on riches gained with false weights

Listen, too, to what the holy prophet Micah declared: "Hear me, tribe. What will make the city fine? Fire? The house of the wicked, hoarding crooked treasure, wrong piled on injustice? Will the cheat be found innocent with false scales and a bag of lying weights? That's how they've filled up their riches, against Yahweh."[^57-9]

### Gildas cites Zephaniah on the coming day of Yahweh's anger

Listen, too, to the threats the famous prophet Zephaniah heaps up: "The great day of Yahweh is near. It's close, and coming very fast. The voice of Yahweh is bitter and mighty. That day will be a day of anger, a day of trouble and distress. It'll be a day of cloud and fog, of trumpets and war cries. It'll be a day of misery and ruin. Darkness and gloom will hang over the walled cities and the high towers.

<a id="page-58"></a>[\[Mommsen 58\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-58) "I'll bring hardship on people, and they'll walk like the blind, because they've sinned against Yahweh. I'll pour out their blood like dust and their flesh like cow manure. Their silver and gold won't save them on the day of Yahweh's anger. The fire of its jealousy will eat up the whole earth, when Yahweh makes an end of everyone living on it and leaves it empty. Gather now, you unruly nation, ahead of the day you fade like a flower and Yahweh's anger comes on you."[^58-1]

### Gildas cites Haggai on Yahweh overturning the thrones of kings

Listen to what the holy prophet Haggai says: "This is what Yahweh says. Once more I'll shake heaven and earth, the sea and the dry land. I'll overturn kingdoms and destroy the power of the nations' kings. I'll throw down chariots and their riders."[^58-2]

### Gildas cites Zechariah's vision of a flying sickle that punishes thieves

Look now at what Zechariah son of Iddo said, chosen by Yahweh. He opens his prophecy this way: "Come back to me, and I'll come back to you, says Yahweh. Don't be like your ancestors. The earlier prophets cried out to them: This is what Yahweh almighty says, Turn from your ways. But they didn't pay attention or obey me."[^58-3] Then Zechariah tells his vision: "The angel asked me, What do you see? I answered, I see a flying sickle, nine metres long. It's the curse that goes out over the whole earth. Every thief will be punished by it with death. I'll send it out, says Yahweh almighty. It'll enter the house of the furious, and of whoever swears falsely in my name."[^58-4]

### Gildas cites Malachi on the day that's coming for the proud

The holy prophet Malachi says too: "The day of Yahweh is coming, burning like a furnace. All the proud and everyone who does wrong will be straw. That day will set them on fire, says Yahweh of armies. It'll leave them neither root nor branch."[^58-5]

### Gildas cites Job on the wicked who prosper for a time

Listen, too, to how holy Job argued over how the godless begin and how they end: "Why do the wicked live? They grow old with no shame. Their children turn out as they wished, their sons in front of their eyes. Their homes do well. They never feel fear or Yahweh's whip. Their cows don't miscarry. Their pregnant animals give birth and don't lose their young, but last like sheep forever. Their little ones play, picking up the lyre and the harp. They end their lives in plenty and die in peace. Doesn't Yahweh see what such men do?"[^58-6]

Not so: "The lamp of the wicked will go out, and ruin will fall on them. Pains like a woman's in labour will grip them in Yahweh's anger. They'll be like chaff in the wind, like dust a whirlwind carries off. Let his children lose their goods. Let his own eyes see his slaughter. Let Yahweh never save him again."[^58-7]

A while later, of the same men: "They've carried off flock and shepherd together. They've driven off the orphans' donkey and taken the widow's ox as a pledge. They've pushed the needy off the road. They've reaped fields before their time that weren't theirs. The poor have worked the vineyards of the powerful with no pay or food. They've made many sleep naked, with nothing to wear. They've taken away what kept them alive."[^58-8]

<a id="page-59"></a>[\[Mommsen 59\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-59) Job says of the wicked: "Yahweh knew their works, and it handed them over to darkness." Job wishes on such a man: "So let his share of the land be cursed. Let his crops dry up." Job goes on: "Let him be paid back in kind. Let every wicked man be broken like a rotten tree. In his anger he rose up and overthrew the weak. So when he falls ill, he won't count on living. Let him have no hope of getting well, but sink into weakness. His pride has hurt a lot of people. He's withered like a mallow in the heat, like an ear of grain that drops from its stalk."[^59-1]

Later Job says what will become of the wicked man's family: "If his sons are many, they'll be slaughtered." Of his money, Job says: "He may gather silver like dust and pile up gold like mud, but honest people will get it all."[^59-2]

### Gildas cites Ezra's book on the sword and fire sent against sinners

Listen as well to what blessed Ezra threatened, the prophet who was a library of the law. He argues this way: "This is what my lord says. My right hand won't spare the sinful. My sword won't stop striking those who shed innocent blood on the earth. Fire will go out from my anger and eat up the world's foundations, and burn the sinners like straw. Doom to those who sin and don't keep my commands, says Yahweh. I won't spare them. Get away, you renegade children. Don't pollute my holy place. Yahweh knows who offends it. So it'll hand them over to death and slaughter. Many evils have already come on the earth."[^59-3]

"The sword and the fire have been sent on you. Who can turn them back? Can anyone drive off a hungry lion in the forest? Can straw be saved once it's lit? Yahweh will send disasters. Who can stop them? Flame will burst out of its anger. Who can quench it? It'll flash like lightning. Who won't be afraid? It'll thunder. Who won't shudder? Yahweh will threaten. Who won't be terrified to face it? The earth will tremble, and the foundations of the sea will surge up from the depths."[^59-4]

### Gildas cites Ezekiel on Noah, Daniel and Job in a sinful land

Listen, too, to what the great prophet Ezekiel declared about criminals. He saw the four wonderful living creatures of the Gospels.[^59-5] When he cried over Israel's punishment, Yahweh first told him: "The house of Israel and Judah has grown very wicked. The land is filled with many peoples, and the city with crime and filth."[^59-6] Yahweh goes on: "Here I am."[^59-7] It says of Jerusalem: "My eye won't spare them, and I won't take pity."[^59-8] Ezekiel hears Yahweh's sentence: "The country is crowded, and the town full of wrongdoing. So I'll break the force of their strength, and their holy places will be desecrated. They'll beg and look for peace, and there won't be any."[^59-9]

Ezekiel heard Yahweh say more: "Yahweh's word came to me. Son of man, it said, if a country sins against me, I'll reach out my hand against it. I'll cut off its bread and bring famine on it. I'll wipe out its people and livestock. Suppose Noah, Daniel and Job all lived there. They couldn't rescue it. Their own goodness would save only themselves, says Yahweh.

"Or I could let wild animals loose to punish the land. It would be laid waste, and no one could cross it for fear of them. Say those three were living there. As I live, says Yahweh, they couldn't rescue their own children. They alone would come through, and the land would be destroyed."[^59-10]

<a id="page-60"></a>[\[Mommsen 60\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-60) Again: "The son won't carry his father's guilt, nor the father his son's. The good are credited with their own goodness, and nothing else. Suppose the wicked man turns from all he's done wrong. Say he keeps all my commands and does justice and great mercy. Then he'll surely live. None of his offenses will be held against him. The good he does will keep him alive.

"Do I want the wicked to die, says Yahweh? Wouldn't I rather they turned from their evil ways and were saved? But suppose a just man turns from his justice and does every wrong the godless do. Then none of his good acts will be remembered. He'll die in the offence he fell into and the sins he committed."[^60-1]

Near the end of his book, Ezekiel hears Yahweh say: "All the nations will know why the house of Israel was carried off captive. It was for their wrongdoing, because they left me. I turned my face away from them and handed them over to their enemies, and they were all killed. I dealt with them as their filth and their crimes deserved, and hid myself from them."[^60-2]

### Gildas turns to the Book of Wisdom, which tells rulers to love justice

That's enough of the holy prophets' threats. From Solomon's Book of Wisdom I've thought it right to add only a few sayings. They encourage and warn the kings as much as they threaten. I don't want them claiming I'd load people's shoulders with heavy bundles of words, more than anyone can carry, and not lift a finger to move them. By that I mean comforting them.[^60-3]

So let's hear what Solomon said: "Love justice, you who judge the earth."[^60-4] If people kept this one saying wholeheartedly, it would be enough to correct our country's leaders. If they'd loved it, they'd surely love Yahweh, its source and origin. "Serve Yahweh with goodness, and look for it with a simple heart." As someone sighed long ago, who'll live to see our people do this, if it can be done anywhere? "It's found by those who don't test it. It shows itself to those who trust it."[^60-5]

These men challenge Yahweh without a second thought. They push its commands aside with stubborn contempt and keep no faith with it. It speaks to them, gently or a little sternly, and they turn their backs, not their faces. "Crooked thoughts cut people off from Yahweh."[^60-6] That's plain to see in the tyrants of our time. But why should my poor voice mix with a meaning so clear? Let the only one who's true speak for me, as I said, the Holy Spirit. Scripture says of it: "The Holy Spirit, who teaches us, will run from deception."[^60-7] The same book says: "The spirit of Yahweh has filled the whole world."[^60-8]

Further on, he sees clearly how the bad and the good will end: "The hope of the wicked is like down blown away by the wind. It's like smoke a breeze scatters, like light foam a storm drives off, like the memory of a guest who stays one day and moves on. The good will live forever. Their reward is with Yahweh, and the Most High cares for them."

"So they'll be given a glorious kingdom and a beautiful crown from Yahweh. Its right hand will shield them, and its holy arm will defend them."[^60-9] The just and the wicked are very different in kind. As Yahweh said, "I'll honour those who respect me. Those who look down on me will be disgraced."[^60-10]

### The Book of Wisdom warns kings that Yahweh will judge them harshly

<a id="page-61"></a>[\[Mommsen 61\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-61) But let's move on to the rest. "Listen, all you kings," it says, "and understand. Learn, you judges of the ends of the earth. Pay attention, you who rule over crowds and take pride in your many nations. Yahweh gave you your power, and the Most High your strength. It'll examine what you do and search your thoughts. You served its kingdom, but you didn't govern rightly. You didn't keep the law of justice or do what it wanted. It'll come at you, terribly and fast. The harshest sentence falls on those in power. The humble are shown mercy, but the mighty will suffer torment to match.

"The ruler of all won't excuse anyone for their rank, or stand in awe of anyone's greatness. He made small and great alike, and cares for all equally. But a harsher punishment waits for the stronger. So I'm speaking to you rulers, so you'll learn wisdom and not fall. Those who keep justice will be acquitted. Those who study holiness will be made holy."[^61-1]

### Gildas ends his words to the kings and urges them to turn to Yahweh

So far I've argued with our country's kings in the prophets' words as much as my own. I want them to know this saying: "Run from sins as from a snake. If you go near them, they'll catch you. They have a lion's teeth that kill people's souls."[^61-2] Sirach, where that comes from, also says: "How great is Yahweh's mercy, and its forgiveness for those who turn to it!"[^61-3]

I don't have Paul's spirit, to say, "I could wish myself cursed and cut off from Christ for my brothers' sake."[^61-4] Still, I can say what the prophet said, and mean it: "It's terrible: a soul is lost!"[^61-5] The Book of Lamentations adds: "Let's search our ways and look into them, and come back to Yahweh. Let's lift up our hearts, with our hands, to Yahweh in heaven."[^61-6] I can say Paul's words too: "I long for every one of you with the tender love of Christ."[^61-7]

### Gildas would stop here, but must now call out Britain's bishops and priests

I'd be glad to stop here, out of shame, like a man tossed by the sea's waves who's rowed at last into the harbour he longed for. But I've seen such mountains of malice rising against Yahweh among our bishops, our other priests and the clergy of our own order. By the law, the witnesses throw the first stones, and then the people.[^61-8] So I have to pelt these men with all my strength, first with the hard rocks of words. Then the people should too, if they keep its rules. It isn't to kill their bodies. It's so that they'll die to their vices and live for Yahweh. Otherwise I'd be accused of favoring some over others.

<a id="page-62"></a>[\[Mommsen 62\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-62) But I ask pardon, as I said earlier, of the priests whose conduct I don't just praise but value above all the world's riches. I long and thirst to share their life for a while before I die, if I can. My sides are already walled in by the saints' two shields, which can't be beaten. My back stands firm against the walls of truth. My head is safely covered by Yahweh's help, my helmet. So let the rocks of honest criticism fly thick and fast.

## Part 7: Gildas turns on Britain's priests and judges them by the Bible

### Gildas says Britain's priests are foolish, greedy and shameless

Britain has priests, but they're fools.[^62-1] It has many ministers, but shameless ones, and clergy, but sly thieves. It has shepherds, so called, but they're wolves ready to slaughter souls. They don't look after the people's good. They look to fill their own bellies. They have churches, but go into them for dirty profit. They teach, but by the worst example, by their vices and bad ways. They rarely offer the sacrifice, and never stand at the altar with a pure heart. They don't condemn anyone's sins, since they commit the same ones. They ignore Christ's commands and work hard to satisfy their own lusts. They take the seat of the apostle Peter with filthy feet, and for their greed they fall into the deadly chair of Judas the traitor.

They hate the truth like an enemy and favour lies like dear brothers. They glare like snakes at the honest poor, as if they were monsters. They honour wicked rich men like angels from heaven, without any shame. They preach from the tips of their lips that the needy must get charity, but won't give a penny themselves. They keep quiet about the crowd's evil crimes, and blow up wrongs against themselves as if Christ had suffered them. They may drive a devout mother or sisters out of the house. Then they make free with women from outside, as if for some private service. To tell the truth, though it's ugly to say, more for them than for me, they disgrace those outsiders.

After that they chase church ranks more eagerly than the kingdom of heaven. Once they have them, they hold on like tyrants, and never grace them with an honest life. The saints' teaching ought to be in their ears all the time. If they ever hear it, they yawn and stare. At games and the silly stories of worldly men they're alert and keen, as if the way to death those tales open were the road to life.

They're hoarse as bulls from fat, and quick, to their ruin, to whatever is forbidden. They hold their faces high in arrogance, while their minds, gnawed by conscience, sink to the depths of hell. One lost penny makes them sad. One coin gained cheers them up. Ignorance, or the weight of their sins, leaves them dull and mute about the apostles' rules, and they even stop the mouths of those who know them. In the lying twists of worldly business they're experts.

<a id="page-63"></a>[\[Mommsen 63\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-63) Many who live that wicked life rush into the priesthood, or buy it with almost all they have, rather than being drawn into it. After they take the seat of bishop or priest, they go on wallowing like pigs in the same old filthy mud of unbearable sins. They've never truly sat in that chair. They've snatched a priest's name without its substance, or an apostle's rank, though they aren't yet fit for full faith or to repent their evils. How can such men come to any position in the Church, let alone the highest, and gain it? Only holy, perfect people who imitate the apostles take it rightly, those "above criticism," in Paul's words.[^63-1] Anyone else takes it as a great sacrilege.

### Gildas condemns priests who buy their offices from tyrants

What's more wicked and criminal than wanting to buy the office of bishop or priest with earthly money, like Simon the magician? Holiness and right living earn it far more fittingly, even with no other crimes mixed in. These men go wrong more eagerly, or hopelessly, because they buy their fake, useless priesthoods from tyrants and their father the devil, not from the apostles or their successors. They set this bought title on top of their wicked lives like a roof, the peak of all their evils. That way nobody readily blames them for old wrongs or new ones. As leaders of many, they can more easily grab what their greed and gluttony want.

Suppose these shameless men had offered such a deal to any holy priest or devout king, let alone to the apostle Peter. They'd have got the answer Simon the magician, their founder, got from Peter: "May your money perish with you."[^63-2]

Sadly, those who ordain these office seekers perhaps came into the priesthood the same way. They don't raise these men up but bring them low, and curse them instead of blessing them. Out of sinners they make not penitents, which would be right, but the sacrilegious and the hopeless. In a way, they set Judas, Jesus's betrayer, in Peter's chair,[^63-3] and Nicolaus, who invented a filthy heresy, in the place of Stephen the martyr.[^63-4] So they hardly loathe in their sons what they did themselves. They even revere it, since it surely happened to the fathers the same way.

In their own diocese, their fellow clergy may stand up to them sternly and refuse them so precious a gain, so that they can't find such a pearl there. Then they first send messengers ahead with care. Crossing seas and wide lands doesn't bother them. They enjoy it. They'll buy such a fine thing, of beauty beyond compare, by selling everything they own. To say it more truly, it's a devil's trick.

<a id="page-64"></a>[\[Mommsen 64\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-64) Then they come back home with great pomp and great show, or rather madness. They strut taller than ever. Before, they gazed at the mountaintops. Now they lift their half-asleep eyes straight up to the sky, to the highest woolly clouds. They push themselves on their country as new creations, or rather as the devil's instruments, as Novatus once did at Rome,[^64-1] that black pig who mauled Yahweh's pearl.[^64-2] In that state they mean to stretch out their hands to Christ's holy sacrifice. Those fingers deserve hell's avenging flames more than the sacred altars.

### Gildas asks the people what good such priests can do them

You poor people, what do you expect from such beasts of the belly, as Paul called them?[^64-3] Will they correct you? Far from urging themselves to good, they work hard at doing wrong, as Jeremiah complains.[^64-4] Will eyes like theirs give you light? They only look greedily at what slopes down to vice, to the gates of hell. As the Saviour said, run from these men, the greediest of wolves, like those of Arabia.[^64-5] Flee as fast as Lot fled the fiery rain on Sodom for the mountain.[^64-6] If you don't, you'll be the blind led by those who can't see, and you'll fall together into the pit of hell.[^64-7]

### Gildas grants some priests are chaste, but asks who among them is like Abel

Someone may say that not all bishops and priests are as bad as those I've described. Not all are stained with the disgrace of splitting the Church, or with pride or filth. I don't deny it strongly either. We know they're chaste and good, but we'll answer briefly. What good did it do Eli the priest that he alone didn't break Yahweh's commands? His sons took meat from the pots with forks before the fat was offered to Yahweh. Eli was punished by the same deadly anger as they were.[^64-8] Which of them, I ask, was killed like Abel, out of envy for a better sacrifice that heavenly fire carried up to heaven?[^64-9] They sneer at even a mild word of criticism.

Which of them has hated the advice of the wicked and refused to sit with the godless?[^64-10] Could it truly be said of him, as of Enoch, "Enoch walked with Yahweh and wasn't found"?[^64-11] He wasn't to be seen, that is, in the world's emptiness, when everyone had left Yahweh and was limping eagerly after idols. Which of them, like Noah at the time of the flood, has kept every enemy of Yahweh out of the saving ark, which is now the Church? That'd show plainly that only the innocent, or those who've truly repented, belong in Yahweh's house.[^64-12]

<a id="page-65"></a>[\[Mommsen 65\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-65) Which of them has blessed the victors and offered a sacrifice, as Melchizedek did? They were only three hundred, the number that stands for the Trinity. They freed a just man and beat the deadly armies of five kings with their victorious troops. They didn't want what belonged to others.[^65-1] Which of them has offered his own son on the altar to be killed at Yahweh's command, as Abraham did?[^65-2] It'd fulfill something like Christ's order to tear out the right eye that leads us into sin.[^65-3] It'd escape Jeremiah's curse on those who keep their swords from blood.[^65-4] Which of them has torn the memory of a wrong out of his heart by the roots, like Joseph?[^65-5]

Which of them has spoken with Yahweh on the mountain, like Moses, unafraid of the blaring trumpets?[^65-6] Who has carried down the two tablets, and a face with horns of light, so terrible, in the figurative sense, that those who didn't believe couldn't bear to look at it?[^65-7] Which of them has prayed for the people's wrongs and cried out from the bottom of his heart, as Moses did? "Yahweh," he said, "this people has committed a great sin. If you'll pardon them, forgive them. If not, wipe me out of your book."[^65-8]

### Gildas asks which priest has shown Phinehas's zeal or Joshua's courage

Which of them, fired by a wonderful zeal for Yahweh, has risen at once to punish fornication, as Phinehas the priest did? He cured the lust with the medicine of repentance, so that Yahweh's anger wouldn't blaze against the people,[^65-9] and it was counted to him as justice forever.[^65-10] Which of them has imitated Joshua son of Nun? Joshua wiped out the seven nations from the promised land, in the moral sense, and established the spiritual Israel in their place.[^65-11] Which of them has shown Yahweh's people their final borders across the Jordan, so everyone knew who got what? Phinehas and Joshua divided them wisely.[^65-12]

Which of them, to lay low countless thousands of the enemies of Yahweh's people, has sacrificed his only daughter to keep a vow of thanks, as Jephthah did?[^65-13] She came out to meet the victors with drums and dances, which are the desires of the flesh. The girl stands for our own pleasure. Jephthah would be following Paul, who says: "I don't seek my own good, but the good of many, so they may be saved."[^65-14]

Which of them has gone out like Gideon, to throw the camps of proud nations into confusion, rout and ruin?[^65-15] His men stood for the mystery of the Trinity, as we said above. They held fine jars in their hands, and sounding trumpets, which stand for the sense of the prophets and the apostles. So Yahweh told Isaiah, "Lift up your voice like a horn."[^65-16] In the Psalms, David said of the Twelve, "Their sound has gone out through all the earth."[^65-17]

<a id="page-66"></a>[\[Mommsen 66\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-66) The vessels flashed at night with brilliant fire. They stand for the bodies of the saints, joined to good works and burning with the Holy Spirit's flame. As Paul says, "We have this treasure in clay pots."[^66-1] Gideon first cut down the idol's grove, which in the moral sense means thick, dark greed. Then he went out in sure faith, after the clear signs of the fleece: the Jewish one left without heaven's rain, and the gentile one soaked with the Holy Spirit's dew.[^66-2]

### Gildas asks which priest has done what Samson, Samuel and Elijah did

Which of them, longing to die to the world and live for Christ, has struck down countless men, as Samson did?[^66-3] They were the pagans' lustful guests, praising their gods, that is, their senses, and their riches.[^66-4] As Paul says, greed "is the service of idols."[^66-5] Samson brought down the two pillars by the strength of his arms.[^66-6] They stand for the evil pleasures of soul and flesh, on which the house of all human wickedness is, in a way, built and propped.

Which of them has driven off fear of the Philistines with prayers and the burnt offering of a lamb still nursing, as Samuel did?[^66-7] He called up thunder and rain from the clouds when no one expected it.[^66-8] He made a king without flattering him, and cast him off when Yahweh wasn't pleased with him, anointing a better man to rule in his place.[^66-9] Which of them, saying goodbye to the people, will stand up as Samuel did and say: "Here I am. Speak up in front of Yahweh and its anointed. Have I taken anyone's ox or donkey? Have I cheated or oppressed anyone? Have I accepted a bribe from someone's hand?" The people answered him: "You haven't wronged us or crushed us, or stolen anything from us."[^66-10]

Which of them is like Elijah, the great prophet? He burned up a hundred proud men with fire from heaven, and spared fifty humble ones.[^66-11] With no false flattery he told a wicked ruler his death was coming, a king who'd consulted not Yahweh through his seers but the god of Ekron.[^66-12] He struck down all of Baal's prophets with a flashing sword, which is Yahweh's word.[^66-13] They stand for human senses, always bent, as we've said, on envy and greed.

Moved by zeal for Yahweh, Elijah took heaven's rain from the land of the wicked. He locked it up for three years and six months, as if behind the strongest bolt of want.[^66-14] Dying of hunger and thirst in the desert, he complained: "Yahweh, they've killed your prophets and dug up your altars. I'm the only one left, and they're out to take my life."[^66-15]

### Gildas asks which priest is like Elisha in judgement or in faith

Which of them has punished a dear disciple at least with banishment, if not with lasting leprosy, as Elisha did? The man had loaded himself with earthly goods, against all custom. Elisha had refused those gifts firmly, though he was begged hard to take them.[^66-16] Which of them, like Elisha, has opened the eyes of a soul among us? His servant had lost hope of living, shaking at the enemy's sudden war gear as they besieged the city. Elisha prayed to Yahweh with all his heart, so that the boy could see the mountain full of heaven's army coming to help, chariots and horsemen shining with faces of fire. He made him believe that Yahweh was stronger to save than the enemy was to fight.[^66-17]

<a id="page-67"></a>[\[Mommsen 67\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-67) Which of them will help another man by touching him, as Elisha did with his body? Elisha was dead to the world but alive to Yahweh. The other lies lifeless in a different way, cut off from Yahweh but living for his vices. The man would leap up at once and thank Christ for a healing almost everyone had given up on.[^67-1]

Whose lips, of all of them, have been made clean like Isaiah's? A cherub carried a burning coal from the altar with tongs, so his sins could be wiped out as he humbly confessed them.[^67-2] Which of them has prayed alongside a devout king, as Isaiah did with Hezekiah, and brought down a hundred eighty-five thousand of the Assyrian army? An angel's hand struck them, with no trace of a wound to be seen.[^67-3]

Which of them has endured the filth and stench of prisons like brief deaths, as blessed Jeremiah did? He went through them for Yahweh's commands and the threats sent from heaven, and for telling the truth even to those who wouldn't listen.[^67-4] In short, which of them has suffered as the holy prophets did? As Paul said, they wandered in mountains, caves and holes in the ground. They were stoned and sawn apart, and tried by every kind of death for Yahweh's name.[^67-5]

### Gildas asks which priest has suffered as the apostles and martyrs did

But why linger on old examples, as if the New Testament had none? Let them hear us, those who think they can walk the narrow road of the Christian faith without any labour, just by holding up a priest's name. I'll pick a few flowers, the finest, from the wide, lovely meadow of the New Testament's young soldiers. Which of you dozes in a priest's seat rather than sitting there rightly? Which of you has been thrown out of the assembly of the wicked, then beaten with rods, and thanked the Trinity with all your heart, as the holy apostles did? They were glad to be thought worthy to suffer insult for Christ, the true god.[^67-6]

Which of you has died in body for bearing honest witness to Yahweh, struck on the head with a fuller's club, like James, the first bishop under the New Testament?[^67-7] Which of you has been beheaded with the sword by a cruel prince, like James, John's brother?[^67-8] Who has been stoned by wicked hands, like Stephen, the first deacon, the gospel's martyr? His only crime was that he saw Yahweh, who those without faith couldn't see.[^67-9] Who has breathed his last fixed upside down on a cross, out of reverence for Christ's gallows, like Peter, the worthy keeper of the keys of heaven's kingdom? He'd honour Christ in death as much as in life.[^67-10]

Which of you has had his head cut off by the sword for confessing Christ, like Paul, called the chosen vessel, the teacher of the gentiles? He'd been through prison chains, shipwrecks and beatings with rods. He'd faced constant dangers from rivers, robbers, pagans, Jews and false apostles. He'd suffered hunger, fasting and sleepless nights, his care for all the churches, his burning for those who fell, his weakness for the weak. Preaching Christ's gospel, he'd gone almost around the world.[^67-11]

### Gildas holds up Ignatius of Antioch to Britain's priests

<a id="page-68"></a>[\[Mommsen 68\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-68) Which of you has been crushed at Rome by the jaws of lions, like the holy martyr Ignatius, bishop of the city of Antioch, for his witness to Christ, after wonderful acts in his name? Hear the words he spoke as he was led to his death. If your faces ever blushed at them, you won't think yourselves priests next to him. You won't even think yourselves ordinary Christians. In the letter he sent to the church of Rome, he says:

"From Syria to Rome I fight with beasts by land and sea, night and day. I'm chained and bound to ten leopards, I mean the soldiers set to guard me, who get crueler the better we treat them. Their wickedness teaches me more, though that doesn't make me righteous. Oh, the saving beasts made ready for me! When will they come? When will they be let loose? When will they get to enjoy my flesh? I hope they're made fiercer still. I'll invite them to eat me. I'll beg them not to be afraid to touch my body, as they've been with some. If they hold back, I'll force them. I'll throw myself at them.

"Forgive me, please. I know what's good for me. Now I'm starting to be Christ's disciple. Let envy go, whether it comes from human feeling or from spiritual evil, so that I may reach Jesus Christ. Let fires, crosses and beasts come, scattered bones, torn limbs, pain through the whole body, and every torture the devil's art can find. Let them all fall on me alone, as long as I win Christ in the end."[^68-1]

What do you see in this, with your soul's eyes asleep? What do you hear of it, with your senses' ears deaf? Shake the dark, black fog of indifference off your hearts, please, so you can see the shining light of truth and humility. A Christian who's not ordinary but perfect, a priest not of low rank but the highest, a martyr not slack but outstanding, says: "Now I'm starting to be Christ's disciple."

You puff yourselves up like that Lucifer thrown down from heaven, in words, not in power. In a way you chew over what your author drew long ago, and show it off in how you carry yourselves: "I'll climb into the sky and be like the Most High."[^68-2] You sound like the Assyrian king Sennacherib, boasting: "I've dug and drunk water, and dried up all the streams of the banks with the soles of my feet."[^68-3]

You'd do far better to imitate and hear the one who's the truly unbeaten pattern of all goodness and humility. He says through David: "But I'm a worm and no man, mocked by men and despised by the people."[^68-4] How strange that he called himself the one humanity mocks, when he wiped out the whole world's shame! He says in the gospel too: "I can do nothing of myself."[^68-5] Yet he's as old as the Father and the Holy Spirit, of one and the same substance. He made heaven and earth with all their beauty beyond price, not by another's power but by his own. You, though, have lifted up your words in pride, while the Book of Sirach asks: "How can dust and ash be proud?"[^68-6]

### Gildas asks which priest has the courage of Polycarp and other martyrs

<a id="page-69"></a>[\[Mommsen 69\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-69) But to come back to my point. Which of you, I say, is like Polycarp, the fine shepherd of the church of Smyrna and Christ's witness? He kindly set a table for the guests who were eagerly dragging him to the stake. Thrown into the fire out of love for Christ, Polycarp said: "The one who's let me suffer this punishment will let me stand in the flames without moving, with no nails to hold me."[^69-1]

Flying past the great forest of the saints in words, I'll give one more example: Basil, bishop of Caesarea. A wicked prince threatened him that he'd surely die if by the next day he wasn't stained with the Arian filth, like the rest. Basil is said to have answered: "Tomorrow I'll be the same man as today. If only you wouldn't change either."[^69-2] He also said: "I wish I had some worthy gift to give the one who'd free Basil sooner from the knot of this bag."[^69-3]

Which of you has held to the rule Peter and the apostles laid down, unbroken, when tyrants threatened? Holy priests in every age have kept it, beating back the human prompting that rushes headlong to wickedness. It says: "We must obey Yahweh rather than men."[^69-4]

### Gildas turns to the prophets again, and first to Yahweh's sentence on Eli

So, as usual, let's flee to Yahweh's mercy and the voices of its holy prophets. Let them hurl the spears of their sayings at the imperfect shepherds for us, as they did at the tyrants before, so that those they wound may heal. Let's see what threats Yahweh speaks through these seers against lazy, disgraceful priests, who don't teach the people well by example or by word. Take Eli at Shiloh. He didn't avenge Yahweh on his sons, who held Yahweh in contempt, with the stern zeal Yahweh was owed. Eli warned them softly and gently, with a father's love. For that he was condemned with this punishment, when the prophet said to him:

"This is what Yahweh says: I showed myself plainly to your father's house when they were slaves to Pharaoh in Egypt. I chose your father's family out of all the tribes of Israel to be my priests." The man of Yahweh asks Eli: "Why have you looked on my incense and my sacrifice with a greedy eye? Why have you honoured your sons more than me, blessing them first at every offering in front of me?"

He gives Yahweh's sentence: "So now Yahweh says: I'll honour those who respect me, and those who despise me will come to nothing. The days are coming when I'll wipe out your name and the line of your father's house." He tells Eli how he'll know: "This will be your sign, and it'll fall on your two sons, Hophni and Phinehas. Both will die on one day, by men's swords."[^69-5]

That's what happens to those who correct the people under them only in words, without fitting punishment. What will become of those who drag others into evil by sinning themselves?

### Gildas cites the prophet who disobeyed Yahweh's word and met a lion

<a id="page-70"></a>[\[Mommsen 70\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-70) What happened to that true prophet is plain too. He was sent from Judea to prophesy against Jeroboam, a wicked king, at Bethel, and forbidden to taste any food there. The sign he predicted appeared, and the king's withered hand was restored. Then another man, said to be a seer too, tricked him into taking a little bread and water. His host said to him: "This is what Yahweh says: You disobeyed Yahweh's mouth and didn't keep the command Yahweh your god gave you. You came back and ate and drank in the very place where I'd forbidden it. So your body won't be laid in your fathers' tomb."

The first Book of Kings tells what happened next: "After he'd eaten and drunk, he saddled his donkey and left. A lion met him on the road and killed him."[^70-1]

### Isaiah condemns priests who rob the poor and are drunk at the altar

Hear the holy prophet Isaiah too, speaking of priests this way: "Doom to the wicked, who'll come to evil! He'll be paid back for what his hands have done. My people's oppressors have stripped them, and women rule over them. My people, those who call you happy are misleading you and wrecking the path you walk. Yahweh rises to judge; it stands to try the peoples. Yahweh will come to judgement with the elders of its people and its princes. You've grazed my vineyard bare. What you've robbed from the poor is in your houses. Why do you crush my people and grind the faces of the needy? says Yahweh of armies."[^70-2]

Isaiah adds: "Doom to those who make unjust laws, who write down injustice to oppress the weak in court and do violence to the cause of my humble people, so that widows are their prey and they plunder orphans. What will you do on the day of reckoning, when disaster comes from far off?"[^70-3] Isaiah says the same of the religious leaders: "These too have lost their senses from wine and stagger from drink. The priests are out of their minds with drunkenness. They're swallowed up by liquor and reel in their cups. They didn't know the seer and missed what's right. Every table is full of filthy vomit, with no room left."[^70-4]

### Isaiah warns the mockers who rule Jerusalem and trust in their pact

"So hear Yahweh's word, you mockers who rule my people in Jerusalem. You've said: We've made a treaty with death, a pact with hell. When the flooding scourge sweeps through, it won't reach us, because we've made lies our hope, and deception will shelter us."[^70-5] Isaiah answers them: "Hail will wipe away what you trusted, and waters will flood your refuge. Your treaty with death will be cancelled, and your pact with hell won't stand. When the flooding scourge goes by, you'll be trampled. Whenever it passes, it'll carry you off."[^70-6]

Isaiah gives Yahweh's word again: "Yahweh said: This people comes near me with its mouth and honours me with its lips, but its heart is far from me. So I'll go on amazing this people with a great, astounding wonder. The wisdom of its wise will perish, and the understanding of its clever men will be hidden.[^70-7] Doom to you who hide your plans deep from Yahweh, whose works are in the dark, who say: Who sees us? Who knows us? This thinking of yours is perverse."[^70-8]

<a id="page-71"></a>[\[Mommsen 71\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-71) Further on: "This is what Yahweh says: Heaven is my throne and the earth my footstool. What house will you build me? Where will I rest? My hand made all these things, and they all came to be, says Yahweh. Who'll I look to, except the poor and broken in spirit, who trembles at my words? Whoever sacrifices an ox is like one who kills a man. Anyone who slaughters a sheep is like one who brains a dog. To make an offering is like giving pig's blood. To burn incense in remembrance is like blessing an idol. They've chosen all these in their ways, and their soul delights in their filth."[^71-1]

### Jeremiah says priests and prophets have lied, and Yahweh will cast them off

Listen too to what Jeremiah, virgin and prophet, says to foolish shepherds: "This is what Yahweh says: What wrong did your fathers find in me, that they went far from me, walked after emptiness and became empty?"[^71-2] Yahweh says to them: "You came in and polluted my land, and made my inheritance a disgrace. The priests didn't say: Where's Yahweh? Those who held the law didn't know me, and the shepherds betrayed me. So I'll still bring a case against you, says Yahweh, and argue it with your children."[^71-3] Later Jeremiah cries: "Shocking, astonishing things have happened in the land. The prophets preached lies, the priests clapped their hands, and my people loved it. So what will happen to it in the end?"[^71-4]

"Who can I speak to and warn, so they'll listen? Their ears are uncircumcised, and they can't hear. Yahweh's word has become a joke to them, and they won't take it in.[^71-5] So I'll stretch out my hand over those who live in the land, says Yahweh. From the least to the greatest, they're all greedy, and from prophet to priest, they all cheat. They treated the wound of my people's daughter lightly, saying: All's well, all's well, when nothing is. Those who did the disgusting thing were put to shame. No, they weren't ashamed at all, and didn't know how to blush. So they'll fall among the fallen. When I come to punish them, they'll collapse, says Yahweh."[^71-6]

Jeremiah warns again: "All these princes of the rebels go about lying. Bronze and iron, they're all corrupt. The bellows failed in the fire, and the smelter worked in vain, because their evil wasn't burned away. Call them rejected silver, since Yahweh has thrown them away."[^71-7]

Yahweh tells them to remember Shiloh: "It's I, it's I, I've seen it, says Yahweh. Go to my place at Shiloh, where my name lived at first, and see what I did to it for the wickedness of my people Israel. Now you've done all these things, says Yahweh. I spoke to you, rising early and speaking, and you didn't listen. I called out to you, and you didn't answer. So I'll do to this house as I did to Shiloh. It carries my name, and you trust in it. I'll do the same to the place I gave you and your fathers, and throw you out of my sight."[^71-8]

### Jeremiah says the shepherds are foolish and the prophets promise false peace

Jeremiah mourns for the people: "My sons have gone out from me and are no more. There's no one left to stretch out my tent or put up its curtains, because the shepherds have acted like fools and haven't looked for Yahweh. So they didn't understand, and their flock is scattered."[^71-9] Yahweh asks: "What's my beloved doing in my house, so much wickedness? Will holy meat take away your evils, which you boasted of? Yahweh called you a fine olive tree, rich, lovely, fruitful and handsome. At the sound of a great word, fire blazed up in it and its thickets burned."[^71-10]

<a id="page-72"></a>[\[Mommsen 72\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-72) Again: "Come, gather, all you wild beasts, hurry to devour. Many shepherds have ruined my vineyard. They've trampled my share and turned my precious portion into an empty desert."[^72-1] He says as well: "This is what Yahweh says to this people, who loved to wander, didn't rest, and didn't please Yahweh. Now it'll remember their wrongs and punish their sins."[^72-2]

Jeremiah protests to Yahweh: "The prophets tell them: You won't see the sword, and there'll be no famine among you. Yahweh will give you true peace in this place. Yahweh said to me: They speak falsely in my name. I didn't send them or command them. They offer you a lying vision, fortune-telling, fraud and the deception of their own hearts. So Yahweh says: Those false seers will be wiped out by sword and hunger. The people they spoke to will be thrown into the streets of Jerusalem, and no one will bury them."[^72-3]

### Jeremiah says Yahweh will punish the shepherds and the prophets of Jerusalem

Jeremiah gives Yahweh's word to Judah's rulers: "Doom to the shepherds who scatter and tear apart the flock of my pasture, says Yahweh. So Yahweh, the god of Israel, says this to those who feed my people: You've scattered my sheep, driven them off, and not looked after them. I'll punish you for the evil you've done, says Yahweh."[^72-4] He goes on: "Prophet and priest are both corrupt. I've found their wickedness in my own house, says Yahweh. So their way will be like a slippery path in the dark. They'll be pushed and fall on it, because I'll bring evil on them, the year of their punishment, says Yahweh.[^72-5]

"I saw madness in the prophets of Samaria. They prophesied by Baal and misled my people Israel. Among Jerusalem's seers I found something like adultery and a road of lies. They strengthened the hands of the worst, so that none turned from his wickedness. They've all become like Sodom to me, and its people like Gomorrah. So Yahweh says this to the prophets: I'll feed them wormwood and give them gall to drink. From them, from Jerusalem, filth has gone out over the whole earth.[^72-6]

"Yahweh of armies says this: Don't listen to the words of those who prophesy to you and fool you. They speak a vision from their own hearts, not from Yahweh's mouth. They tell those who blaspheme me: Yahweh has said you'll have peace. They've told all who follow their own wicked will: No evil will happen to you. Who has stood among Yahweh's advisers and seen and heard its word? Who has paid attention to what it says and listened? Look, the whirlwind of Yahweh's anger is going out, and a storm will burst on the heads of the wicked. Yahweh's fury won't turn back until it's done and finished what its heart intends. In the last days you'll understand its plan."[^72-7]

### Gildas cites Joel, who calls on drunken priests to weep

You think or do little about what holy Joel declared, warning lazy clergy, in tears over the people's loss for their sins: "Wake up, you who are drunk, and cry. Wail, all you who drink wine till you're senseless, because joy and delight are taken from your mouths. Mourn, you priests who serve the altar, because the fields are ruined. Let the land go into mourning, because the grain is spoiled, the grapes dried up, the oil gone. The farmers have given up. Weep, you landowners, for the wheat and barley, because the harvest is lost. The vine is parched and the figs have shriveled. The pomegranate, the palm, the apple and every tree in the countryside have withered, because the sons of men have put joy to shame."[^72-8]

<a id="page-73"></a>[\[Mommsen 73\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-73) You must take all this in a spiritual sense, so your souls don't wither in so deadly a famine of Yahweh's word. Joel tells them what to do: "Weep, priests who serve Yahweh, and say: Spare your people, Yahweh. Don't make your inheritance a disgrace, or let foreigners rule them, so the nations won't say: Where's their god?"[^73-1] But you don't hear any of it. You do everything that makes Yahweh's furious anger blaze hotter.

### Gildas cites Hosea, who calls the priests a trap for the people

Note well, too, what holy Hosea the prophet said to clergy of your sort: "Listen to this, priests. Pay attention, people of Israel and royal house. Take it to heart, because judgement is for you. You've become a trap for the lookout, and a net spread over Tabor." That's a device of hunters.[^73-2]

### Amos says Yahweh hates their feasts and will send a famine of its word

Let a rejection like this be aimed at you too, from Yahweh, through the prophet Amos, who says: "I've hated and rejected your feast days. I won't accept the smell of your solemn gatherings. Even if you offer your burnt offerings and sacrifices, I won't take them. I won't look at your thank gifts. Take the noise of your songs away from me. I won't listen to the psalm of your instruments."[^73-3]

A famine of the gospel's food is raging in you, eating up your soul's very insides, as Amos also predicted. "The days are coming, says Yahweh," he says, "when I'll send hunger on the land: not for bread or drink, but a famine of hearing Yahweh's word. The waters will be stirred from one sea to the other and from north to east. They'll run around looking for Yahweh's message and won't find it."[^73-4]

### Gildas cites Micah against the princes and prophets who devour the people

Hear holy Micah too, sounding briefly like a trumpet of heaven against the people's sly rulers: "Listen now, princes of the house of Jacob. Isn't it for you to know justice? You hate good and seek evil. You tear the skin off them and the flesh off their bones. You've devoured my people, flayed them, broken them and cut them up like meat in a pot. They'll cry out to Yahweh and it won't hear them. It'll turn its face from them at that time, because of the wicked things they've done in their schemes.[^73-5]

"This is what Yahweh says about the prophets who mislead my people. They bite with their teeth and promise peace, but against anyone who puts nothing in their mouths they stir up war. So you'll have night in place of vision, and darkness instead of divination. The sun will set on these seers, and the day go dark over them. Those who see dreams will be shamed and the fortune-tellers mocked. They'll all run one another down, because no one will answer them. That is, unless I fill myself with strength in the spirit of Yahweh, with justice and power, to tell Jacob its wickedness and Israel its sins.[^73-6]

<a id="page-74"></a>[\[Mommsen 74\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-74) "So hear this, leaders of Jacob's house, and the rest of Israel's people. You loathe what's right and twist everything straight. You build Zion with blood and Jerusalem with crime. Its rulers judge for bribes, its priests answer for pay, and its prophets tell fortunes for money. Yet they lean on Yahweh and say: Isn't Yahweh among us? No harm will reach us. So because of you Zion will be plowed like a field. Jerusalem will be like a watchman's hut in an orchard, and the Temple mount like a forest."[^74-1]

Micah mourns: "Poor me! I've become like someone gleaning stubble at harvest, or a grape left after the vintage, when there's no cluster to eat. Sadly, the first soul has perished by earthly works! Reverence for sinners keeps springing up from the earth, and no one among men sets them right. They all go to court for blood, and each one torments his neighbour. They ready their hands for evil."[^74-2]


### Gildas cites Zephaniah on Jerusalem's princes, judges and priests

Pay attention to what the great Zephaniah argued about the gluttons of his day, the ones who led the way for you. He was speaking of Jerusalem, which in the spiritual sense means the Church, or the soul. "O city that was bright and free," he says, "the trusting dove! She didn't obey the voice or take correction. She didn't trust in Yahweh or come near her god."[^74-3] Then he shows why: "Her princes are like roaring lions. Her judges are like the wolves of Arabia, which leave nothing till dawn. Her prophets carry a spirit of contempt. Her priests have desecrated holy things and broken the law. But Yahweh within her is just, and it does no wrong. Every morning it'll give its judgement."[^74-4]

### Gildas cites Zechariah, who warns the shepherds who feed on their flock

Hear the blessed Zechariah too, warning you in Yahweh's word: "This is what Yahweh almighty says: Judge fairly. Each of you, show mercy and kindness to his brother. Don't use your power to hurt the widow, the orphan, the stranger or the poor. Don't let any of you nurse a grudge against another. But they were stubborn and wouldn't obey. They turned their backs in their foolishness and stopped their ears. They set their hearts so nothing could move them, so they wouldn't take in my law, or the words Yahweh almighty sent by its spirit through the earlier prophets. So great anger came from Yahweh."[^74-5]

Zechariah warns again: "Those who spoke have spoken trouble. The fortune-tellers have seen false visions and told lying dreams. They gave empty comfort. So the people have withered like sheep and suffered, because no one healed them. My anger burns against the shepherds, and I'll punish the lambs."[^74-6]

<a id="page-75"></a>[\[Mommsen 75\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-75) A little later: "Listen to the herders wailing, because their greatness has come to misery. Hear the lions roaring, because the Jordan's banks are ruined. This is what Yahweh almighty says: Those who owned the flock slaughtered it and felt no regret. Those who sold the sheep said: Blessed be Yahweh, we've grown rich. Their shepherds had no pity on them at all. So I won't spare the people of the land any longer, says Yahweh."[^75-1]

### Gildas cites Malachi on priests who offer Yahweh lame and stolen animals

Take in, besides, what holy Malachi the prophet has announced to you. "You priests," he says, "who despise my name, and ask: How have we despised your name? By offering polluted bread on my altar. Then you say: How have we made it unclean? By saying Yahweh's table counts for nothing, and looking down on what's set on it. If you bring a blind animal to sacrifice, isn't that wrong? If you offer one that's lame or sick, isn't that evil? Give it to your governor, then. See if he'll take it, or think well of you, says Yahweh almighty. Now plead with your god and beg it. You've done these things yourselves. Should I think well of any of you?"[^75-2]

Malachi gives Yahweh's charge against the priests: "You've brought in the lame and the sick from what you stole, and offered it as your gift. Should I accept that from your hand? says Yahweh. Cursed is the cheat who has a male in his flock, and makes a vow, and sacrifices a feeble one to Yahweh. I'm a great king, says Yahweh of armies, and my name is feared among the nations."[^75-3]

"Now this command is for you, priests. Suppose you won't listen, and won't take it to heart to give glory to my name, says Yahweh. Then I'll send poverty on you and curse your blessings, because you haven't cared. Look, I'll throw your arm away and scatter the manure of your feasts on your faces."[^75-4]

Meanwhile, if any inner hearing is left in you at all, listen to what he says of the holy priest. It may make you turn the tools of your wickedness to good more eagerly. "My covenant was with him," he says, speaking by the story of Levi, or of Moses, "one of life and peace. I gave him fear, and he feared me and stood in awe of my name. The law of truth was in his mouth, and no wrong was found on his tongue. He walked with me in harmony and fairness, and turned many away from wrongdoing. A priest's lips should guard knowledge, and people should seek instruction from him, because he's Yahweh's own messenger."[^75-5]

Now Malachi turns his meaning around again and goes back to condemning the wicked. "But you've left the road," he says, "and made many stumble in the law. You've broken the covenant with Levi, says Yahweh of armies. So I've made you despised and low before every people, since you haven't kept my ways and have played favorites in judging. Don't we all have one father? Didn't one god create us? Then why does each of you despise his brother?"[^75-6]

Malachi warns of the day of judgement: "Look, Yahweh of armies will come. Who can imagine the day of its coming? Who'll stand to see it? Yahweh will go out like a blazing fire, like the washers' soap. It'll sit to smelt and refine the metal. It'll purify the sons of Levi and strain them like gold and silver."[^75-7]

He gives Yahweh's answer to their complaints: "Your words have been hard against me, says Yahweh. You've said: It's useless to serve Yahweh. What did we gain by keeping its commands and walking sadly in the sight of Yahweh of armies? So now we'll call the proud happy. Those who do wrong have prospered. They've tested Yahweh and come off safe."[^75-8]

### Gildas cites Ezekiel on priests who break Yahweh's law and neglect its people

Note what the prophet Ezekiel said: "Calamity will come on disaster," he says, "and report on rumour. They'll ask the seer for a vision, but the priest will lose the law, and the old will have no advice to give."[^75-9]

<a id="page-76"></a>[\[Mommsen 76\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-76) Again: "This is what Yahweh says: Because your words are lies and your divinations empty, look, I'm against you, says Yahweh. I'll stretch out my hand against the prophets who see false things and speak nonsense. They won't have a place among my people, or be written in the register of Israel's house, or enter the land of Israel. Then you'll know that I'm Yahweh. They've misled my people by saying: Yahweh's peace, when it gives none. One man builds a wall, and they plaster it, and it'll fall."[^76-1]

Ezekiel turns on those who sell false prophecies: "Doom to the women who sew pillows under every elbow, and make veils for the heads of people of all ages, to hunt down souls. My people are hunted down. They took hold of them and dishonoured me among my people, for a handful of barley and a scrap of bread. They killed those who shouldn't die and spared those who shouldn't live, while you tell lies to a people that listens."[^76-2]

Later Ezekiel hears Yahweh's charge: "Son of man, say: You're a land that has had no rain, no shower on the day of anger. Her princes are like roaring lions tearing prey. They devour souls by force and take bribes, and her widows have multiplied within her. Her priests have broken my law and desecrated my holy things. They made no difference between sacred and unholy, and didn't tell the unclean from the clean. They hid their eyes from my Sabbath days, and I was dishonoured among them."[^76-3]

### Ezekiel says a watchman who gives no warning must answer for the dead

Yahweh says through Ezekiel: "I looked for a man of them who lived rightly and would stand in front of me for the land, so I wouldn't destroy it in the end. I found none. So I poured out my anger on it, in the fire of my rage, to consume them. I've brought their ways down on their own heads, says Yahweh."[^76-4]

Later Yahweh makes Ezekiel its watchman: "The word of Yahweh came to me. Son of man, it said, speak to the children of my people. Say to them: Suppose I bring a sword on a land. Its people take one man from among them and make him their watchman. He sees the blade coming on the country, blows the trumpet and warns the people. Then anyone who hears the horn and ignores it, when the sword comes and takes him, has his blood on his own head. He heard the call and paid no attention, so his death is on him. The lookout has kept his own soul safe.

"But suppose the watchman sees the sword coming and doesn't sound the alarm, and the people take no warning. The enemy comes and takes a life from among them. That man is taken for his own wrongdoing, but I'll require his blood from the watchman's hand. You, son of man, I've made a watchman for the house of Israel. You'll hear a word from my mouth. Suppose I say to a sinner: You'll surely die. If you don't speak to turn the wicked man from his way, he'll perish in his sin, but I'll require his blood from you. If you warn him of his way, so he gives it up, and he doesn't, he'll die in his wickedness. You'll have saved your soul."[^76-5]

### Gildas turns from the prophets to what the Gospels say of priests

These few of the prophets' many witnesses must be enough. They beat back the pride or laziness of stubborn priests, so they won't think we tell them these things on our own invention, rather than on the authority of the law and the saints. So let's see what the Gospel's trumpet, sounding over the world, says to disorderly clergy.

<a id="page-77"></a>[\[Mommsen 77\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-77) As I've said already, we aren't talking about those who hold the apostles' seat lawfully. They know well how to give their fellow servants spiritual food in due time, if there are many such now. We mean the unskilled shepherds who abandon the sheep, feed on emptiness, and lack a skilled herder's words. Whoever refuses or denies these decrees is plainly no lawful pastor. He isn't even an ordinary Christian. The rules aren't mine, since I'm very small. They come from the Old Testament and the New.

As one of our own has said well: "We sincerely wish that the Church's enemies may be ours too, without any treaty. Her friends and defenders should be held not just our allies but our fathers and lords."

Let each of them weigh his conscience truly, and he'll find out whether he sits in the priest's chair by right. Let's see what the world's Saviour and Maker says. "You're the salt of the earth," he says. "But if it loses its taste, how can it be made salty again? It's good for nothing anymore, except to be thrown out and trampled by people."[^77-1]

### Christ calls priests the light of the world, and Gildas asks who shines

This one witness alone could fully refute any shameless man. But more must be added, to prove still more clearly in Christ's words how heavy the loads of crime are that these false priests crush themselves under. Christ tells his followers: "You're the light of the world. A city set on a hill can't be hidden. Nor do people put a burning lamp under a basket. They put it on a stand, so it shines for everyone in the house."[^77-2]

Which of today's priests, in the grip of blind ignorance, is like a bright flame for all who sit at home at night, with the glow of both knowledge and good works? Which is thought a safe refuge for all the Church's children, open and plain to see, as a city set on a high hilltop is a strong one for its citizens? Then there's what follows: "Let your light shine before people, so they see your good works and glorify your Father in heaven."[^77-3] Which of them can do that even for a single day?

Instead, a dense fog of them, a black night of sins, lies so heavy over the whole island that it turns nearly everyone off the straight path. It sends them wandering down the tangled back roads of crime. Through their works the heavenly Father isn't just left without praise. He's blasphemed unbearably.

<a id="page-78"></a>[\[Mommsen 78\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-78) I'd really like to explain all these witnesses of holy Scripture, those set in this letter and those still to come, in some historical or moral sense, as far as my small ability allows.

### Gildas sets down Christ's sayings on judging others and on false prophets

But this little work mustn't run on and on for those who despise it, sneer at it and turn from it, since what they reject is Yahweh's, not mine. So the witnesses gathered, and those still to gather, are set down plainly, without roundabout words. Christ warns: "Whoever breaks one of the least of these commands and teaches people to do so will be called the smallest in the kingdom of heaven."[^78-1] Later in that sermon he says: "Don't judge, so you won't be judged. The verdict you give is the one you'll get."[^78-2]

Which of you, I ask, will look at the next words? "Why do you see the speck in your brother's eye," he says, "and not notice the beam in your own? How can you offer to take the splinter out of his, with a plank in yours?"[^78-3]

Or take what Christ says next: "Don't give what's holy to dogs, or throw your pearls before pigs. They may trample them underfoot and turn and tear you apart." That has happened to you very often.[^78-4] Warning the people not to be misled by sly teachers like you, he said: "Beware of false prophets, who come to you in sheep's clothing but inside are greedy wolves. You'll know them by their harvest. Do people pick grapes from thorns, or figs from thistles? So every good tree yields good fruit, and a rotten one a poor crop."[^78-5] Christ ends that warning: "Not everyone who says to me, lord, lord, will enter the kingdom of heaven. Only those who do what my Father above wants will get in."[^78-6]

### Christ sends his apostles out as sheep, but these priests come as wolves

What will become of you, who cling to Yahweh with your lips only and not your hearts, as Isaiah said?[^78-7] How do you fulfill what follows: "Look, I send you out like sheep among wolves"?[^78-8] You go out the other way round, as the pack goes into the flock. Or what Christ says: "Be shrewd as snakes and innocent as doves"?[^78-9] You're sharp enough, sure, at biting someone with a deadly mouth. You aren't clever at putting your body in the way to shield your head, which is Christ. You trample him with every effort of your evil works.

Nor do you have the doves' innocence. You're more like the raven's blackness. Once you flew out of the ark, which is the Church, you found the stench of bodily pleasures and never came back to her with a pure heart.[^78-10]

But let's see the rest. "Don't fear those who kill the body but can't destroy the soul," he says. "Be afraid of him who can send both to ruin in hell."[^78-11] Think back on which of these you've done. Which of you won't be wounded deep in his heart by the next witness, which the Saviour speaks to the apostles about wicked bishops? "Let them be. They're blind guides for people who can't see. If one who can't see leads another, both will fall into a pit."[^78-12] The people in your charge, or rather the people you've misled, badly need to hear it.

### Gildas cites Christ's words on teachers who say and don't do

<a id="page-79"></a>[\[Mommsen 79\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-79) Listen to Jesus's words to the apostles and the crowds. I'm told you aren't ashamed to bring them out often yourselves. "The scribes and Pharisees sit in Moses's chair. So keep and do whatever they tell you. But don't do as they do, because they talk and don't act."[^79-1] Teaching that bad works darken is surely dangerous and useless to priests. "Doom to you, hypocrites, who shut the kingdom of heaven in people's faces! You don't go in yourselves, nor let those who are trying go in."[^79-2]

You'll be punished hard in the world to come. It won't be only for the great crimes you commit, but also for those who die every day by your example. Their blood will be required from your hands on the day of judgement.

Look at the evil the parable of the servant holds out. He says in his heart: My master is taking his time. Maybe that's why he'd begun to beat the others who served with him, eating and drinking with drunks.[^79-3] "So the lord of that slave will come on a day he doesn't expect," he says, "at an hour he doesn't know. He'll cut him off," from the holy priests, that is, "and put his share with the hypocrites," surely with those who use a priest's cover to hide much wickedness. "There," he says, "there'll be crying and grinding of teeth." In this life that rarely comes to them from grief at the daily ruin of Mother Church's children, or from longing for the kingdom of heaven.[^79-4]

### Gildas turns to Paul, who says those who knew Yahweh didn't honour it

But let's see what Paul says first on such a matter. He's Christ's true disciple and the teacher of the gentiles, and every church leader should copy him, as he urges himself: "Take me as your model, as I take Christ."[^79-5] In his first letter he says: "Though they knew Yahweh, they didn't glorify it as god or give thanks. Their thoughts came to nothing, and their foolish hearts were darkened. They said they were wise and became fools."[^79-6] This may seem said of the gentiles. But see how well it fits this age's priests and their people.

Paul goes on: "They exchanged Yahweh's truth for a lie, and worshipped and served the creature rather than the Creator, blessed forever. So Yahweh abandoned them to shameful passions."[^79-7] He says what came of it: "Since they didn't see fit to acknowledge Yahweh, it gave them up to a worthless mind, to do what's wrong. They're filled with every wickedness, malice, lust, sexual sin, greed and spite. They're full of envy and murder," of their people's souls, that is, "rivalry, cunning and ill will. They're gossips who spread lies about others, hated by Yahweh, insulting, proud, boastful, inventors of evil, disobedient to parents, senseless, disorderly, without mercy or love. They knew Yahweh's justice, yet didn't understand that those who do such things deserve death."[^79-8]

### Gildas asks which of these priests is free of the sins Paul lists

Which of those named above has truly been free of all these? Even if one were, he might be caught by the sense that follows, where Paul says: "not only those who do them, but also those who approve of those who do them." So none of them is free of this evil.[^79-9]

<a id="page-80"></a>[\[Mommsen 80\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-80) Further down: "By your hard heart that won't repent, you're storing up anger for yourself on the day of fury, when Yahweh's just judgement is revealed. It'll pay each one back for what they've done."[^80-1] Paul warns them too: "Yahweh shows no favoritism. All who sinned outside the law will also perish apart from it. Those who did wrong within it must answer to it. Yahweh doesn't count people righteous for hearing it read. It'll justify those who obey."[^80-2]

What severity, then, bears down on those who don't do what must be done or avoid what's forbidden? They even flee the reading of Yahweh's words, heard ever so faintly, as if from the fiercest snake.

### Paul asks who can separate us from Christ's love, and calls for clean lives

But let's go on to what follows. "So what can we say?" he says. "Should we stay in sin, so grace may grow? Not at all. How can we who died to it still live in it?"[^80-3] Later in the letter: "Who'll separate us from Christ's love?" he says. "Trouble, or hardship, or persecution, or hunger, or nakedness, or danger, or the sword?"[^80-4] Which of you, I ask, will feel this deep in his heart? You don't labour for faith at all. You suffer much to do wrong and offend Christ.

Or take what Paul tells the Romans later: "The night is far gone, and the day is near. So let's throw off the works of darkness and put on the armour of light. Let's live decently, as in the daytime. Let's have no wild parties or drunkenness, no sleeping around or indecency, no quarreling or envy. Put on Jesus Christ, and make no provision for the flesh and its desires."[^80-5]

### Paul warns builders on Christ's foundation and tells the church to shun sinners

Again, in the first letter to the Corinthians: "Like a wise master builder, I laid a foundation, and another builds on it. But let each one watch how he goes about it. No one can lay any base but the one that's there, which is Jesus Christ. Anyone may put up gold, silver, precious stones, wood, hay or straw on it. Each one's work will be shown for what it is. The day of Yahweh will show it, because fire will reveal it. The flames will test what sort of work each one has done. If a man's building stands, he'll get his reward. If his work burns, he'll suffer loss.[^80-6]

"Don't you know that you're Yahweh's temple, and Yahweh's spirit lives in you? If anyone violates it, Yahweh will destroy him."[^80-7]

Paul tells the Corinthians: "If any of you thinks he's wise in this world, let him become a fool, to gain real understanding. The wisdom of this age is foolishness to Yahweh."[^80-8] Later in the same letter he says: "Your boasting isn't good. Don't you know a little yeast spoils the whole batch? So clean out the old, so you can be a new dough."[^80-9] How will it be cleaned out, that is, sin, which they pile higher day after day with all their might?

Paul goes on: "I wrote to you in a letter not to mix with the sexually immoral. I didn't mean the people of this world who live that way at all, or the greedy, or robbers, or idol worshipers. You'd have had to leave this life altogether. But now I'm writing to you not to keep company with anyone who's called a brother and sleeps around, or is grasping, or worships false gods, or spreads lies about others, or is a drunk, or a swindler. Don't even eat with such a man."[^80-10]

<a id="page-81"></a>[\[Mommsen 81\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-81) But a thief never condemns another for theft or robbery. He'd rather wish him well, guard him and love him, as a partner in his crime.

### Paul warns of false apostles who disguise themselves as Christ's

Then Paul says, in the second letter to the Corinthians: "So since we have this ministry, as we've received mercy, let's not lose heart. Let's renounce the hidden things of shame, not walking in cunning or corrupting Yahweh's word," by bad example, that is, and by flattery.[^81-1] Later on he says this of wicked teachers: "Such men are false apostles, dishonest workers, passing themselves off as Christ's. No wonder, since Satan himself disguises himself as an angel of light. So it's no great thing if his servants pose as ministers of justice. Their end will match what they do."[^81-2]

### Paul tells the Ephesians not to live like the heathen or get drunk

Note too what Paul says to the Ephesians. Don't you know you're held guilty on this count in some way? "So I tell you this," he says, "and swear in Yahweh: don't live any longer as the gentiles do, in the emptiness of their minds. Their understanding is darkened. They're cut off from Yahweh's way by the ignorance in them, because their hearts are blind. In despair, they've given themselves up to lust, to practice every kind of filth and greed."[^81-3] Which of you has willingly done what follows? "So don't be foolish, but understand what Yahweh's will is. Don't get drunk on wine, which leads to excess. Be filled with the Holy Spirit."[^81-4]

### Paul reminds the Thessalonians he never flattered them or sought gain

There's also what Paul says to the Thessalonians: "We never came to you with flattering words, as you know, or as a cover for greed. We didn't seek glory from people, from you or from others, though we could have stood on our honour as Christ's apostles. We became like little children among you, like a nurse caring for her own. Longing for you so much, we were eager to share with you not just the gospel but our own lives."[^81-5] If you keep this apostle's love in everything, then be sure that you sit lawfully in his chair.

Or take what Paul tells the Thessalonians later: "You know the commands I gave you. This is Yahweh's will, your holiness: that you keep away from sexual sin. Each of you should learn to keep his own body pure and honourable, not in the passion of desire like the gentiles who don't know Yahweh. No one should overstep or cheat a brother in business, because Yahweh avenges all these things. Yahweh didn't call us to filth but to holiness. So whoever rejects this isn't refusing a man but Yahweh."[^81-6]

Which of you has kept carefully and watchfully what follows? "So put to death the parts of you that are earthly: sexual sin, impurity, lust and evil desire. Because of these, Yahweh's anger falls on the children of unbelief."[^81-7] You see, then, which wrongs stir its fury most.

### Paul warns of hard times, when men will love themselves and money

So hear what the same holy apostle Paul predicted, by the spirit of prophecy, about you and men like you. He wrote plainly to Timothy: "You can be sure that in the last days dangerous times will come. People will love themselves. They'll be greedy, conceited, proud, blasphemers, disobedient to parents, ungrateful, wicked, cold, without self-control, cruel, unkind, traitors, reckless, swollen with pride. They'll love pleasure more than Yahweh. They'll look religious but deny its power."[^81-8]

<a id="page-82"></a>[\[Mommsen 82\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-82) "Keep away from them." David says so too, in the Psalms: "I've hated the gathering of the wicked, and I won't sit with the godless."[^82-1] A while later Paul says what we see sprouting in our own time: "always learning, and never coming to know the truth. As Jannes and Jambres opposed Moses, so these men stand against what's true. They're corrupt in mind and worthless in faith. But they won't get far, because their foolishness will be plain to everyone, as those two men's was."[^82-2]

### Paul tells Titus and Timothy how a priest should live and teach

Paul shows plainly how priests should carry out their duty, writing this to Titus: "Make yourself an example of good works in teaching, in integrity, in seriousness. Keep your speech sound and above criticism, so an opponent may be ashamed, having nothing bad to say of us."[^82-3] Again, to Timothy: "Work hard like a good soldier of Christ Jesus. No one serving Yahweh gets tangled in worldly business, so he can please the one who enlisted him. An athlete isn't crowned unless he competes by the rules."[^82-4] That's how he urges the good on.

What he goes on to say is a warning against bad men. That's how you look to anyone with sense. "Whoever teaches otherwise is proud and knows nothing. He doesn't agree with the sound teaching of Jesus Christ, or the doctrine that fits the faith. He's sick with a craving for disputes and quarrels about words. From these come envy, rivalry, slander, evil suspicions and the clashes of men corrupt in mind and robbed of the truth, who think religion is a way to make money."[^82-5]

### Gildas turns to the readings said when priests are ordained, first Peter's

But why use more scattered witnesses, and toss on the waves of varied meanings in the poor little boat of my talent? At last I've thought it necessary to turn back to the readings rightly taken from nearly the whole of holy Scripture for this. They're read not just to be recited. They support the blessing that consecrates the hands of priests and ministers. They teach them for good not to drop the commands faithfully kept in them, and so fall short of a priest's dignity. That'll make it plainer to all that eternal torments lie ahead for those who don't carry out these teachings and rules in what they do, as far as they can. They aren't priests or Yahweh's ministers.[^82-6]

So let's hear what Peter, the first of the apostles, marked down on this. "Blessed be the god and father of Jesus Christ," he says. "In its great mercy it's given us new birth into the hope of eternal life, through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead. Ours is an inheritance that can't perish, wither or be spoiled, kept in heaven for you, who are guarded by Yahweh's power."[^82-7] Why then do you so foolishly dishonour such a legacy? It doesn't fall away like earthly things. It never fades and lasts forever.

Peter goes on: "So get your minds ready for action. Be sober, and set your hope fully on the grace that's offered you when Jesus Christ is revealed."[^82-8] Search the depths of your hearts. Are you clear-headed? Do you keep the gift of priesthood whole, to be tested at Jesus's coming?

<a id="page-83"></a>[\[Mommsen 83\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-83) Again Peter says: "As children of blessing, don't shape yourselves to the desires you had before in your ignorance. As the one who called you is pure, be the same in all your conduct. That's why it's written: Be holy, because I am."[^83-1] Which of you, I ask, has followed holiness with all his mind's fire, hurrying eagerly to fulfill this as far as he can?

But let's see what the second reading, again from Peter, holds. "Dear friends," he says, "purify your souls by obeying the faith through the Spirit, in love and brotherhood. Love one another from a true heart, and keep at it. You've been born again, not from seed that perishes, but from one that can't, by the word of the living Yahweh, which lasts forever."[^83-2]

### Gildas says they heard Peter's words at their ordination and ignored them

Peter gave these commands, and they were read on the day you were ordained, so you'd keep them unbroken. You haven't carried them out in judgement at all. You haven't even thought much about them or understood them. Peter's letter goes on: "So put away all malice and all trickery, pretence, envy and slander. Like newborn babies, crave the pure milk of reason, so you can grow by it to salvation, because Yahweh is kind."[^83-3] Think back whether you've heard these words with deaf ears too, and trample on them often.

Peter adds: "You're a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people adopted, so you can proclaim the powers of the one who called you out of darkness into his wonderful light."[^83-4] Through you, Yahweh's mighty works don't just go untold. Your vile examples make every nonbeliever despise them.

You may have heard on that same day the reading from the Acts of the Apostles. Peter stood up among the disciples and said: "Brothers, the Scripture had to be fulfilled, which the Holy Spirit predicted through David's mouth about Judas."[^83-5] Peter said of Judas: "This man bought a field with the reward of wickedness."[^83-6] You heard this with a careless, or rather a dull, heart, as if it weren't read about you. Which of you, I ask, doesn't seek land with the wages of sin?

Judas robbed the purse. You plunder the Church's offerings and the souls of her children. He went to the Jews to sell Jesus. You go to tyrants and your father the devil, to despise Christ. He sold the Saviour of all for thirty pieces of silver. You'd do it for one small coin.[^83-7]

### Gildas sets Matthias against these priests, who follow Judas

What more? Matthias's example is put in front of you, to your shame. He took his lot among the holy apostles by Christ's choice and judgement, not by his own will.[^83-8] You've gone blind and can't see it. See how far you fall short of his merits, when you've fallen willingly into the love and the ways of Judas the traitor. It's plain, then, that anyone who knowingly calls you priests from the heart is no outstanding Christian.

I'll say plainly what I think. The criticism could be gentler. But what good is it only to touch a wound with the hand, or rub it with ointment? This one is swollen and stinking. It needs the burning iron and the open remedy of fire. That's if it can be healed at all, when the sick man doesn't look for a cure and keeps moving farther from the doctor.

O enemies of Yahweh, and not priests! Old hands at evil, and not bishops! Traitors, and not successors of the holy apostles, nor ministers of Christ! You heard the sound of the words of the second reading, from Paul. But you kept neither its warnings nor its power.

<a id="page-84"></a>[\[Mommsen 84\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-84) That same day you stood at the altar like idols, which don't see or hear, while he thundered at you, then and every day. "Brothers," he said, "the saying is trustworthy," and deserves full acceptance.[^84-1] He called it reliable and worthy. You brushed it off as false and unworthy. "If anyone wants to be a bishop, he desires a good work."[^84-2] You crave the office out of greed, not for spiritual progress, and lack the good work that fits it. "So such a man must be above criticism." Here tears are needed more than words. It's as if Paul had said he must be the most blameless of all.[^84-3]

"The husband of one wife." This, too, is ignored among us, as if no one heard it, or as if it said "the man of many wives." Next on the list: "Sober, sensible." Which of you has even wished for this in himself? "Hospitable." If that happens by chance, it's done to win popular favour, not to keep the command, and it does no good, as Jesus our Saviour says: "Truly I tell you, they've received their reward."[^84-4]

"Respectable, not a drinker, not violent, but gentle, not argumentative, not greedy."[^84-5] What a deadly reversal! What a horrible trampling of heaven's commands! Don't you take up the weapons of your acts and words tirelessly to fight these, or rather bury them? To keep and strengthen them, you should have suffered punishment willingly, and laid down your life if need be.

### Gildas sets Paul's rules for bishops and deacons against the priests of Britain

But let's see what follows. "He must manage his own household well," he says, "and keep his children obedient, with all chastity."[^84-6] So a father's purity is incomplete if his sons' isn't added to it. But what will it be where neither man is seen to be chaste, the boy spoiled by a bad father's example? "If a man doesn't know how to manage his own household, how will he take care of Yahweh's church?"[^84-7] These are words that plain results prove beyond doubt. "The deacons too must be modest, not two-faced, not given to much wine, not greedy for shameful gain, holding the mystery of the faith in a pure conscience. Let them be tested first, and then serve if they're found blameless."[^84-8]

I shudder to linger on these long. I can say one thing truly: all of them are turned into their opposites. Clergy are taken into the sacred ministry who are shameless, two-faced, drunk and greedy for dirty money. I say it with pain at heart. They hold the faith, or to speak more truly, unbelief, in an impure conscience. They aren't tested in good works but known beforehand for bad ones. They serve with countless crimes to their name.

You also heard on that day how Jesus asked who the disciples thought he was. It would have been far more right to drag you that day to prison or the torture rack than to the priesthood. Peter answered: "You're the Christ, the son of the living Yahweh." For this confession Jesus said to him: "Blessed are you, Simon Bar Jonah, because flesh and blood didn't reveal this to you, but my Father in heaven."[^84-9] So Peter, taught by Yahweh the Father, confesses Christ rightly. But you, schooled by your father the devil, unjustly deny the Saviour by your evil acts.

To the true priest it's said: "You're Peter, and on this rock I'll build my church."[^84-10] But you're like "the foolish man who built his house on sand."[^84-11] Note that Yahweh doesn't work with fools who put up a home on loose, shifting ground. As it says: "They made kings for themselves, but not through me."[^84-12] What follows says the same: "and the gates of hell won't prevail against it," where they mean sins.[^84-13]

<a id="page-85"></a>[\[Mommsen 85\]](../de-excidio-britanniae-latin.md#page-85) What's said of your own deadly building? "The rivers came and the winds blew and beat against that house. It fell, and its fall was great."[^85-1] Jesus says to Peter and his successors: "I'll give you the keys of the kingdom of heaven."[^85-2] But to you: "I never knew you. Get away from me, you who do evil,"[^85-3] so you'll go with the goats, set apart on the left, into the eternal fire.[^85-4] Again, every holy priest is promised: "Whatever you untie on earth will be untied in heaven too. Anything you bind stays bound there as well."[^85-5]

But how will you release anything so it's free in the sky as well? You're cut off from above for your crimes and chained by the ropes of your monstrous sins. As Solomon says too: "Each one is tied up in the cords of his own wrongdoing."[^85-6] Then how will you bind anything on earth so it's held fast above, except yourselves? You're so caught in your crimes in this world that you'll never rise to heaven. Unless you turn to Yahweh in this life, you'll fall into the cursed dungeons of hell.

### A priest answers for souls lost by his fault, and Gildas calls for repentance

No priest should congratulate himself on a clean body and conscience alone. If any of those in his charge are lost through his ignorance, laziness or flattery, they'll be required from his hands on the day of judgement, as from a murderer of souls. A death dealt by a holy man is no sweeter than one from a bad one. Otherwise Paul wouldn't have said, leaving it to those after him like a father's bequest: "I'm clean of everyone's blood. I didn't shrink from telling you all Yahweh's mystery."[^85-7]

You're drunk with long habit and endless sin. The waves of piled-up crimes keep crashing over you and tossing you. So seek with all your strength the one plank of repentance, as after a shipwreck, to escape on it to the land of the living. Then Yahweh's fury might be lifted from you, since it says in its kindness: "I don't want the sinner's death, but that he turn and live."[^85-8]

May almighty Yahweh itself, Father, Son and Holy Spirit, the god of all comfort and mercy, keep the very few good shepherds from all evil. Let it defeat our common enemy and make them citizens of the heavenly city of Jerusalem, the gathering of all the saints. Honour and glory be its forever and ever. Amen.
