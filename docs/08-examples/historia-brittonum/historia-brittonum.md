# The History of the Britons

*Historia Brittonum*, translated by [Ryan Allen](historia-brittonum-how-it-was-made.md#ryan-allen)

**Read in:** [UK English](uk/historia-brittonum-uk.md)

## About this translation

**Written:** 829–830 CE

The *History of the Britons* tells Britain's story from its first settlers to the wars with the Saxons, and it's the first book to name Arthur, as the leader of the Britons in twelve battles. A Welsh writer put it together in Latin in 829–830 CE, which the book itself counts as the fourth year of King Merfyn of Gwynedd, more than a hundred years before the *Welsh Annals* that follow it in its oldest copy. Later copies credit it to a monk named Nennius, but scholars doubt it, so the writer's name is uncertain. Historians don't read it as fact: much of the book is legend, a founding story made up to give the Britons a glorious ancestry, tracing them back to Troy the way Rome traced itself to Aeneas. This translation follows that copy, London, British Library, Harley MS 3859, made about 1100, at the end of the eleventh century or the start of the twelfth, as Theodor Mommsen printed it in 1898, and leaves out the prologue naming Nennius and the Irish version Mommsen prints beside the text, which belong to later copies.

## Part 1: The ages of the world are counted from Adam to the time of writing

### The years are counted from the creation to King David

<a id="page-145"></a>[\[Mommsen 145\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-145) From the beginning of the world to the Flood was two thousand two hundred forty-two years.[^145-1] From the Flood to Abraham was nine hundred forty-two, from Abraham to Moses six hundred forty, and from Moses to David five hundred.

### The years run from David to the exile in Babylon

From David to Nebuchadnezzar was five hundred sixty-nine years, and from Adam to the exile in Babylon four thousand eight hundred seventy-nine.

### The years run from the exile in Babylon to the Crucifixion

From then to Christ was five hundred sixty-six years, and from Adam to Christ's Passion five thousand two hundred twenty-eight.

### The years since the Crucifixion and since Christ's birth are counted

<a id="page-146"></a>[\[Mommsen 146\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-146) Seven hundred ninety-six years have passed from the Passion to now, and eight hundred thirty-one from the Incarnation.[^146-1]

### The first three ages run from Adam to David

So the first age of the world ran from Adam to Noah, the second from Noah to Abraham, and the third from Abraham to David.

### The last three ages run from David to Judgment Day

The fourth lasted from David to Daniel, the fifth from Daniel to John the Baptist, and the sixth goes from John to the Judgment. Then our Lord Jesus Christ will come to judge the living and the dead, and the world by fire.[^146-2]

## Part 2: Britain is described, and Brutus, the Picts and the Irish come to settle it

### Britain is named for Brutus, and four peoples live in it

<a id="page-147"></a>[\[Mommsen 147\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-147) The island of Britain is named after a certain Brutus, a Roman consul.[^147-1] It stretches from the southwest toward the north and west,[^147-2] eight hundred miles long and two hundred wide. It has twenty-eight cities and countless headlands, with forts beyond counting built of stone and brick. Four peoples live in it: the Irish, the Picts, the Saxons and the Britons.[^147-3]

### Britain has three islands, Wight, Man and Orkney

<a id="page-148"></a>[\[Mommsen 148\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-148) It has three large islands. One faces Brittany and is called the Isle of Wight. The second lies in the middle of the sea between Ireland and Britain, and its name is Eubonia, that is, Man. The third is at the farthest edge of the British world, beyond the Picts, and is called Orkney. So there's an old saying, when people speak of judges or kings: "He judged Britain and its three islands."[^148-1]

### Britain's rivers run every way, and the Britons once filled the land

It has many rivers, flowing out to every side, east, west, south and north. Two are finer than the rest, the Thames and the Severn, like Britain's two arms, and boats once carried its wealth along them for trade. The Britons once filled the land from one sea to the other and ruled it.

### Brutus is born under a prophecy and is driven from Italy to Britain

<a id="page-149"></a>[\[Mommsen 149\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-149) If anyone wants to know when this island was first lived in after the Flood, I've found two accounts of it.

<a id="page-150"></a>[\[Mommsen 150\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-150) The Romans' annals tell it this way. After the Trojan War, Aeneas came to Italy with his son Ascanius. He defeated Turnus and married Lavinia, the daughter of Latinus, whose line ran back through Faunus and Picus to Saturn. When Latinus died, Aeneas held the kingdom of the Romans, or the Latins. He founded Alba, and later his new wife gave him a son named Silvius. Silvius took a bride of his own, and she became pregnant. Aeneas was told his daughter-in-law was expecting, and he had Ascanius send his seer to examine her and find out whether she carried a boy or a girl.[^150-1]

The man examined her and came back. Ascanius killed him for his prophecy: the woman, he'd said, was carrying a son who'd bring death, since he'd kill his father and his mother and be hated by everyone. So it happened. She died giving birth, and the child was raised and named Brutus.

<a id="page-151"></a>[\[Mommsen 151\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-151)

<a id="page-152"></a>[\[Mommsen 152\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-152) Long afterward, as the seer's prophecy said, Brutus was playing with the others and shot his father dead with an arrow, by accident, not on purpose. He was driven out of Italy.[^152-1] He came to the islands of the Tyrrhenian Sea, and the Greeks drove him out over the death of Turnus, the king Aeneas had killed. He went on as far as Gaul and founded the city of Tours there, which is called Turnis.[^152-2]

<a id="page-153"></a>[\[Mommsen 153\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-153) After that he reached this island, which took its name from his, Britain, filled it with his people and settled there. People have lived in Britain from that day to this.

### Brutus rules Britain while Eli judges Israel

Aeneas reigned over the Latins for three years, Ascanius for thirty-seven, then Silvius, Aeneas's son, for twelve, and Postumus for thirty-nine. The kings of Alba are called Silvii after Postumus, and his brother was Brutus.[^153-1] While Brutus ruled Britain, the priest Eli was judging Israel, and foreigners held the Ark of the Covenant.[^153-2] At the same time Postumus reigned among the Latins.

### The Picts take Orkney and settle the north of Britain

<a id="page-154"></a>[\[Mommsen 154\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-154) Many years later, eight hundred at the least, the Picts came and occupied the islands called Orkney. From there they laid waste to large parts of the country and took them over, on the left-hand side of Britain, the north.[^154-1] They're still there, holding a third of Britain, to this day.

### The Irish come from Spain, and their fleet attacks a glass tower

Last of all, the Irish came from Spain to Ireland. The first to come was Partholón, with a thousand men and women, and they grew to four thousand.[^154-2] Then a plague struck them, and within a week they'd all died. Not one of them was left. The second to reach Ireland was Nemed, a son of Agnoman.

<a id="page-155"></a>[\[Mommsen 155\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-155) He's said to have sailed the sea for a year and a half before his fleet was wrecked and he put in at a harbor in Ireland. He lived there many years, then set out again with his people and went back to Spain.

Later three sons of a Spanish soldier came with thirty ships, and thirty wives in each, and stayed a year.[^155-1] Then they saw a glass tower in the middle of the sea, with people on top. They tried to speak to them, but never got an answer.

Within twelve months they hurried to attack it with all their fleet and all their women, except for one ship, which was wrecked, carrying thirty couples. The rest sailed on to storm it, and while everyone was ashore on the beach around the tower, the sea rose over them and they drowned. Not one of them escaped. From the people of that one boat, left behind because it broke up, all of Ireland has been filled to this day.

<a id="page-156"></a>[\[Mommsen 156\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-156) After that, more came from Spain, a few at a time, and won many regions.

### Damhoctor, Istoreth, Builc and Liethan's sons settle parts of Britain

The last to come was Damhoctor, who settled in Britain with all his people, and they're there to this day. Istoreth son of Istorinus took Dál Riata with his people, and Builc the island of Eubonia and others around it with his. Liethan's descendants made their home in Dyfed and nearby lands, such as Gower and Kidwelly.[^156-1] They held them until Cunedda and his sons drove them out of every part of Britain.

### A Scythian noble driven from Egypt leads his people to Spain and Ireland

If anyone wants to know when Ireland lay empty, with no one living there, this is what the most learned of the Irish told me. When the children of Israel came through the Red Sea, the Egyptians chased them and were drowned, as we read in the Law.[^156-2]

<a id="page-157"></a>[\[Mommsen 157\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-157) A noble from Scythia was living among the Egyptians with a large household, driven out of his own kingdom, and he was there when they went down in the sea. But he didn't go out to hunt God's people. The ones who'd survived met and decided to drive him out, so he wouldn't besiege their land and take it, since their strongest men had gone under in the Red Sea. So he was driven out.

He wandered through Africa for forty-two years. His people came to the altars of the Philistines by the Lake of Salt, and passed between Rusicada and the mountains of Azaria. They followed the river Malva and crossed Mauretania to the Pillars of Hercules. Then they sailed the Tyrrhenian Sea and reached Spain. There they lived for many years, grew and multiplied into a great people. A thousand and two years after the Egyptians were lost in the Red Sea, they came to Ireland, and to the lands of Dál Riata, in the time of Brutus at Rome.

<a id="page-158"></a>[\[Mommsen 158\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-158) Rome's consuls began with this Brutus, and later the tribunes of the people and the dictators.[^158-1] Then for four hundred forty-seven years the Republic was governed that way again, after the rule of kings had done it harm.

The Britons came to Britain in the third age, and the Irish took Ireland in the fourth. The Irish in the west and the Picts from the north fought the Britons together, as one, without a break, since the Britons went unarmed.[^158-2] Long afterward the Romans won rule over the whole world.

### The years are counted from the Saxons' coming to King Merfyn's fourth year

From the Saxons' first year in Britain to King Merfyn's fourth, four hundred twenty-nine years are counted.[^158-3] From the Lord's birth to Patrick's coming to the Irish is four hundred five years.

<a id="page-159"></a>[\[Mommsen 159\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-159) From Patrick's death to Saint Brigid's is sixty years, and Columba was born four years before Brigid died. The count begins. From the Incarnation to Patrick's arrival in Ireland were twenty-three of the nineteen-year cycles, four hundred thirty-eight years in all. From then to the current one are twenty-two more, four hundred twenty-one years, and we're now two years into its ogdoad.[^159-1]

### A second account traces Brutus back through Alanus to Adam

I found another account of this Brutus in the old books of our ancestors. After the Flood Noah's three sons divided the world into three parts: Shem spread his borders in Asia, Ham in Africa and Japheth in Europe.

<a id="page-160"></a>[\[Mommsen 160\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-160) The first man of Japheth's line to come to Europe was Alanus, and his three children came with him, Hessitio, Armenon and Negue. Hessitio had four, Francus, Romanus, Brutus and Albanus. Armenon had five, Gothus, Valagothus, Gebidus, Burgundus and Longobardus. Negue had three, Vandalus, Saxo and Boguarus.[^160-1] From Hessitio descended four peoples: the Franks, the Latins, the Albans and the Britons. From Armenon came five, the Goths, the Walagoths, the Gepids, the Burgundians and the Lombards. From Negue there were four, the Bavarians, the Vandals, the Saxons and the Thuringians. These peoples spread out over all of Europe.

<a id="page-161"></a>[\[Mommsen 161\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-161) Alanus, they say, was born to Fetebir, and his line runs back, father to father, through Ougomun, Thoi, Boib, Simeon, Mair, Ethach, Aurthach, Ecthet, Oth, Abir, Ra, Ezra, Izrau, Baath, Iobaath, Iovan, Japheth, Noah, Lamech, Methuselah, Enoch, Jared, Mahalalel, Cainan, Enos and Seth to Adam, son of the living God. I learned this from what the old men handed down.

### The Britons come from Brutus, and Japheth's seven sons people the world

The first people to live in Britain were the Britons, named for Brutus. Brutus's father was Hessitio, and Hessitio's was Alanus. Alanus's mother was Rea Silvia, daughter of Numa Pompilius, whose father was Ascanius, and Ascanius's line ran back through Aeneas, Anchises, Tros, Dardanus, Flise and Iuvan to Japheth.[^161-1] Japheth had seven sons. The first, Gomer, was the father of the Gauls; the second, Magog, of the Scythians and the Goths; the third, Madai, of the Medes; the fourth, Iuvan, of the Greeks; the fifth, Tubal, of the Iberians, the Spanish and the Italians; the sixth, Mosoch, of the Cappadocians; and the seventh, Tiras, of the Thracians. These are the sons of Japheth, whose father was Noah, and Noah's was Lamech.

<a id="page-162"></a>[\[Mommsen 162\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-162) Now let me go back to where I left off.

## Part 3: The Romans rule Britain from Julius Caesar to Maximus, and then leave

### Britain spurns Rome's envoys, and Julius Caesar sails against it

Once the Romans ruled the whole world, they sent envoys to the Britons to take hostages and tribute from them, as they took them from every land and island.[^162-1] But the Britons, high-handed and proud, rejected the Romans' message. Then Julius Caesar, the first to win sole power and keep it, was furious. He came to Britain with sixty ships and put in at the mouth of the Thames, and they were wrecked there while he fought Dolobellus, proconsul to Bellinus, king of the Britons. Bellinus was the son of Minocannus, who'd taken every island of the Tyrrhenian Sea.[^162-2] Caesar went home without a victory, his soldiers cut down and his fleet broken.

### Caesar comes back with three hundred ships and fights at the Thames

Three years later he came again with a great army and three hundred ships, and reached the mouth of the river called the Thames.

<a id="page-163"></a>[\[Mommsen 163\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-163) They joined battle there. Many of his horses and men fell, because the proconsul had planted iron stakes and the seed of war, that is, Cetilou, in the river's fords.[^163-1] This hidden trick put the Roman soldiers in great danger, and they left without a peace that time. The third battle was fought near a place called Trinovantum, and Julius took rule over the British people forty-seven years before Christ's birth, five thousand two hundred fifteen years from the beginning of the world.

So Julius was the first to reach Britain and hold its kingdom and people. In his honor the Romans decreed that the month of Quintilis should be called July.[^163-2]

<a id="page-164"></a>[\[Mommsen 164\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-164) On the Ides of March Gaius Julius Caesar was killed in the senate house, and Octavian Augustus became sole ruler of the whole world. He alone took tribute from Britain, as Virgil says:

"The Britons woven into it raise the purple curtain."[^164-1]

### Claudius comes to Britain and makes Orkney pay tribute

The second to come after him was the emperor Claudius, who ruled in Britain forty-eight years after Christ's coming. He fought a great and bloody war, and lost soldiers doing it, but he won in Britain. Afterward he went by ship to the islands of Orkney, conquered them and made them pay tribute. In his time Britain stopped paying it to the Romans, though it was paid to British emperors. He reigned thirteen years and eight months. His tomb is shown at Mainz, among the Lombards: he died there on his way to Rome.[^164-2]

### King Lucius and all the Britons' kings are baptized

A hundred sixty-seven years after Christ's coming, Lucius, who ruled the Britons, was baptized with all the lesser kings of the whole British people, after the Roman emperor and Eucharistus, the Roman pope, sent envoys to him.[^164-3]

### Severus builds a wall across Britain against the Picts and the Irish

<a id="page-165"></a>[\[Mommsen 165\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-165) The third was Severus, who crossed over to the Britons. He built a wall and an earthwork across Britain from one sea to the other, a hundred thirty-two miles,[^165-1] to make the lands he'd won back safer from the raids of barbarians. In the Britons' language it's called Guaul. He set it between the Britons and the Picts and Irish, because the Irish from the west and the Picts from the north fought the Britons together, as one, since they were at peace with each other. Not long after, Severus died in Britain.

### Carausius makes himself emperor in Britain to avenge Severus

The fourth was the emperor Carausius, a usurper, who came to Britain and ruled it by force.[^165-2] He rose up over the killing of Severus. With all the leaders of the Roman people who were with him in Britain, he cut down all the lesser kings of the Britons and took harsh revenge on them for Severus. Then he made himself emperor in Britain.

### Constantine is buried at Cair Segeint, where he sowed three seeds

<a id="page-166"></a>[\[Mommsen 166\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-166) The fifth was Constantine, son of Constantine the Great, and he died in Britain. His tomb can be seen near the city called Cair Segeint, as the letters cut on its stone say.[^166-1] He sowed three seeds, of gold, silver and bronze, in its pavement, so that no poor person would ever live there. Its other name is Minmanton.

### Maximus rules Britain in Saint Martin's time

The sixth was the emperor Maximus, who reigned in Britain. From his time the consuls began, and they were never called Caesars after that. In his time Saint Martin became known for his miracles and signs, and spoke with him.[^166-2]

### Maximianus takes Britain's soldiers to Europe and settles them in Brittany

The seventh to reign in Britain was Maximianus. He left Britain with all the Britons' soldiers, killed Gratian, king of the Romans, and held power over all of Europe.

<a id="page-167"></a>[\[Mommsen 167\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-167) He wouldn't send the men who'd gone with him back to Britain, to their wives, their children and their lands. He gave them many regions instead, from the lake on top of Mount Jove to the city called Cant Guic and on to the western mound, that is, Cruc Ochidient.[^167-1] These are the Britons of Brittany, and they've never come back here to this day. Because of this, foreign peoples took Britain, and its citizens were driven out, until God comes to help them.

In our ancestors' old tradition there were seven Roman emperors in Britain; the Romans say nine. The eighth was another Severus, who went back and forth between Britain and Rome, and he died in Rome. The ninth was Constantius. He reigned sixteen years in Britain, and his reign ended with his death there, in its sixteenth year.

### The Britons stop paying Rome after four hundred and nine years

Up to this point the Romans had ruled the Britons for four hundred nine years. But the Britons threw off Roman rule, paying them no tribute and refusing their kings.

<a id="page-168"></a>[\[Mommsen 168\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-168) The Romans didn't dare come to Britain to govern anymore, because the Britons had killed their leaders.

### Maximus is made emperor in Britain and crosses to Gaul against Gratian

I have to speak of the usurper Maximianus again.[^168-1] Gratian reigned six years with his brother Valentinian, and Ambrose, bishop of Milan, stood out for his Catholic teaching. Valentinian ruled with Theodosius for eight years. A gathering of three hundred eighteen church fathers at Constantinople condemned every heresy.[^168-2] Jerome, then a priest at Bethlehem, was known everywhere. While Gratian held the empire, Maximus was made emperor in Britain by an army mutiny. As soon as he crossed into Gaul, Gratian was beaten at Paris through the treachery of his own general, Merobaudes.[^168-3] Gratian fled, but was caught at Lyon and killed. Maximus made his son Victor his partner in rule. Saint Martin, bishop of Tours, was famous for great miracles.

<a id="page-169"></a>[\[Mommsen 169\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-169) A long time later, in the consulship of Valentinian and Theodosius, Maximus was stripped of his royal clothes at the third milestone from Aquileia, brought to trial and sentenced to death. Victor, his son, died in Gaul the same year at the hands of Count Arbogast.

### The Britons turn on Rome's governors, then beg Rome for help

Three times the Britons killed Roman leaders. But when the barbarian peoples, the Irish and the Picts, pressed them hard, they begged the Romans for help. Their envoys went in great grief, with sand on their heads, and brought great gifts for the Roman consuls to make up for the crime of killing the leaders.

<a id="page-170"></a>[\[Mommsen 170\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-170) These were well received, and the Britons promised under oath to take the yoke of Roman law, however hard it was.

The Romans came to their aid with a huge force and set up emperors in Britain. Once one was in place with his commanders, the army went home to Rome. They did this by turns for three hundred forty-eight years. The Britons, under the weight of that rule, kept killing the Roman leaders and then asking for rescue. The Romans came back each time to govern, to help and to punish. They went home in great triumph every time, having stripped Britain of its gold, silver and bronze, its fine clothes and its honey.

## Part 4: Vortigern takes in Hengist's Saxons, and Germanus makes a poor man king

### Vortigern rules in fear, and Hengist and Horsa land with three ships

<a id="page-171"></a>[\[Mommsen 171\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-171) The Romans' leaders had been killed in the war with the Britons that I spoke of, and the usurper Maximus was dead.[^171-1] Once Roman rule in Britain was over, the Britons lived in fear for forty years. Vortigern ruled Britain, and all through his reign he was hard pressed: he feared the Picts and the Irish, an attack from Rome, and Ambrosius.[^171-2]

Meanwhile three ships came, driven from Germany into exile, carrying Horsa and Hengist. They were brothers, children of Wihtgils, and their line ran back, father to father, through Witta, Wecta, Woden, Frealaf, Fredulf, Finn and Fodepald to Geta, who was, they say, a son of God. He wasn't the God of gods, amen, the God of armies, but one of the idols they worshiped.[^171-3]

<a id="page-172"></a>[\[Mommsen 172\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-172) Vortigern took them in kindly and gave them the island called Thanet in their language and Ruoihm in the Britons'.[^172-1] He received the Saxons in the reign of Gratian, in his second consulship, with Equitius, three hundred forty-seven years after Christ's Passion.[^172-2]

### Germanus comes to Britain to preach, and a wicked king shuts him out

In Vortigern's time Saint Germanus came to preach in Britain.[^172-3] He became known among the Britons for his miracles; through him many were saved, and more were lost.[^172-4]

I've decided to write down some of what God did through him. Here's the first.

<a id="page-173"></a>[\[Mommsen 173\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-173) There was a wicked king named Benli, a cruel tyrant. Germanus wanted to go to him at once and preach to him. When he reached the town gate with his companions, the gatekeeper came out to greet them. They sent the man in to Benli, who gave them a harsh answer and swore: "If they wait till the end of the year, they'll never set foot inside my town."

<a id="page-174"></a>[\[Mommsen 174\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-174) While they waited for the man to come back with the tyrant's word, the day wore on toward evening, night was coming, and they didn't know where to go. Then one of the king's servants came out from the middle of the town, bowed to the holy man and told them everything the king had said. He invited them to his house and led them out to it, where he made them welcome.

He had no livestock of any kind but one cow. He killed her calf, cooked it and set it in front of them. Saint Germanus told them not to break any of its bones, and none were broken. The next morning it stood beside its mother, alive and unharmed.[^174-1]

### A sweating man is baptized at the king's gate and goes in to him

They got up early again to try to greet the tyrant. While they were praying and waiting by the gate of the fort, a man came running, sweat streaming off him from head to foot. He bowed to them, and Saint Germanus said, "Do you believe in the Holy Trinity?"

"I do," he answered, and was baptized. Germanus kissed him and said, "Go in peace. You'll die within the hour, and God's angels are waiting for you in the air, to take you with them to the God you've believed in."

<a id="page-175"></a>[\[Mommsen 175\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-175) He went in happy. The king's officer held him and tied him up. He was taken to the tyrant and put to death, since that wicked man killed anyone who hadn't come to serve in the fort by sunrise. Germanus and his companions stayed all day by the town gate, and never got to greet him.

### Germanus tells the servant to bring his sons out of the fort tonight

As usual the servant came, and Saint Germanus told him, "Make sure not one of your people stays in the fort tonight." He went back in and brought out his sons, nine of them, and they all walked with him to the house where Germanus was staying. Saint Germanus told them to keep fasting, shut the doors and said, "Stay awake, and whatever happens over there, don't look. Pray without stopping, and cry out to your God."

<a id="page-176"></a>[\[Mommsen 176\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-176) A little while into the night, fire fell from heaven and burned the fort, which has never been rebuilt, and everyone who was with the tyrant. None of them has been seen to this day.

### Germanus baptizes the servant Cadell and blesses him and his sons

The next day the man who'd taken them in became a believer and was baptized with all his sons, and the whole district with them. His name was Cadell. Germanus blessed him. "Your line will never lack a king," he went on, "and you'll rule from today." This man is Cadell Ddyrnllwg.[^176-1]

So it happened, and what the prophet said came true: "He raises the needy from the dust and lifts the poor from the dung heap, to sit with princes and hold the throne of glory."[^176-2] As Saint Germanus had said, the servant became a king, and so did all his sons. Their descendants rule the whole land of Powys to this day.

### The Saxons settle on Thanet and ask Vortigern for the food he promised

Once the Saxons had settled on the island of Thanet, Vortigern said he'd give them food and clothing without fail.[^176-3] They were pleased, and in return swore to fight hard against his enemies. But once these barbarians had grown in number, the Britons couldn't keep them supplied.

<a id="page-177"></a>[\[Mommsen 177\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-177) When they demanded what they'd been promised, the Britons said, "We can't feed and clothe you. There are too many of you now. Go away; we don't need your help." The Saxons then made a plan with their leaders to break the peace.

### Hengist sends home for more warriors, and his daughter sails with them

Hengist was a learned man, cunning and shrewd. He'd sized up the king as weak and his people as unarmed. After talking it over with his men, he said to Vortigern, "We're few. If you like, we'll send home and invite soldiers from our country, so there are more of us to fight for you and your people."

Vortigern told them to go ahead. They sent envoys, who crossed the Tithic valley and came back with sixteen ships carrying picked warriors.[^177-1] Hengist's daughter sailed in one of them, a girl of great beauty.

<a id="page-178"></a>[\[Mommsen 178\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-178) When they landed, Hengist gave a feast for Vortigern, his soldiers and his interpreter, Ceretic. He had her serve them wine and beer. They got very drunk and ate their fill.

While they were drinking, Satan entered Vortigern's heart, and he fell in love with her. Through Ceretic he asked her father for her. "Whatever you want from me, you'll have," he said, "even half my kingdom."

<a id="page-179"></a>[\[Mommsen 179\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-179) Hengist talked it over with his elders, who'd come with him from the island of Oghgul, to decide what to ask for her.[^179-1] They all agreed on one thing: to demand the land called Canturguoralen in their language, and Kent in ours.[^179-2] Vortigern gave it to them, though Guoyrancgonus was ruling in Kent and didn't know his kingdom was being handed to the pagans, and he himself was being put secretly in their power. So the girl was given to Vortigern as his wife. He slept with her, and loved her deeply.

### Hengist has Vortigern send for Octa and Ebissa to fight in the north

Hengist said to Vortigern, "I'm your father and your adviser. Never ignore my advice, and you'll never need to fear being beaten by any man or any people, because my people are strong. I'll invite my son and his cousin, who are warriors, to make war on the Irish. Give them the lands in the north, by the wall called Guaul."

Vortigern told him to send for them, and he did: Octa and Ebissa came, with a fleet of forty.

<a id="page-180"></a>[\[Mommsen 180\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-180) Sailing around the Picts, they laid waste to Orkney, then landed and occupied many lands beyond the Frenessic Sea, as far as the Picts' border.[^180-1]

Hengist kept bringing more ships over, a few at a time, until they left the islands they'd come from empty of people. As his people grew in strength and numbers, they came to the city of the people of Kent.[^180-2]

### Vortigern marries his own daughter, and Germanus comes with the clergy to rebuke him

On top of all his other wrongs, Vortigern took his own daughter as his wife, and she had a son by him.[^180-3] When Saint Germanus learned of it, he went with all the Britons' clergy to rebuke him. While a great gathering of churchmen and lay people was meeting, the king told his daughter ahead of time to go out to it, put her son in Germanus's lap and say Germanus was the child's father. She did as she'd been told. Germanus took the child kindly. "I'll be a father to you," he said. "I won't let you go until I'm given a razor, shears and a comb. Then you're to take them to your real father."

<a id="page-181"></a>[\[Mommsen 181\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-181) The boy obeyed. He went to his grandfather, Vortigern, who was also his father by blood, and said, "You're my father. Shave my head and cut my hair."[^181-1] Vortigern said nothing, and wouldn't answer him. He got up in a rage and fled from Saint Germanus. Germanus and the whole gathering of the Britons condemned him with a curse.

## Part 5: Vortigern's fort keeps falling, and a fatherless boy shows him two dragons under it

### Vortigern's seers send him to build a fort at his kingdom's edge

Later the king called his seers to him, to ask them what to do.[^181-2] "Go to the farthest edge of your kingdom," they said, "and you'll find a strong fort to defend yourself in. The people you've taken in are jealous of you. They'll kill you by treachery, and after your death they'll take every land you loved, with all your people."

<a id="page-182"></a>[\[Mommsen 182\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-182) Then he set out with them to look for one. They traveled through many regions and provinces, and finding nothing, came at last to Gwynedd. Searching the mountains of Snowdonia, he finally found a place on one of them where a fort could rightly stand.[^182-1] "Build it here," his seers told him, "because it'll be safe from barbarian peoples forever."

He brought together craftsmen, that is, stonemasons, and collected wood and stone. When everything was ready, it all vanished in one night. Three times he had the materials gathered again, and each time they disappeared. He sent for the seers and asked them what caused this trouble and why it happened. They answered, "Unless you find a child with no father, and he's killed, and the ground is sprinkled with his blood, your fort will never be built."

### Vortigern's envoys search Britain for a boy with no father

On his seers' advice he sent envoys all over Britain, to see if any could be found.

<a id="page-183"></a>[\[Mommsen 183\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-183) They went through every province and many regions, and came to the plain of Elleti, in Glywysing, where boys were playing ball.[^183-1] Two of them were quarreling, and one said to the other, "You fatherless nobody, you'll never come to any good."

The envoys questioned the other children closely about him, then asked his mother whether he had a father. She said no. "I don't know how he was conceived in my womb," she said, "but I'm sure of one thing: I've never been with a man." She swore to them that he had none. They took him with them to King Vortigern and presented him to the king.

### The boy is brought to the king and questions his seers

The next day a meeting was held to put him to death.[^183-2] "Why have your men brought me to you?" he asked Vortigern.

"To kill you," came the answer, "and sprinkle your blood around this fort, so it can be built."

"Who told you to do that?"

"My seers."

"Have them called here to me."

<a id="page-184"></a>[\[Mommsen 184\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-184) They were sent for, and the boy put the question to them: "Who revealed to you that my blood must be sprinkled here, and that unless it is, nothing will ever be built? Let it be known who told you this about me."[^184-1]

Then he went on, "Now, king, I'll make it all clear to you and show you the whole truth. But I'll ask your seers: what's in the ground here? I'd like them to say what lies under it."

"We don't know," they said.

"I've found it out," he said. "There's a pool under the middle of it. Come and dig, and you'll find it."

They came and dug, and the ground fell in. The boy said to the seers, "Tell me, what's in the water?" They said nothing, and couldn't tell him.

"I'll tell you," he said. "There are two jars, and that's how you'll find them." They came and saw it was so.

He asked them, "And what's shut up inside?" Again they said nothing, and couldn't tell him.

"There's a tent between them," he declared. "Separate them, and you'll find it."

The king had them pulled apart, and it was found there, folded up, as the boy had said.

<a id="page-185"></a>[\[Mommsen 185\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-185) Once more he asked the seers, "What's in the middle of it? Tell me now." They had no idea.

He revealed it himself: "There are two worms in it, one white and one red. Spread it out." They opened it and found them asleep.[^185-1]

"Wait and watch what they'll do," said the boy. They began to try to drive each other out. One would set its shoulders against the other to push it back to the middle, and they did this three times. At first the red one seemed the weaker, but afterward it was stronger than the white and drove it out over the edge. Then one chased the other across the pool, and the tent vanished.

He asked the seers, "What does this strange sign mean, the one we've just seen?"

"We don't know," they said.

<a id="page-186"></a>[\[Mommsen 186\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-186) He answered, "This mystery has been revealed to me, and now I'll make it plain to you. The tent is a figure of your kingdom, and the pool of this world. The two worms are two dragons. The red one is yours. The white one belongs to the people who've occupied many peoples and lands in Britain, and they'll hold it almost from one coast to the other. Later our people will rise and bravely drive the English back across the sea. But you, leave this fort, since you can't build it. Travel around many regions to find a safe place for one, and I'll stay here."

The king asked the young man, "What's your name?"

"I'm called Ambrosius," he answered. That is, he was seen to be Emrys Wledig.[^186-1]

"What family do you come from?"

"My father is one of the consuls of the Roman people."[^186-2]

Vortigern gave him the fort, with all the kingdoms of the west of Britain, and went with his seers to the north. He came to Guunnessi and built a city there named after him, Cair Guorthigirn.[^186-3]

## Part 6: Vortimer fights the Saxons, and Hengist's men murder Vortigern's lords at a meeting

### Vortimer fights Hengist and Horsa, and the Saxons send overseas for more ships

<a id="page-187"></a>[\[Mommsen 187\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-187) Meanwhile Vortigern's son Vortimer fought fiercely against Hengist and Horsa and their people.[^187-1] He drove them back as far as the island of Thanet, and three times he shut them in there, besieged them, struck them, crushed them and terrified them. They sent envoys across the sea to Germany, calling for ships with a huge number of fighting men. After that they made war on the kings of our people. Sometimes they won and pushed their borders out; at other times they lost and were driven back.

### Vortimer fights four battles against the Saxons

Vortimer eagerly fought four battles against them. The first was on the river Derguentid.[^187-2] The second was at the ford called Episford in their language and Rithergabail in ours, and there Horsa fell, with Vortigern's son Catigern.[^187-3]

<a id="page-188"></a>[\[Mommsen 188\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-188) The third was in the open field by the Stone of the Inscription, on the shore of the Channel.[^188-1] The barbarians were beaten and Vortimer won. They fled to their ships and drowned, scrambling into them like women.

Not long after, Vortimer was dead. Before his death he told his household to put his tomb at the harbor the Saxons had left from, on the shore of the sea. "I'm trusting you with this," he said. "Though they hold a port elsewhere in Britain, and have lived there, they'll never stay in this land." But they ignored his order and didn't bury him in the place he'd told them to.

### The Saxons come back in force while Vortigern stays their friend

The barbarians came back in force, since Vortigern was their friend because of his wife.[^188-2] No one was bold enough to drive them off, because they didn't take Britain by their own strength, but because God chose it. Who can stand against God's will, or fight it? The Lord did as he wanted, and he governs every people.

<a id="page-189"></a>[\[Mommsen 189\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-189) After the death of King Vortimer, Vortigern's son, and Hengist's return with his hordes, the Saxons hatched a false plan: to trick Vortigern and his army. They sent envoys to ask for peace, so there'd be lasting friendship between them. Vortigern and his elders talked it over and weighed what to do. In the end they all agreed to it. They took the answer back, and afterward a meeting was arranged, at which the Britons and the Saxons would both come unarmed, to seal the bond.

### Hengist tells his men to hide knives in their shoes at the peace meeting

<a id="page-190"></a>[\[Mommsen 190\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-190) Hengist ordered every man of his household to put his knife under his foot, in the middle of his shoe. "When I call out to you and say, '*Eu Saxones eniminit saxas*,' pull your knives out, rush at them and stand firm against them.[^190-1] Don't kill their king, but keep hold of him for my daughter's sake, since I gave her to him as his wife: it's better for us that he's ransomed from our hands."

They arranged the meeting and came together. The Saxons talked in a friendly way while all the time scheming like foxes, and they sat man beside man, as companions. Hengist shouted as he'd said, and all three hundred of King Vortigern's elders had their throats cut. He alone was taken and chained, and to ransom his life he gave them many regions, that is, Essex and Sussex.

### Germanus preaches to Vortigern, who runs to the land named for him

Saint Germanus preached to Vortigern to turn to his Lord and leave his unlawful marriage.[^190-2][^190-3]

<a id="page-191"></a>[\[Mommsen 191\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-191) Vortigern fled in misery to the land that took its name from him, Gwrtheyrnion, to hide there with his wives.[^191-1] Saint Germanus followed him there with all the Britons' clergy. He stayed forty days and nights, standing on a rock to pray, on his feet without a break.

Again Vortigern withdrew in disgrace, to Vortigern's fort, which is in Dyfed by the river Teifi. As usual Saint Germanus followed him, and stayed there with all the clergy for three days and as many nights, fasting for the same cause. On the fourth night, around midnight, the whole fort suddenly fell in flames, struck by fire from heaven, and Vortigern died with his wives and all who were with him. This is the end of Vortigern, as I found it in the book of Saint Germanus.[^191-2] Others have told it differently.

### Others tell how Vortigern wandered, hated by all his people

<a id="page-192"></a>[\[Mommsen 192\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-192) Vortigern's whole nation came to hate him for his crime, the powerful and the powerless, slave and free, monks and lay people, small and great. He wandered from place to place, until at last his heart burst and he died without honor. Others say the earth opened and swallowed him on the night the fire took the fort around him, since nothing was ever found of those who burned with him.

He had three sons. The first was Vortimer, who fought against the barbarians, as I said above. The second was Catigern. The third was Pasgen, who reigned in two lands, Builth and Gwrtheyrnion, after his father's death, by the gift of Ambrosius, chief among all the kings of the British people. The fourth was Faustus, born to him by his own child.[^192-1] Saint Germanus baptized, raised and taught him. Faustus founded a great house on the bank of the river called Renis, which stands to this day. Vortigern also had one daughter, who was the mother of Saint Faustus.

### Vortigern's line is traced back from Ffernfael, who rules Builth now

Here's his ancestry, traced back to its beginning.

<a id="page-193"></a>[\[Mommsen 193\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-193) Ffernfael, who rules now in Builth and Gwrtheyrnion, is the son of Tewdwr, king of Builth. Tewdwr's line runs back, father to father, through Pasgen, Guoidcant, Moriud, Eldat, Eldoc, Paul, Mepurit, Briacat, Pasgen, Vortigern Guortheneu, Guitaul and Guitolin to Glovi.[^193-1] Bonus, Paul and Mauron were three brothers, Glovi's children. Glovi built a great city on the bank of the Severn, called Cair Glovi by the Britons and Gloucester by the Saxons. That's enough about Vortigern and his family.

## Part 7: Patrick is sent to Ireland and preaches to the Irish for forty years

### Germanus goes home, and Patrick is held captive by the Irish

After Vortigern's death Saint Germanus went back to his own country.[^193-2]

<a id="page-194"></a>[\[Mommsen 194\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-194) At that time Saint Patrick was a captive among the Irish. His master was named Milchu, and Patrick kept his pigs. At sixteen he came home from captivity, and later, by God's will, he was schooled in sacred learning. He traveled all the way to Rome and stayed there a long time, to read, to search into the mysteries of God and to go through the books of holy Scripture. He'd been there seven years when Celestine, bishop and pope of Rome, sent Bishop Palladius, the first to go, to turn the Irish to Christ.[^194-1] But God stopped him with storms, since no one on earth can receive anything unless heaven gives it to them.[^194-2]

<a id="page-195"></a>[\[Mommsen 195\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-195) Palladius set out from Ireland, reached Britain and died there, in the land of the Picts.

### Patrick is sent to the Irish and made a bishop under a new name

When news came of Bishop Palladius's death, another envoy, Patrick, went to turn the Irish to the faith of Christ, in the reign of Theodosius and Valentinian.[^195-1] Celestine, the pope of Rome, sent him, and so did an angel of God named Victor, while Bishop Germanus advised and urged it. Germanus had an older man, Segerus, go with him to a remarkable man who lived nearby, King Amatheus, a high bishop.[^195-2] There the saint, who knew everything that was to happen to him, was consecrated by King Amatheus and took the name Patrick, since he'd been called Maun before. Auxilius, Iserninus and others were ordained to lower ranks with him.

### Patrick sails to Ireland and preaches there

Once Patrick had been blessed and everything had been done in the name of the Holy Trinity, he boarded the ship made ready for him and came to Britain, where he preached for a few days.

<a id="page-196"></a>[\[Mommsen 196\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-196) Then, wasting no time on detours, he crossed the Irish Sea at full speed with a fair wind. The boat reached Ireland loaded with strange things from overseas and with spiritual treasure, and he baptized the Irish.

### The years are counted to the Irish baptism, and Patrick begins under King Lóegaire

From the beginning of the world to the baptism of the Irish is five thousand three hundred thirty years. Patrick began preaching the faith of Christ in the fifth year of King Lóegaire.[^196-1]

### Patrick works miracles, founds churches and ordains bishops for forty years

So Saint Patrick preached the gospel of Christ to foreign peoples for forty years. He worked miracles as the apostles did: he gave sight to the blind, healed lepers, made the deaf hear and drove demons out of the bodies they'd taken over. He raised the dead, nine in all, and with his own gifts he ransomed many captives, men and women. He wrote three hundred sixty-five *abegetoria* or more.[^196-2] He founded the same number of churches, three hundred sixty-five, and ordained three hundred sixty-five bishops or more, filled with the spirit of God.

<a id="page-197"></a>[\[Mommsen 197\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-197) Up to three thousand priests received orders from Patrick. In one region, Connacht, he turned twelve thousand people to the faith of Christ and baptized them, and in a single day he did the same for seven kings, the sons of Amolgith.[^197-1]

He fasted forty days and forty nights on the top of the hill of Eile, that is, Cruachan Eile, and there, high in the air, he humbly made three requests for the Irish who had taken up the faith.[^197-2] The first, as the Irish say, was that everyone might do penance, even at the very end of their life. The second was that the barbarians would never destroy them. The third was that none of the Irish would still be alive when the Judgment comes, since they'll be wiped out seven years before it, in Patrick's honor.

On that hill he blessed the peoples of Ireland; he'd climbed it to pray for them and to see the fruit of his work. Countless birds of many colors came to him for his blessing. That means all the holy men and women of Ireland will come to him on Judgment Day, as to their father and teacher, to follow him to the Judgment.

<a id="page-198"></a>[\[Mommsen 198\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-198) Later, at a great age, he died. Now he rejoices forever and ever. Amen.[^198-1]

### Patrick is likened to Moses in four ways

Moses and Patrick are alike in four ways. First, an angel spoke with each from a burning bush.[^198-2] Second, each fasted on a mountain forty days and forty nights. Third, they lived to the same age, a hundred twenty years. Fourth, no one knows where either lies, since each was buried in secret.

Patrick spent fifteen years in captivity, was consecrated by the holy Amatheus in his twenty-fifth year, and preached eighty-five years in Ireland.[^198-3] The subject called for saying more about Saint Patrick, but I wanted to keep it short.

## Part 8: Arthur leads the Britons' kings in twelve battles against the Saxons

### Octa comes to Kent, and Arthur leads the British kings in war

<a id="page-199"></a>[\[Mommsen 199\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-199) At that time the Saxons grew in number and strength in Britain.[^199-1] After Hengist's death his son Octa came down from the north of Britain to the kingdom of Kent, and the rulers of Kent are descended from him. Then, in those days, Arthur fought against the Saxons with the Britons' kings, but he was the leader in the battles.[^199-2] The first was at the mouth of the Glein. The second, third, fourth and fifth were on another river, the Dubglas, which is in the region of Lindsey. The sixth was on the Bassas. The seventh was in the forest of Celidon, Cat Coit Celidon in Welsh.[^199-3]

<a id="page-200"></a>[\[Mommsen 200\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-200) The eighth was at Fort Guinnion, where Arthur carried an image of holy Mary, ever virgin, on his shoulders.[^200-1] The pagans fled that day, and many of them were cut down through the power of our Lord Jesus Christ and of Saint Mary the Virgin, his mother. The ninth was fought in the City of the Legion. The tenth was on the bank of the Tribruit. The eleventh was on the hill called Agned. The twelfth was on Mount Badon, where nine hundred sixty men fell in a single day in one charge of Arthur's, and no one struck them down but Arthur himself. He came out the winner in all of them.[^200-2]

<a id="page-201"></a>[\[Mommsen 201\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-201) Beaten every time, the Saxons asked Germany for help, and their numbers grew many times over without a break. They brought kings over from Germany to govern them in Britain, until the time of Ida, whose father was Eoppa; Ida was the first to rule Bernicia.[^201-1]

## Part 9: The English kings' families are traced, and Urien fights Ida's heirs

### Ida's line is traced from Woden, and his heir Ecgfrith fights the Pictish king

<a id="page-202"></a>[\[Mommsen 202\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-202) From Woden the line ran down through Beldeg, Beornec, Gechbrond, Aluson, Inguec, Aedibrith, Ossa and Eoppa to Ida.[^202-1]

Ida had twelve sons. Their names are Adda, Aethelric, Decdric, Edric, Deothere and Osmer, with one queen, Bearnoch, and Ealric.[^202-2]

Ealdric was the father of Aelfret, also called Aethelfrith Flesaur. He in turn had seven: Anfrid, Oswald, Osbiu, Oswiu, Osgudu, Oslapf and Offa.[^202-3]

Oswiu's children were Alhfrith, Aelfwine and Ecgfrith.

<a id="page-203"></a>[\[Mommsen 203\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-203) Ecgfrith is the one who went to war against his cousin Bridei, king of the Picts, and fell there with the whole strength of his army. The Picts and their ruler won, and the Saxons, those savages, never again tried to take tribute from the Picts. Since that war it's been called Gueith Lin Garan.[^203-1]

Oswiu had two wives. One was Rhiainfellt, daughter of Royth and granddaughter of Rum; the other was Eanflaed, whose father, Edwin, was Aella's son.

### The kings of Kent are traced from Hengist

Here's the family of the kings of Kent. Hengist was the father of Octa, and the line went on through Oisc, Eormenric, Aethelberht, Eadbald and Eorcenberht to Ecgberht.[^203-2]

### The kings of East Anglia are traced from Woden

Here's where the rulers of the East Angles came from. Woden's descendants ran through Casser, Titinon, Trigil, Rodmunt and Rippan to Guillem Guechan, the first to reign over the East Angles in Britain. Guecha was followed by Guffan, Tydil, Ecni, Edric, Aldul and Elric, each the father of the next.

### The kings of Mercia are traced from Woden

Then the family of the Mercians.

<a id="page-204"></a>[\[Mommsen 204\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-204) From Woden it went down through Guedolgeat, Gueagon, Guithleg, Guerdmund, Offa, Ongen and Eamer to Pybba.

Pybba had twelve sons. Two of them I know better than the rest: Penda and Eowa.

Penda was Eadlit's father, and Pybba was Penda's.

Aethelbald descended from Pybba through Penda, Eowa and Alweo.

Ecgfrith's forebears go back through Offa, Thingfrith, Eanwulf, Ossulf and Eowa to Pybba.[^204-1]

### The kings of Deira and Bernicia are traced from Woden

These are the kings of the Deirans. Woden's descendants ran through Beldeg, Brond, Siggar, Sebald and Zegulf to Soemil, who first split Deira from Bernicia.[^204-2]

Soemil's line went on through Sguerthing, Giulglis, Usfrean and Iffi to Aella, Edwin, Osfrith and Eadfrith.[^204-3]

Edwin's two sons fell with him in the battle of Meicen, and his family never held the kingdom again, since not one of them came out of that fight alive. All were killed with him by the army of Cadwallon, king of Gwynedd.[^204-4]

<a id="page-205"></a>[\[Mommsen 205\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-205) Oswiu was the father of Ecgfrith. Ailguin's descendants were Oslach, Alhun, Adlsing, Echun and Oslaph, one generation after another.

Ida's line ran through Eadric, Ecgulf, Liodguald and Aetan, also called Eata Glinmaur, to Eadbyrth and Bishop Ecgbirth, the first of their nation.[^205-1]

Ida, Eoppa's son, held the regions in the north of Britain, beyond the Humber, and reigned twelve years, and he joined Din Guayrdi to Bernicia.[^205-2]

### British poets sing while Dutigirn fights the English, and Maelgwn reigns

At that time Dutigirn fought bravely against the English.[^205-3] Then Talhaearn Tataguen won fame for his poems, and Aneirin, Taliesin, Bluchbard and Cian, who's called Gueinth Guaut, all won theirs at the same time for verse in British.[^205-4]

<a id="page-206"></a>[\[Mommsen 206\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-206) Maelgwn, a great king, ruled among the Britons, in Gwynedd. His ancestor Cunedda had come there earlier with his eight sons from the north, from the land called Manaw Gododdin, a hundred forty-six years before Maelgwn came to the throne. They drove the Irish out of that country with enormous slaughter, and the Irish never returned to live there again.[^206-1]

### Urien and three other British kings fight Ida's heirs

Adda, Ida's son, reigned eight years; Aethelric, born to Adda, four; Theodric, Adda's brother, seven; and Frithuwald, six. In Frithuwald's time the kingdom of Kent received baptism, when Gregory sent it the faith.[^206-2] Hussa ruled seven years, and four kings fought against him: Urien, Rhydderch Hen, Gwallog and Morcant.[^206-3] Theodric battled hard against that Urien and his sons. In those days the enemy were beaten one time, our own people the next. Urien shut them up on the island of Lindisfarne for three days and nights. While he was on that campaign he was murdered, on Morcant's orders, out of envy, since of all the kings he had the greatest courage in waging war.

Aethelfrith Flesaur ruled twelve years in Bernicia and twelve more in Deira, twenty-four in all over the two kingdoms. He gave Din Guoaroy to his wife, Bebbab, and it was named Bebbanburth after her.[^206-4] Edwin, Aella's son, reigned seventeen years. He took Elmet and drove out Ceretic, its king.[^206-5]

Edwin's daughter Eanflaed was baptized on the twelfth day after Pentecost, with all her people, men and women. Edwin himself received baptism the next Easter, and twelve thousand people with him. If anyone wants to know who christened them, it was Rhun son of Urien. For forty days he never stopped baptizing that whole race of savages, and through his preaching many came to believe in Christ.[^206-6]

### Oswald and Oswiu reign in the north, and Oswiu fights Penda at Gai

<a id="page-207"></a>[\[Mommsen 207\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-207) Oswald, whose father was Aethelfrith, reigned nine years. He's the one called Oswald Lamnguin.[^207-1]

<a id="page-208"></a>[\[Mommsen 208\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-208) He killed Cadwallon, king of Gwynedd, in the battle of Heavenfield, with great slaughter of Cadwallon's army.[^208-1] Oswiu, Aethelfrith's other son, held the throne twenty-eight years and six months. In his time a plague struck, while Cadwaladr ruled the Britons after his father, and Cadwaladr died of it.[^208-2] Oswiu killed Penda on the plain of Gai, and that was the slaughter of the field of Gai, where the Britons' kings who had marched with King Penda as far as the city called Iudeu fell.[^208-3]

### Oswiu pays his treasure to Penda, and Penda's reign is told

Before that, Oswiu had handed over to Penda all the riches he had with him there, and Penda shared them out among the Britons' rulers: that's the Atbret Iudeu, the giving back at Iudeu.[^208-4] Only Cadafael, king of Gwynedd, got away with his army, by rising in the night, and that's why he's called Cadafael Cadomedd.[^208-5] Ecgfrith, Oswiu's son, reigned nine years.[^208-6] In his time the holy bishop Cuthbert died on the island of Lindisfarne.[^208-7] Ecgfrith is the one who made war on the Picts and fell there.

Penda, whose father was Pybba, held power ten years. He was the first to split the Mercians' kingdom from the Northumbrians', and by treachery he killed Anna, king of the East Angles, and Saint Oswald of the Northumbrians. He fought the battle of Maserfield, where his brother Eowa, Pybba's son and ruler of the Mercians, fell, as did Oswald of the Northumbrians. Penda won by the devil's magic.[^208-8] He wasn't baptized and never believed in God.

### The years are reckoned by consuls from Creation through Vortigern's reign

<a id="page-209"></a>[\[Mommsen 209\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-209) From the beginning of the world to the consuls Constantine and Rufus, five thousand six hundred fifty-eight years are counted.[^209-1]

From the two Gemini, Rufus and Rubellius, to Stilicho is three hundred seventy-three.[^209-2]

From Stilicho to Valentinian, Placidia's son, and the reign of Vortigern is twenty-eight.

From the start of Vortigern's rule to the quarrel of Guitolin and Ambrosius is twelve, that is, Guoloppum, the battle of Guoloph.[^209-3] Vortigern held power in Britain when Theodosius and Valentinian were consuls. In the fourth year of his reign the Saxons came to Britain, in the consulship of Felix and Taurus, four hundred years after the Incarnation of our Lord Jesus Christ.[^209-4]

From when the Saxons arrived in Britain and Vortigern took them in, to Decius and Valerian, is sixty-nine.[^209-5]

## Part 11: Strange things of Britain and Ireland are listed, among them Arthur's dog's footprint

### Lumonoy holds sixty islands, one wave floods the Trahannon, and a bath runs hot

<a id="page-213"></a>[\[Mommsen 213\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-213) The first strange thing is the lake of Lumonoy.[^213-1] It holds sixty islands where people live, sixty rocks ring it, each with an eagle's nest, and sixty rivers pour into it, but only one runs out to the sea, the one called the Lemn.[^213-2]

The second is the mouth of the Trahannon, where the tide covers the shores in a single wave as high as a mountain, then falls back as it does anywhere else.[^213-3]

The third is a hot pool in the region of Huich, walled around with brick and stone. People go there to bathe at any time, and each finds the water just as they like it, cold or warm, whichever they wish.[^213-4]

### Salt springs rise inland, and two waves fight in the Severn's mouth

The fourth is there too: springs of brine, which people boil down for the salt that cures every kind of food. They aren't near the sea, but come up out of the land.

<a id="page-214"></a>[\[Mommsen 214\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-214) Another is Duorig Habren, that is, the Two Kings of the Severn. When the water rises into the Severn's mouth at high tide, two heaps of foam gather, each on its own, and fight like rams. Each charges the other, they clash, then they pull back and come on again, at every flood. They've done it from the beginning of the world to this day.[^214-1]

### The Liuan's pool swallows the Severn's tide and spits it out

Another strange sight is Oper Linn Liuan, where a river empties into the Severn.[^214-2] When the Severn rises at high tide, the sea pours in there as well, and the pool at its mouth takes it in like a whirlpool, so it goes no farther upstream.

There's a strip of beach beside it that stays dry while the Severn is in flood. When the Severn and the sea ebb, the pool of Liuan spits out everything it swallowed, heaving it up in one wave as high as a mountain, which breaks over that shore.

If the whole army of the region stood facing that flood, the men would be dragged under, their clothes soaked through, and their horses too. But if they turned their backs, it wouldn't hurt them. When the tide goes out, the beach it covered is bare again, and the water draws back from it.

### Fish fill a spring with no stream, and wind blows from a pit

<a id="page-215"></a>[\[Mommsen 215\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-215) In the region of Cynllibiwg there's another strange thing, a spring called Finnaun Guur Helic.[^215-1] No stream runs into it or out of it. People go there to fish, wading in from the east, the right, the left or the west, and each side gives up a different sort.

It's remarkable to find four kinds in a spring that nothing flows into or out of, when it's neither big nor deep: the water reaches only to the knee. It's twenty feet long and as wide, with high banks all around.

By the Wye, apples grow on an ash tree on a wooded slope near its mouth.[^215-2]

In the region of Gwent there's a pit that a wind blows out of all the time, without a break. Even in summer, when the air is still, the gusts never stop, and nobody can bear to stand in front of that deep hole. The Britons call it Vith Guint, and the Latin is *flatio venti*, the breath of the wind. It's a remarkable thing for air to stream up out of the earth.

### Saint Illtud finds an altar that God holds up in a sea cave

In Gower there's another strange thing, an altar at a place called Loyngarth, which God's will holds up.[^215-3] I think its story is better told than kept quiet.

<a id="page-216"></a>[\[Mommsen 216\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-216) Saint Illtud was praying in a cave whose mouth opens on the sea that washes that shore, when a ship came sailing in toward him. Two men were aboard, and with them the body of a holy man, an altar hanging above him, held up by God's will. The man of God went out to meet them, and all the while it stayed in place over the dead saint's face.

"This man of God asked us to bring him to you," they told Saint Illtud, "and to lay him in the earth beside you. Don't tell anyone his name, so people won't swear oaths by him." They buried him, then went back to their ship and sailed away.

Saint Illtud founded a church around them both, the holy man's body and the altar, which God's will holds up to this day. A lesser king came to test it with a rod in his hand. He bent it in a loop all the way around, gripped both ends and pulled it toward him, and so proved the thing was true; he was dead in less than a month. Another man looked underneath and lost his sight, and he too died before thirty days had passed.

### In Gwent a spring holds a log that people stand on to wash

Gwent has one more: a spring by the wall of Meurig's well, with a log in the middle that people stand on to wash their hands and faces. I've tried it and seen it myself.

<a id="page-217"></a>[\[Mommsen 217\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-217) At high tide the Severn spreads over the whole shore, covering it all the way up to the spring, which fills with the Severn's flood. The water carries the log out to the open sea, where it tumbles about for three days, and on the fourth it's found back in its place. Once a farmer buried it in the ground to test this, and on the fourth day it was in the spring again, and before the month was out he was dead.

### Arthur's dog leaves a footprint at Builth, and his son's tomb changes length

In Builth there's another: a pile of stones, the topmost bearing a dog's footprint. Cavall, the warrior Arthur's hound, pressed it into the rock while they were hunting the boar Twrch Trwyth, and Arthur later heaped up the mound beneath it. Its name is Carn Cabal.[^217-1] People carry the stone off in their hands for a whole day and night, but the next morning it's back on its heap.

In Ergyng there's another, a grave beside a spring named Licat Anir. The man buried there was Anir, the son of the warrior Arthur, who killed him on that spot and laid him in it.[^217-2]

<a id="page-218"></a>[\[Mommsen 218\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-218) People come to measure the mound and find it six feet long one time, nine another, then twelve, then fifteen. Whatever length you get once, the next time it'll be different, and I've tested it myself.

### A grave on Cruc Maur fits whoever lies down beside it

Ceredigion has one more, on a hill known as Cruc Maur.[^218-1] On its top is a grave, and anyone who comes and lies down beside it finds it exactly their length. For a short person it shrinks, and for a big one, even six feet tall, it grows to fit. A traveler worn out by the road who kneels three times at the grave will never tire again until the day they die, not even if they went alone to the farthest ends of the earth.

### A shore has no sea, a hill turns, and a stone walks by night

The first strange thing is a shore with no sea.[^218-2]

The second is a hill there that turns around three times a year.

The third is a ford there that rises and falls with the tide.

<a id="page-219"></a>[\[Mommsen 219\]](historia-brittonum-latin.md#page-219) The fourth is a stone that walks by night over the valley of Citheinn. It was once thrown into the whirlpool of Cereuus, in the middle of the sea named Mene, and the next day it was found, beyond any doubt, back at the edge of Citheinn.

### An Irish lake is ringed by four metals, and another turns wood to stone

Lough Leane is a lake there, ringed four times around: first by tin, then lead, then iron, then bronze.[^219-1] It holds plenty of pearls, which kings wear in their ears.

Another turns wood into stone. People carve shapes out of it and throw them in, and there they lie until the year comes around, when each has become rock. It's known as Luch Echach.[^219-2]
